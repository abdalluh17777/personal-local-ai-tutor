## Spelling Rules for Adding -ing to a Verb (Present Participle)
| **NO CHANGE** | __**Most verbs**__ | Add -ing | **Read--Reading** | **Talk--Talking** | **Say--Saying** | **Want--Wanting** |
| **NO CHANGE** | __**Verbs that end in -y**__ | Add -ing | **Study--Studying** | **Carry--Carrying** | **Play--Playing** |
| **CHANGE** | __**Verbs that add in -e**__ | Drop the -e and add -ing | **Wake—Waking** | **Make—Making** | **Tape--Taping** |
| **CHANGE** | __**Verbs that end in -ie**__ | Change the ie to y | **Die—dying** | **Lie—lying** |
| **CHANGE** | __**Verbs that end in CVC (consonant, vowel, consonant)**__ | __**1 syllable**__ | Double the last letter | **Stop—Stopping** | **Sit—Sitting** | **Ban--Banning** |
| **CHANGE** | __**Verbs that end in CVC**__ | __**2 syllables**__ | If the stress is on the last syllable, double the last letter | **Begin—Beginning** | **Refer—Referring** | **BUT** | **Listen—Listening** | **Cancel--Canceling** |
## Spelling Rules for Adding -s/-es to the End of a Word (Plural Noun or Third Person Singular Simple Present)
| **Most words** | Add -s to the end of the word | **Verbs** | Walk--Walks | Cook--Cooks | Sit--Sits | Run--Runs | **Plural nouns** | Hat--Hats | Table--Tables | Chair--Chairs | Tree--Trees |
| **Words ending in consonant -y** | Change the -y to -ie, then add -s | **Verbs** | Carry--Carries | Study--Studies | Fly--Flies | **Plural Nouns** | Sky--Skies | Belly--Bellies | Rally--Rallies |
| **Words ending in these consonants** | S | X | Ch | Sh | Add -es | **Verbs** | Pass--Passes | Fix—Fixes | Watch—Watches | Bash--Bashes | **Plural Nouns** | Mess—Messes | Tax—Taxes | Patch--Patches | Dish—Dishes |
## Simple Present Tense Usage
- General facts
  - EX: It never snows in El Cajon
- Routines/habits
  - EX: She drives from Mexico to San Diego once a week
- Permanent situations
  - EX: I live in San Diego
- Stative verbs
  - EX: I don't like the weather in San Diego
## Present Continuous Usage
- Actions that are happening right now
  - EX: We are strolling on the beach
- Actions that are in progress
  - EX: This semester, she is working at a restaurant to help pay for school
- Actions that are new or temporary
  - EX: He is living in San Diego; he moved here a few months ago
- Actions that are changing over time
  - EX: Rent in San Diego is increasing rapidly
## Contrasting Simple Present and Present Continuous
Simple present is used for permanent situations. Present continuous is used for situations that are new or temporary.
- She works at Ross Dress for Less; she has worked there for a while and plans to stay
- She is working at Ross Dress for Less; she started the job a short time ago, or she plans to change jobs soon
Some verbs are stative. This means they are usually used for a permanent situation. Some of these verbs cannot be used in continuous form; other stative verbs change meaning when they are used in continuous form.
- GOOD EX: I own a car
- BAD EX: I am owning a car
- GOOD EX: I think Balboa Park is beautiful
- BAD EX: I am thinking Balboa Park is beautiful
- GOOD EX: I am thinking about Balboa Park
