## In this lesson
You edit your revised essay with the book's checklist for grammar, sentence structure, punctuation and mechanics, proofread it, and format the final draft. You also start an error log that works together with the Mistakes page of your tutor app, so that you learn from the errors you make most often. Careful editing shows your reader the attention you gave to your work, and an error log makes each new paper more accurate.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Explain what editing and proofreading involve and why readers notice errors.
- Use the editing checklist to find and correct errors in four areas.
- Proofread with the book's techniques, and use spelling and grammar checkers with care.
- Format a final draft for an academic course.
- Keep an error log linked to the Mistakes page, and edit for your own most frequent errors.

## Rules and explanations

### Editing comes after revising
Revising improves the **content**: support, unity, coherence and word choice (Lesson 29). Editing examines the **surface**: spelling, grammar, usage and punctuation, and the format of the finished paper. Budget time for it. Readers do not notice correct spelling, but they do notice misspellings; they look past your sentences to your ideas unless the sentences are awkward; they notice when every sentence has the same rhythm; and they notice the care you took with an error-free document.

### The editing checklist
| Area | Check for | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Grammar | sentence fragments; run-on sentences; conjunctions between independent clauses; subject-verb agreement; verb tense and irregular verb forms; subject, object and possessive pronouns; *who/whom*; clear antecedents; pronoun agreement; comparative and superlative forms; dangling modifiers; nouns: number, agreement and articles | Lessons 1, 4, 9, 10 |
| Sentence structure | variety (not only simple sentences); the best coordinating and subordinating conjunctions; long sentences with too much information; parallel structure | Lessons 2, 3, 5 |
| Punctuation | correct end punctuation; a reason for every exclamation point; apostrophes in singular and plural possessives; quotation marks | Lessons 6, 7, 8 |
| Mechanics and usage | spelling; capital letters; abbreviations; commonly confused words (to/too/two); the format your lecturer requires | Lessons 8, 50 |

The book's checklist has no question about commas, semicolons or colons: add them from Lessons 6 and 7.

### Proofreading, and using checkers with care
- A spelling checker cannot recognize that you meant *principle* but wrote *principal*. A grammar checker often questions correct sentences, because it does not understand your meaning. Accept the suggestions that make sense, but consider why each one came up. Do the same with suggestions from an AI tool, including your tutor: ask for the rule.
- Proofreading needs patience: it is easy to read past a mistake. Set the paper aside for a few hours or a day. Some professional proofreaders read a text **backward** to concentrate on spelling and punctuation. Or read the paper **aloud slowly**, paying attention to every word, letter and punctuation mark.
- Ask a reliable reader (your tutor) for a final pass.

### Format
For most academic papers: 1-inch margins, Times New Roman 12 point, double-spaced. Follow any specific instructions on titles, margins, page numbers and the place of your name; research papers often follow the APA or MLA style guide. Check the format before you submit.

**Psychology note:** psychology uses APA style. An APA student paper has a title page, a page number in the top right corner of every page, double spacing, 1-inch margins, a 0.5-inch indent at the start of each paragraph, and a readable font such as 12-point Times New Roman or 11-point Calibri.

### Keeping an error log
The book gives an **Error Analysis Checklist** to fill in when your paper comes back with your instructor's comments. It is a picture and is not in your lesson material, so keep your own error log, together with your tutor app's **Mistakes page**.

**The Mistakes page.** When you write or speak in chat or Live conversation, the tutor records the mistakes it finds. The page groups them by **rule** and shows the category (grammar, vocabulary, spelling, word order or punctuation), how many times the mistake was seen and when, a short explanation, and your examples (wrong → right). The tutor practises your most frequent patterns with you. Mark a pattern **resolved** when you stop making it; it reopens by itself if it comes back.

**Your error log.** Keep it in a notebook or a document:

| Date | Assignment | My sentence | Correction | Rule | Checklist area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

How to use it:
1. After every piece of feedback (your tutor's review of a draft, or a new entry on the Mistakes page), copy each error into the log with its rule.
2. Every week, compare your log with the Mistakes page. Which rules have the highest counts?
3. Your three to five most frequent rules are your **personal editing checklist**. Before you finish any paper, read it once for each of them.
4. When a rule disappears from your new writing, mark it resolved on the Mistakes page.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no capital letters, so check capitals for names, titles, nationalities and the first word of each sentence.
> - Arabic uses the article "al-" with general and abstract nouns, but English does not: write "Education is important" and "Stress affects memory", not "The education ..." or "The stress ...".

## Writing task
Edit and proofread the second draft of your essay from Lesson 29, and produce the **final draft** of your Unit 3 essay. (If you started a new topic, edit the latest draft you have.)

1. Set the draft aside for a few hours or a day.
2. **Personal sweep:** read once for each of the three to five rules in your error log.
3. **Checklist sweeps:** read once for each area: grammar, sentence structure, punctuation (add commas, semicolons and colons), and mechanics and usage.
4. Run a spelling checker, but think about every suggestion.
5. **Proofread:** read the essay aloud slowly; then read it backward, sentence by sentence, for spelling and punctuation.
6. **Format** it: title, 1-inch margins, double spacing, page numbers, and APA citations if you used sources.
7. Paste it into the chat and ask your tutor to mark (not correct) the remaining errors. Correct them, and add each one to your error log.

Self-editing checklist:
- I read once for each of my personal top errors.
- Every sentence is complete, with no fragments, run-ons or comma splices.
- Every verb agrees with its subject and is in the correct tense.
- Every pronoun has a clear antecedent, and every noun has the correct number and article.
- Lists and pairs are parallel.
- Apostrophes, quotation marks, commas and capital letters are correct.
- I checked commonly confused words that a spelling checker cannot catch.
- The format follows the required style.

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
