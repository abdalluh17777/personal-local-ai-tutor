# I. Non-Stop Non-Translation (NSNT) Free Writing
- Do you often translate from your native language to English when you think, write, and speak?
- Do you find it hard to write your paragraphs and essays without translating from your native language?
- Have you had challenges in making other people understand you?
If your answer to any of these three questions is a yes, you may want to learn to think and write in English through __**the Non-Stop Non-Translation (NSNT) free writing approach**__. It is one of the most effective ways to __**practice thinking and writing without translating**__. Free writing may serve different purposes, such as generating ideas and expressing viewpoints. However, in this NSNT approach, the goal is to help you think and write in English. As you gradually train yourself not to rely on your native language in your thinking, you will be able to speak, read, AND write more naturally in just English.
# II. Importance of Thinking in English While Writing
1. As you learned from Unit 1 Introduction to Academic Writing, (*Open Unit 1 here.*) in the very beginning of this course, the purpose of writing is to communicate your ideas accurately and effectively. As no two languages are exactly the same, your meaning usually gets lost or misinterpreted to some degree during direct translation from one language to the other. Consequently, your writing may confuse the readers or cause misunderstanding.
2.. When you formulate ideas in your native language and then translate them into English, the process takes longer than when you deal with only one language. As you prepare for your academic study in the U.S., you will be required to read and write a lot. You will not have enough time to complete them within the time frame set by your professors if you have to translate most of the times.
3. Old habits die hard. This saying means that it is hard to break an old habit. The longer you rely on translating, the harder it will be to quit the habit. At this intermediate level of English, you are in a good position to START switching the language in which you normally think when you are writing in English: from your native language to English.
# III. Steps in NSNT Free Writing
1. Prepare a notebook just for this practice. If you choose to type, set up a special folder or file on your computer. Record the date. You need to write or type in English ***nonstop*** so that you will have *no time translating* from your native language to English. Try three minutes, four times a week, and then gradually increase the duration and frequency as you feel more comfortable. Set your timer if needed.
2. You may decide on your own topic or choose one of the topics provided to get started. If you start rambling or end up writing something totally different, it is OK! The most important is that there is only *one language – English –* in your mind.
3. If you run out of ideas, just keep writing. You may write “Oh, I don’t know what more to write. Need ideas… 2 more minutes…” If you are really stuck, just write “Monday, Tuesday…”, or “January, February …”, or even “one, two, three…” ***Do not let the tip of your pen leave your notebook page, or do not stop typing.***
4. Do not worry about grammar and spelling. Do not stop to check your dictionary. The purpose is not to produce perfect writing, but to think in English. If you want to correct mistakes, do so after you complete the NSNT practice for that day.
***Example:***
1/15/2022
My first time to write like this. teacher said no stopping, it’s hard, but I’ll try. The first topic my feelings about this new semester. I feel writing is hard, but important. I want to take college classes after ESL. I want to practice good writing. What else? No stop now, keep writing. One, two, three, four, five, Oh, yes, writing. I took writing II last semester, I did well. I want to learn more and take this class. what else to write? no stopping, no translate, it’s hard, no native language, keep writing, writing. My language different from english, no translate is hard. Many mistakes but teacher said don’t worry about spelling, grammar, just keep writing. I feel uncomfortable with mistakes. Teacher say no stopping and no translate most important.
The above is a very typical example in the first few days or even weeks of your NSNT practice, but soon you will feel much more comfortable and have more ideas to write about, all in English!
It takes time to acquire this new skill, but if you keep practicing, you will be well on your way to thinking and writing in English. It may take some students a semester or even a year or more, so do not feel discouraged if you do not see progress right away.
***Just like working out, it takes consistent efforts to strengthen muscles.***

# IV. Weekly Writing Prompts
To get you started, here are some suggested topics for each week of the semester. You can modify them as you wish.
[Interactive exercise]
Week 1 Click here for additional writing prompts: How do you feel about this first week of the semester? What are your plans for this semester? How are you going to study differently this semester from how you studied in the past? What is your classroom like? Describe it. If you are taking this course online, describe your surroundings (your house, room…). What does “academic honesty” mean to you? As you study Unit 1 Introduction to Academic Writing, which of the ten misconceptions is the most relevant to you? How are you going to improve in that area? What are your expectations of this course? (Spring Semester) Do you have a New Year’s resolution? If yes, describe it. If no, why not? (Spring semester) What was an interesting event during your winter break? Describe it. (Fall semester) What was an interesting event in summer? Describe it.
Week 2 Click here for additional writing prompts: How do you feel about this second week of the semester? Do you feel more comfortable now than last week? Why or why not? Have you met some old friends or classmates this semester? What have you talked about? Have you made some new friends in the past two weeks? How did you meet each other? Have you tried the free ESL tutoring this week? If yes, what did you work on? If not, why not? Have you visited the college library since the beginning of the semester? If so, how do you like it and why? Have you read some books lately? Do you have a favorite book? Describe it. Do you have a favorite author? Describe him or her. (Spring semester) Martin Luther King Jr. Birthday is celebrated in the U.S. in January every year. What do you know about Martin Luther King Jr. and the American Civil Rights Movement? (Fall semester) Labor Day falls on the first Monday in September each year. What do you know about Labor Day in the U.S.? Do you have Labor Day in your country? How do you celebrate it? Martin Luther King Jr Birthday and Labor Day both started to fight for human rights though they were in two very different areas. What do human rights mean to you?
Week 3 Click here for additional writing prompts: Have you had a major assignment in one of your courses? Describe it. Have you had a major test in one of your courses? How did you do? Describe it. Should schools eliminate tests? Why or why not? Do tests encourage students to study harder? Why or why not? Have you had a very positive or negative experience with tests? Describe your experience. Did you try the NSNT free writing last week? This week? How do you feel about it? There are many student clubs on campus. Are you part of at least one of them? If yes, describe the club. If not, what club would you like to join and why? In what ways can college students train their leadership skills?
Week 4 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is dating like in your country? Do you believe in love at first sight? Why or why not? What are the advantages and disadvantages of Internet dating? Do you think arranged marriage is a good idea? Why or why not? What do you think are the most important factors to make a marriage strong and healthy? What do you think should be the minimum age for marriage? Why do you think so? Many people say that love is blind. How do you understand this saying? Do you believe it? What are the advantages and disadvantages of being single? What is your definition of a home? (Spring semester) Do you have Valentine’s Day or a similar day in your country? If so, describe it.
Week 5 Click here for additional writing prompts: What are some of the ways people from different cultures can learn from each other? What does the word “respect” mean to you? What is one aspect of culture from another country that you appreciate? Describe it. What is one of the most important aspects of your native culture that you would like to introduce to your classmates? What is one of the values from your native country that other people may misunderstand? Explain the reasons. Was there a time when you felt especially respected or disrespected? Describe it. Was there a time when an aspect of your native culture was honored in America? Describe it. Why do you think prejudice exists in the world? What are two of the ways people can try to overcome prejudice in their daily life? What can professors and students do to create a more positive and respectful learning environment on campus?
Week 6 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is the most important holiday in your native country? Describe it. How do you celebrate it now that you are in the U.S.? What is one area about your culture that you are particularly proud of? Describe it. If you could create a holiday, what would it be and why? What is the most significant holiday gift you have received? Describe it. In most countries, people like to visit families and relatives during holidays. Why do you think this tradition is important? What kinds of gifts are acceptable and what kinds are unacceptable in your native country? (Spring semester) President’s Day is celebrated in the U.S. in February. What do you know about any of the American presidents? Describe him. Is there a leader from your native country that you admire? Describe him or her.
Week 7 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is one of the most significant differences between education in the U.S. and education in your native country? Is it a good idea for smart children to skip grades? Why or why not? (For example, after finishing Grade 3, a child skips Grade 4 and goes directly to Grade 5.) What has been your most interesting educational experience? Describe it. Has your educational goal changed recently? Explain why or why not. Many people believe that education is the best gift parents can give to their children. Do you agree? Why or why not? Should college students spend the most of their energy studying? Explain your reasons. Should a college education be available to all people for free? Explain your reasons. Should all college students be required to take at least one online course? Why or why not?
Week 8 Click here for additional writing prompts: Textbooks in the U.S. are very expensive. So far, how do you like this free textbook (Building Academic Writing Skills)? Why do you feel this way? What do you like about it? What suggestions do you have to make the book better? What is one of the important aspects of writing you have learned this semester? Explain your view. What is considered good writing? Why do you think so? How do you view yourself as a writer? Why do you think so? What is one of the important aspects of learning for you this semester (any class)? Describe it. You may have had at least one in-class timed writing exam. How did you feel about it? What area of study do you need to improve? What will you do to improve it in the second half of the semester? What resources have you used at Harper so far (library, tutoring, Language Lab, etc.)? In what ways do they help you?
Week 9 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is the meaning of your name? Is it a typical name in your culture? Does it have a clear connection to your personal identity? In the U.S., many people like to call each other by the first name. In your native culture, how do you call your grandparents, parents, uncles and aunts, brothers and sisters, cousins, professors, neighbors, and friends? What are the ways to make American friends? What are effective ways to make a good first impression when meeting new people? Is it better to have lots of friends or just one or two good friends? Why? Is it possible to have cross-generational friends? (for example, an 18-year-old and a 70-year-old become good friends.) Many people like to make many "Facebook friends". If you use any social media, how many friends do you have there? How many of them do you keep in touch? Descirbe what you often discuss with them. Have you been to a party and later regretted going? If so, describe it. What are your hobbies? Describe them. What is one sport that you like to watch but have not played. Explain why you have not played it.
Week 10 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is your most memorable travel experience? Describe it. If you could take just one person to travel around the world for a year, whom would you take and why? Imagine you are going on a two-week vacation out of the state by yourself without your cell phone. What challenges would you encounter? How would you solve them? What is your favorite season? Describe it. Chicago is known for its cold winters. Describe how you spent your first winter in Chicago. If you have not spent a winter in Chicago, image what it would be like and describe it. (Spring semester) What is your plan for Spring Break? (Spring semester) What does “spring” mean to you? (Spring semester) What are some interesting spring time activities in your native country? Describe them. (Fall semester) What does “fall” mean to you? (Fall semester) What are some interesting fall activities in your native country? Describe them.
Week 11 Click here for additional writing prompts: Do mothers and fathers take care of their children in different ways? Describe the similarities and differences. What is the best advice you have received from your parents? Explain it. Is it common to take children to a parent's workplace in your native country? What is the most important: being smart, being healthy, or being good-looking? Why? What is the new trend in fashion these days? Do you consider yourself a fashion-conscious person? Some people believe that appearance is very important, and some others believe that a person’s inner self (character, personality, etc.) is more important? What do you think and why? (Spring semester) How did you spend your Spring Break? Which part was the best? Which part was the worst? (Fall semester) Americans celebrate Halloween on October 31. How do you feel about Halloween? Are you going to celebrate it this year? Why or why not?
Week 12 Click here for additional writing prompts: Look at yourself in the mirror, what three words come to your mind to describe how you look? Explain them. If you could change one thing about yourself, what would it be and why? If you could change one thing about one of your family members, who would it be, what the change would it be, and why? If you could change one thing about this class, what would it be and why? If you could change one thing about your college, what would it be and why? If you could change one thing about your native country, what would it be and why? If you could change one thing about the U.S., what would it be and why? If you could change one thing about the world, what would it be and why?
Week 13 Click here for additional writing prompts: What is your favorite social media site? Why is it your favorite? What are the benefits of social media? Describe them. What are the disadvantages of social media? Describe them. Should parents supervise their teenage children’s the use of social media? Why or why not? What would you do to entertain your family or friends without spending any money? (Fall semester) Americans celebrate Veterans Day in November. What part of the American history do you find the most fascinating? Describe it. What part of the history of your native country do you think the most important? Describe it. If you could live the life of a person in history, whom would you choose? Why would you choose that person?
Week 14 Click here for additional writing prompts: Thanksgiving Day is coming. What are you most thankful for? Why do you feel this way? What have you been eating for meals and snacks in the past two days? Is your diet healthy? Why or why not? What are the ways you can improve your diet? What is your favorite holiday food in your native country? In the U.S.? Describe them. What are the ways families and friends can strengthen their relationships? What are the advantages and disadvantages of moving away from the parents’ home? (Fall semester) Thanksgiving Day is near. What are you plans for the holiday? (Fall semester) Is there a similar holiday in your native country? How do people celebrate it there?
Week 15 Click here for additional writing prompts: Do you think the minimum driving age (currently sixteen in the U.S.) should be raised or lowered? Why? If you could add one new rule for new drivers, what would it be and why? Do you think the minimum drinking age (currently twenty-one in the U.S.) should be raised or lowered? Why? Should adults drink alcohol in front of children under eighteen years old? Why or why not? (Spring semester) “Earth Day” is celebrated in the U.S. in late April. What does “Earth Day” mean to you? Our living environment (nature, natural resources, etc.) has changed a lot over the last few decades. What are some of the things people can do to protect the environment? What part of nature do you enjoy the most in your neighborhood? Describe it. Read the short poem below by Percy Bysshe Shelley, an English romantic poet in the early 19th century. Write how you feel as you read the poem. “Away, away, from men and towns, To the wild wood and the downs, To the silent wilderness, Where the soul need not repress its music.”
Week 16 Click here for additional writing prompts: What has been your greatest accomplishment this semester? Describe it. What is your future academic plan? Describe it. What is your future career plan? Describe it. What advice would you give to an ESL student who is going to take this course? What advice would you give to someone who is starting college for the first time? What suggestions would you have to improve this free textbook (Building Academic Writing Skills)? (Spring semester) What are your plans for the summer? (Fall semester) What are you plans for the winter break? What do you think your life will be like in the next five years? What do you think the five ten years will bring to this world, your native country, the U.S.?
