# II. Rules for Punctuations
[Interactive exercise]
Periods - click here for explanations and examples: A period is used at the end of a statement. It rained last night. It does not snow in this part of the country.
Question marks - click here for explanations and examples: A question mark is used at the end of a direct question. Did it rain last night? What is the weather like in your hometown?
Exclamation marks - click here for explanations and examples: An exclamation mark is used to show emotion or emphasis. How beautiful is the night sky! We are going, rain or not!
Commas - click here for explanations and examples: Commas in English can be complicated. The following are some of the common rules: In a series of three items or more In summer, many people like to take a vacation, practice their hobbies, and spend more time with their loved ones. My cousin Ralph likes to try out new recipes with all sorts of nuts: almonds, walnuts, pecans, hazelnuts, cashews, and many more. 2. After transition signals at the beginning of a sentence First, he toasts the nuts. Next, he leaves them on the pan to cool. 3. After prepositional phrases at the beginning of a sentence On the counter in his kitchen, Ralph has ten pans of different sizes and shapes. For his new broccoli salad, he adds toasted pecans and walnuts. 4. Before coordinating conjunctions in compound sentences (FANBOYS) Ralph’s broccoli salad is tasty, but it contains much fat from the dressing and the nuts. It is high in calories, so I usually eat only a small cup of the salad. 5. After a dependent clause at the beginning of a complex sentence When people compliment on the salad, Ralph always looks truly happy. If someone gently suggests ways for improvement, Ralph is not always gracious. 6. After adverbial conjunctions Ralph is a very likeable young man; however, he is nuts about nuts. He loves nuts; as a result, his holiday gift wish list is always full of nut goodies like peanut brittle. 7. Before (and after) a word or a phrase to explain the noun mentioned right in the previous part of the sentence Last year, he was so surprised to receive a ballet ticket to the Nutcracker, my Christmas gift to him. Five pounds of mixed nuts, my second Christmas gift, will help him with more yummy creations. 8. Between a person and his/her quote I said to him, “I hope you enjoy my gifts!” Ralph answered happily, “I will!” 9. Between the date and the year (no comma for just month and year) Ralph hosted his very first “nutty” party on December 31, 2017. He had his second part right on the next day, January 1, 2018. 10. Between the street name and the city, the city and the state in an address Ralph used to live at 100 Walnut Street, Nuttsville, Virginia. He has moved to 25 Coconut Street, Almond, North Carolina. 11. After salutation in an informal letter and after the closing of any letter Dear Ralph, Thank you for the wonderful chocolate hazelnut shortbread. I really enjoyed it. Love, Linda
Semi colons - click here for explanations and examples: A semi colon is like a long comma or a short period. Do not capitalize the first letter after a semi colon unless it is a proper name. Between two complete and closely related sentences Sports are common hobbies; baseball is especially popular among school age children. They join Little League teams; their parents usually act as coaches and volunteers. 2. In front of some adverbial conjunctions or transitions These children play to win; nevertheless, the most valuable experience is building friendships. They learn to respect each other; for example, the players usually shake hands with the members of the opponent team no matter which team has won.
Colons - click here for explanations and examples: A colon indicates “I mean the following”. Before a list of things Here are three important pieces of advice for an online class: treat the class as seriously as a face-to-face class, follow a reasonable schedule, and keep in touch with the professor. Two books are required for most reading courses: a textbook and a novel. 2. After the salutation in a formal business letter Dear Ms. Gonzales: Dear Dr. Khalil:
Apostrophes - click here for explanations and examples: Apostrophes are used in two main ideas: in contraction and in possessives. In contractions Computer games have become popular in recent years. They’re often on many people’s holiday gift lists. (They are) More and more people like to play those games. It’s become an addiction for them. (It has) 2. In possessives Many stores have separate counters for computer games: adults’ games in one area and children’s games in another. My friend’s favorite game is League of Legends. My cousins’ favorite is Counter-Strike.
Quotation marks - click here for explanations and examples: Quotation marks are used to quote exact expressions or to cause attention. At the beginning and end of a quote Students often ask professors, “How do we improve our grade?” Professors usually reply, “Focus on improving your skills, and your grade will surely improve.” 2. Around unusual expressions when they are used in an uncommon way When students exchange answers during an exam, they are not “helping” each other. They are actually cheating. It is important to list the source of information when we “borrow” other people’s ideas and use them in our writing.
