Public Laundromats in the U.S.A.
When I moved to Chicago, I was shocked because I had to use a public laundromat. Most people living in the apartments do not have their own washer and dryer, but I think this situation creates some problems for them. The transportation of dirty linen and clothes is the first problem. People need to collect everything and go down to the apartment basement where the machines are. Sometimes, they have to drive to the nearest laundromat if all washing machines are occupied by their neighbors. They may wait even half a day for a free washer or dryer. The second problem is having the exact change. Each washing costs $2.75 to $3.50. People need to frequently collect quarters or change money in the bank. The problems do not end here. The biggest problem of using public laundromats is hygiene. No one knows what kind of things people have washed in the same machines: underwear, linen, cloth diapers, even shoes. One day, while waiting for an available washer, I decided to take out a neighbor’s laundry from the washing machine because the laundry had been there for a long time. When I did this, I felt sorry and angry. I found different things which could not be washed together because it was not hygienic to do so. Of course, for eight months in America, I had to use the public laundromat more than a few times because I did not have another choice. Imagine my happiness and relief[1] when I finally bought my own first washing machine!
*By E. Yugay (student), ESL Writing III, Harper College. Used with permission.*

# III. Topic Sentence in Enumerative Paragraphs
[Interactive exercise]
♠♠ Click here to review topic sentences from Unit 3 Parts and Characteristics of a Good Paragraph: A topic sentence shows the main idea of a paragraph. It usually appears in the beginning of a paragraph. A good topic sentence includes the topic, controlling idea, and optional predictors. The controlling idea is the writer’s opinion or feeling about the topic. A topic sentence must be a complete sentence (subject + verb). A topic sentence cannot be a question, an announcement, or a fact.
*For detailed explanations and examples of topic sentences, open Unit 3 here.*
The same topic can produce many different topic sentences if the controlling idea is changed. Of course, the paragraph will be different as a result. This shows that you have the flexibility to decide on your focus based on your interests. **Write from your heart!**
Study the following topic sentences and discuss how writers focus on different areas of the same topic. You will notice that some topic sentences are more formal/academic in nature and some are more personal.
| Topics | Topic sentences with different controlling ideas |

| benefits of owning a dog | There are thee benefits to owning a dog. | My neighbor’s puppy Skippy has brought much joy to my family. |

# IV. Supporting Ideas in Enumerative Paragraphs
[Interactive exercise]
♠♠ Click here to review supporting sentences from Unit 3 Parts and Characteristics of a Good Paragraph: To ensure good, relevant support: Ask a question about the controlling idea in the topic sentence. Your answer could be the basis for supporting ideas. Make an outline. Delete irrelevant and repetitive ideas. Remember the “Hamburger Way”. Use facts, explanations, examples, quotes, etc. to make the paragraph interesting.
*For detailed explanations and examples of supporting sentences, open Unit 3 here.*
In an enumerative paragraph, the supporting ideas follow a logical order. The most important point is usually listed last. Study the paragraph below and discuss how the writer uses different types of details – explanations, examples, and a quote – to support the topic sentence.
The Joy of Having My Three Children
It is a blessing that I have three children, aged 15, 11, and 6. First, they are not lonely anymore. When I had two children, they complained to me many times. My son wanted a younger brother, and my daughter wanted a baby sister. They also fought for toys. After my third child was born, their life changed a lot. Most times, they played with her and even taught her how to use chopsticks. Now all three children are very happy together. As a result, I am delighted. However, with three children, I feel really old sometimes, but they make me feel young. Each of my children has many activities, and I am very involved in their lives. Last month, when my second child participated in a cross-country competition[9] organized by the school district, I ran next to her and cheered her on. I felt young and energetic as if I were 16 years old again. The part I love the most is that they help me become a better person. Each child has a different personality. Sometimes, I cannot understand why they act in a certain way, but I find out that they are trying to imitate[10] my husband and me. “Children are mirrors of their parents”. This saying is so true. I can see my own problem through them because their action reflects my behavior. Now I am more careful when they are around. I do not yell, I do not judge other people, and I always say “Thank you”. I want to set a good example for them. Even though I am often physically tired, I love them because love makes miracles happen. My children are the joy of my life.
*By D. Kim (student), ESL Writing III, Harper College. Used with permission.*

[Interactive exercise]
♠♠ Click here to review writing conclusion from Unit 3 Parts and Characteristics of a Good Paragraph: It usually restates the main idea but in different words or sentence structure. It usually summarizes the main points in the body of the paragraph. It usually expresses an opinion, makes a prediction, puts forth a recommendation, or asks a question related to the topic. A conclusion must not bring up a new topic.

Paragraph #1 Shoes or No Shoes (by A. Zhumagulov): Shoes or No Shoes? It is puzzling to me why Americans wear shoes at home. There are there reasons why I am having a hard time understanding this custom. First, shoes are uncomfortable. When I come home, I want to relax. Arriving home in the evening, I need to switch my mindset to rest. When I take off my shoes, I subconsciously send a signal to my brain that the day is over and that it is time for rest. Street shoes like sneakers, dress shoes, and boots are just uncomfortable for the legs and feet. Secondly, wearing shoes at home is not hygienic. Outdoor shoes carry dirt. Even if we don't step on dirt, shoes accumulate a lot of dust when we go outside. Footwear is also a magnet for germs which can become a source of infectious diseases. The soles of the shoes are breeding grounds for harmful bacteria from parks, railway stations, or public toilets. This means that the bacteria, of course, remain on the soles. When I enter my own house, I just want to keep it clean without worrying too much about dirt and bacteria in it. Lastly, the culture in which I grew up is very different from the American culture in this area. In my country, it is not customary to wear shoes at home. I was brought up and taught to take off my shoes when I entered the house. It was like respecting the needs of the family members. I remember a time when I was punished at the age of five for running into the house in sneakers. In the same way, if I entered someone else’s house with shoes off, I would be showing respect for the host. Taking off my shoes at home is a matter of habit for me. Maybe one day in the future, more people in America will follow this habit that brings comfort, cleanness, and respect. By A. Zhumagulov, Writing III, Harper College. Used with permission.

Paragraph #7: To Communicate or Not to Communicate?: To Communicate or Not to Communicate Three subjects often cause disagreement between teenagers and their parents: friends, free time, and school. To begin with, it is hard for them to discuss friends. Teenagers tend to make friends based on “cool” aspects like popularity and appearance. However, their parents want to know if these friends are “good” students and have “good” behaviors. If some friends do not have good grades, or if they like partying, the parents may not want them to be their children’s friends. The second topic the adolescents and their parents often fight about is how to spend free time. Young people like to play games, go to the movies, and hang out with their friends. On the other hand, their parents consider those activities meaningless or a waste of time bur prefer to see their children read books, work out, and help with housework. The most frequent fight happens when they discuss study. The teenagers think that study is one part of their lives, but the parents believe that study should be all their children should do to prepare for a successful career. As a result of this disagreement, when they discuss future college majors, the parents often hope for careers in business, law, or health care because of the job opportunities and stable income. If their children have passion in music or literature, the family may be concerned about the job prospects. To sum up, the fights between the parents and their children often put everyone in a bad mood. Only time, love, and understanding will help restore harmony in the family.
Paragraph #8 The Inconvenience of Overseas Travels: The Inconveniences of Overseas Travels As more and more people travel overseas for personal or business reasons, they are faced with a few conveniences. The first trouble is to get their passports and visas. Even though it is easy to get them in some countries, it can be a major hassle for most people. For example, many small or remote towns do not have a foreign consulate, so people have to spend money and time going to a big city to get their visa. Once their visa is ready and they are all excited to leave, they must clear the hurdles at the airport security. There are usually long lines and intense checks. Occasionally a police dog may be used to sniff potential dangers in the crowd. It can be very scary. When the travelers are lucky to pass the security, they must face other new challenges of security and customs clearance in their destination countries after a long and fatiguing flight or sometimes a series of flights. In addition, luggage claim, a new language, and other issues can exhaust the already extremely tired body. The inconveniences do not end here. Many people struggle with terrible jet lag in the next two days to even weeks. If they go overseas to visit families and friends, they will not be able to enjoy the reunions right away, nor will they delight in the delicacies prepared to welcome them. If they have business affairs, their work is also affected while they are trying to overcome the jet leg. When they finally feel at ease, it is time to travel back home. As interesting as overseas travels may be, travelers have to overcome obstacles from leaving to returning.

# VII. Transitions in Enumerative Paragraphs
[Interactive exercise]
♠♠ Click here to review transitions from Unit 3 Parts and Characteristics of a Good Paragraph: Transitions connect ideas and guide readers. The transitions before the first supporting idea and before the conclusion are often optional. “Finally” is for the last supporting idea in the body of the paragraph, not for the conclusion.
*For detailed explanations and examples of transitions, open Unit 3 here.*
You already know very well how to use transitions like “first, second, next, in addition, finally”. They are very useful expressions but not the only ones. Take a look at a variety of transitions used in the paragraphs in this unit. Notice the punctuation as well.
| **Enumerative paragraphs in this unit** | **Transitions between the supporting sentences and before the conclusion** |

**Summary**
1. An enumerative paragraph focuses on explaining a few points (reasons, ways, approaches, aspects, etc.) to support a main idea.
2. An enumerative paragraph should have a title, a topic sentence, 2-4 supporting ideas with details, a conclusion, and transitions.
3. Following the writing process (pre-writing, writing, and post-writing) can ensure a well – organized and well – supported paragraph.
