## Student Spotlight--Cody Gibson
June 25, 2014
Geomatics major, Class of 2015
Hometown: Shawnee, Oklahoma
[Image: Cody Gibson.jpg]
*image courtesy Cody Gibson*
Did you know that while 24 people have been to the moon, just three have been to the deepest part of the ocean? We have more understanding of the moon's geography than the geography of Earth's sea floor.
"The ocean is only five to eight percent mapped," said Cody Gibson, a geomatics student at the University of Alaska, Anchorage and adventurer who is committed to mapping the mysterious depths of the ocean. The challenge? Average depths of about 14,000 feet--that's 2.65 miles. Cody works for Fugro Pelagos, a company with surveyors around the world who specialize in mapping the sea floor.
Before we learn more of Cody's story, an important question: what does a person studying geomatics do? Besides wear orange vests around campus, of course.
"Essentially we're mapping the Earth, using math, physics, law practices, everything possible," he said.
With just one semester left until his December graduation, Cody is looking forward to committing full-time to Fugro, the company he first connected with as an intern almost two years ago. Geomatics graduates are in such high demand in Alaska, he said, it's rare for a student to graduate without a job offer in hand.
"If they don't have an offer by graduation," he said, "they'll have one in a matter of weeks." Most will end up working in land surveying, but Cody is excited to be working in his specialized field, hydrography, which keeps him out on the water through much of the summer, surveying and outfitting boats with state-of-the-art mapping equipment.
"University of Alaska, Anchorage is my 17th school since I was in first grade," Cody said with a grin. "My mom just liked to travel a lot so we traveled often. She'd start at a new school, I'd start at a new school. She's a teacher--English, drama, music, all sorts of things."
[Image: Cody 2.jpg]
*Image courtesy Cody Gibson*
UAA is Cody's third college. After graduating from high school in Oregon, he studied photography at Southern Oregon University. After Oregon, he transferred to University of Colorado, Colorado Springs. There he took all their photography courses and started thinking about alternatives. He caught sight of a roadside survey crew and it got him thinking.
He began researching geomatics programs and decided to go to UAA. Alaska sounded like the best adventure, so he headed North.
"I love it. I've spent less than a month out of the state since I moved here," he said.
When he's not working or in class, he's exploring Alaska. He enjoys solo kayaking adventures, hiking, and biking. He keeps a camera close by on his adventures, too. His first summer in Alaska he spent working for an aerial mapping company.
[Image: Cody 3.jpg]
*Cody in his kayak. Image courtesy Cody Gibson*
If all goes to plan, Cody's next adventures may take him back up in the air to get a better look at the oceans below.
"There's an overlay between hydrography and LIDAR, which is where they shoot lasers down from a plane to map the earth by millions and millions of data points," he explained. "There's a crossover there caller LIDAR bathymetry and that's really what I'm interested in--mapping the ocean with LIDAR."
That's something Fugro does, he said. Geomatics gives him the opportunity to travel around the world. His degree will take him everywhere from Belize to Trinidad to Saudi Arabia for winter work.
Alaska, too, will remain on his list for personal and professional exploration.
"I've only been to a small percentage of Alaska and there's so much left to do," he said. "I came up to Alaska for an adventure and exceeded my expectations by a long shot."

## Writing Interview Questions
**When an author writes a profile, they need to conduct an interview first in order to gather information about their subject. The author of this profile is Jamie Gonzalez. Jamie Gonzalez asked Cody about his life, his studies and his future career. What questions do you think Jamie Gonzalez asked?**
