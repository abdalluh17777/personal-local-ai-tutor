# Lesson 34: Future Tenses in Writing

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from Building Academic Writing Skills by Lin Cui, Harper College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): Unit 13 Expressing Meaning in Future Tenses.*

## In this lesson
You will learn three ways to write about the future: the simple future (will and be going to), the simple present for schedules, and the present progressive for plans. You will also learn future time clauses, where a present verb has a future meaning. These forms help you write predictions and plans, for example about health care, technology or your own studies. Begin with the Pretest in your lesson material before you read on.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Form the simple future correctly with will + base verb and am/is/are going to + base verb.
- Choose will or be going to for predictions, plans, promises, decisions and invitations.
- Use the simple present for fixed schedules and the present progressive for planned actions.
- Write future time clauses with when, while, before, after, until and as soon as.
- Find and correct future verb errors in a paragraph.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simple future | a tense for activities or situations that will happen in the future | More patients will enjoy personalized medical care ten years from now. |
| base verb | the simple form of a verb, with no -s, -ed or -ing | will **help**, is going to **help** |
| future time clause | a dependent clause (it cannot stand alone) that starts with when, while, before, after, until or as soon as and refers to the future | **When AI replaces more and more human jobs in the future**, many people will lose their job. |
| non-action verb | a verb for a state, not an action; it is not used in the progressive | know, like, own |
| pre·DIC·tion | a guess (about the future) | Will and be going to can both make predictions. |
| ar·ti·FI·cial in·TEL·li·gence (AI) | a technology to make machines act like humans | Artificial Intelligence (AI) has already transformed a lot of areas of our lives. |
| re·MOTE | far away | the remote areas of the world |
| terminally ill patient | a person who is very sick and has no chance of recovery | Medical advances are going to help terminally ill patients. |
| PUR·chase | buy | The hospital is going to purchase some new equipment. |
| SEN·ior | an old person | Those pets will make the seniors happy. |

## Rules and explanations
### 1. The simple future: forms
| | "Be" verb (be) | "Do" verb (all other verbs) |
|---|---|---|
| will | will be | will + base verb: will help |
| be going to | am/is/are going to be | am/is/are going to + base verb: is going to help |

- Meaning: activities or situations that will happen in the future.
- Time expressions (words that show when): later today, tomorrow, next week, in 2035, soon, in the future, in the near future.
- There will be an increased need for doctors in the remote areas of the world.
- Medical advances are going to help terminally ill patients.
- Will there be a cure for the common cold in the near future?
- Check the form. After will: only the base verb (no -s, no -ing, no extra "be"). Be going to needs all its parts: am/is/are + going + to + base verb.

### 2. Will or be going to?
| Meaning | am/is/are going to | will |
|---|---|---|
| Future prediction (same) | There is going to be an increased need for doctors. | There will be an increased need for doctors. |
| Future schedule (same) | The new doctors are going to begin their medical practice next week. | The new doctors will begin their medical practice next week. |
| Prior plan (decided before now) | The hospital is going to purchase some new equipment with the donated money. | X |
| A future action based on strong evidence now | The patient is eating! He is going to recover! | X |
| Promise, desire, determination | X | No matter how long the surgery will be, this patient will endure (go through) it. |
| Decision made at the moment of speaking | X | A: The patient is in pain. Who will get the doctor? B: I will! |
| Invitation | X | Will you join us in the new hospital opening ceremony tomorrow? |

After "no matter how", a present verb is also correct and more common: "No matter how long the surgery is, this patient will endure it."

### 3. Simple present for schedules
Use the simple present for events in a fixed or official schedule (planes, movies, semesters). Common verbs: start, begin, end, finish, open, close, arrive, leave, land, take off.
- The new Children's Hospital opens next month. Its affiliated Urgent Care Center starts two months later.

### 4. Present progressive for plans
Use am/is/are + verb-ing for actions that are already planned, including personal plans. Add a future time expression. Common verbs: go, come, move, see, fly, play, meet, buy. Do not use non-action verbs this way.
- Dr. Marcus is flying in from New York this afternoon to perform the heart surgery. She is meeting the patient's family afterwards.

### 5. Future time clauses
Form: when / while / before / after / until / as soon as + subject + simple present or present progressive. The independent clause (the main clause, which can stand alone) keeps the future tense.
- When AI replaces more and more human jobs in the future, many people will lose their job.
- One of my friends will need to find another job before AI takes over her work in three months.
- She will be meeting some business friends while she is traveling next month.

The verb in the time clause looks present, but it means future. The book compares it to a Santa Claus in a shopping mall: he looks like Santa Claus, but without the costume he could be your neighbor.

Punctuation (Unit 7, Lesson 14): put a comma after a time clause that comes first. Use no comma when it comes second.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no verb "be" in present-tense sentences, so it is easy to leave "be" out of be going to. Always check that am, is or are comes before going to.

## Answer key

**Pretest** (book): 1. will take: correct. 2. will be apply → will apply. 3. transport → will transport. 4. will use: correct. 5. will also assist: correct. 6. are going to substitute: correct. 7. is going to available → is going to be available. 8. going to use → are going to use. 9. is going to replace → replaces (time clause). 10. will take → takes (time clause).

## Writing task
Book Exercise 7. Choose two topics and write one paragraph for each (8 to 10 sentences).
- What will ________ become in ten years? (education, job search, family, health care, traveling, social media ...)
- What will your life be like in ten years? What will you do to achieve these accomplishments?
- How will artificial intelligence (or another technology) help humanity in 2035?
- From Exercise 2: Will there be a cure for all types of cancer in thirty years? Why do you think so? Or: What will elderly care be like in the year 2060?

Start with a topic sentence. Use will and be going to, and at least two future time clauses in each paragraph. You may begin with NSNT free writing (Lesson 1).

Self-editing checklist:
- will + base verb (no -s, no -ing, no extra "be").
- am/is/are + going to + base verb.
- A present verb in every time clause, not will.
- A comma after a time clause that starts the sentence.
- Time expressions (in ten years, in 2035, soon) match future verbs.
- be going to for plans; will for promises and decisions.
