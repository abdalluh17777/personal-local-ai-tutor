# Lesson 33: Grammar: Embedded Questions

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 9.4: Grammar Presentation-Embedded Questions; 9.5: Communication Practice.*

## In this lesson
You will learn to put a question inside a sentence (an embedded question) with statement word order, and to use it as a subject, an object, the object of a preposition or an adjective complement. You will find embedded questions in "What to Expect from Adolescence" (Lesson 32), complete and edit sentences, and use them in speaking and writing. Academic writers use them to state research questions: *Researchers want to know why teenagers take risks.*

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Change a direct question into an embedded question with statement word order.
- Use embedded questions as subjects, objects, objects of prepositions and adjective complements.
- Find and correct question word order inside a statement.
- Tell noun clauses, adjective clauses and adverb clauses apart when they begin with the same word.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| embedded question | a question inside a sentence; it uses statement word order | I don't know where my shoe is. |
| dependent clause | a group of words with a subject and a verb that cannot stand alone as a sentence | When they will arrive is unknown. |
| noun clause | a dependent clause that does the job of a noun (subject, object ...) | What she studies is difficult. |
| statement word order | subject + verb, as in a normal statement | ... where my shoe **is** (not ~~where is my shoe~~) |
| question word order | (question word +) auxiliary + subject + verb | What does she study? |
| auxiliary (helping) verb | a verb used with the main verb to make questions and tenses: do/does/did, be, have, will, can ... | What **does** she study? |
| object of a preposition | the noun or clause after a preposition (about, of, in, on ...) | I would like you to focus on where my shoe is. |
| adjective complement | a clause after it + be + adjective that completes the adjective | It is unclear where my shoe is. |
| coming of age | becoming an adult; the celebration of it | ... when their coming of age is celebrated depends on their culture. |
| speculate | to guess possible reasons without being sure | ... some speculate that 16 gives adolescents freedom. |

## Rules and explanations

### What an embedded question is
Embedded questions are noun clauses (dependent clauses). They use **statement word order**, not question word order. They can be the subject, a direct or indirect object, the object of a preposition or an adjective complement:

| Function | Example (question: *Where is my shoe?*) |
|---|---|
| subject | Where my shoe is will eventually be determined. |
| object | I don't know where my shoe is. |
| object of a preposition | I would like you to focus on where my shoe is. |
| adjective complement | It is unclear where my shoe is. |

### Patterns
| | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Question word + auxiliary + subject + verb? | What does she study? |
| Embedded question: subject | [Question word + subject + verb] + verb | What she studies is difficult. |
| Embedded question: object | Subject + verb + [question word + subject + verb] | I wonder what she studies. |
| Embedded question: adjective complement | It + be + adjective + [question word + subject + verb] | It was obvious what she studies. |

### How to make an embedded question
1. **do / does / did:** remove it and put the tense on the main verb: *What does she study?* → *what she studies*.
2. **be, a modal (can, should ...) or will:** put the subject before the verb: *Where is my shoe?* → *where my shoe is*.
3. **The question word is the subject:** the order does not change: *What will happen?* → *... you cannot choose what will happen.*
4. **Yes/no questions** use *if* or *whether* (Lesson 28): *Do you want a job?* → *He asked if I wanted a job.*
5. **The end punctuation** belongs to the whole sentence: a statement ends with a period (*I don't know where my shoe is.*); a question ends with a question mark (*Do you know where my shoe is?*).

### Noun, adjective or adverb clause?
All three kinds of dependent clause can begin with the same word, for example *when* or *that*:
- **Noun clause** (subject or object): *When they will arrive is unknown.*
- **Adjective clause** (describes a noun): *The day when they arrived was hectic.*
- **Adverb clause** (tells when the main action happens): *When they arrive, we will begin.*

Knowing the difference helps your reading and your word order and punctuation in writing.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no helping verb like *do/does/did* in questions, so this English pattern is new. In an embedded question, remove *do/does/did* and put the tense on the main verb: *Where did he go?* → *I don't know where he went* (not ~~where did he go~~).

## Answer key

**1. Embedded questions in the reading** (book)
1. *when you will start to experience changes* / *what will happen*: object (of *choose*). (The book also marks *how you feel*, after *no matter*, as an object.)
2. *what you can see and feel*: object of a preposition (*about*).
3. *what your favorite song was*: object (of *asked*). (*When you were seven years old* is an adverb clause of time, not an embedded question.)
4. *what people usually refer to*: subject complement. (the book's key says object; this is a mistake because the clause comes after the verb *is*, and *be* has no object: a clause after *be* that tells what the subject (*This*) is, is called a subject complement)
5. *how much your body changes during this time period*: object of a preposition (*of*).
6. *Why you feel these things*: subject.
7. *what you're going through*: object of a preposition (*about*).
8. *what's happening*: object of a preposition (*over*).
9. *how normal it is*: object (of *realize*).

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 120 to 180 words about your adolescence (discussion question 1 in Lesson 32: What was your adolescence like?).
- Use at least five embedded questions: at least one as a **subject** (*What I remember most is ...*), one as an **object** (*I did not understand why ...*), one as the **object of a preposition** (*I worried about what my friends thought*) and one as an **adjective complement** (*It was not clear what I wanted to study*).
- Use the simple past for your own teenage years.

Self-editing checklist:
1. Did I use at least five embedded questions in four different functions?
2. Does every embedded question have statement word order (subject before verb)?
3. Did I remove *do/does/did* and put the tense on the main verb?
4. Did I use *if* or *whether* for an embedded yes/no question?
5. Does each sentence end with the correct mark (a period for a statement, a question mark only if the whole sentence is a question)?
6. Did I use the simple past for past experiences?
