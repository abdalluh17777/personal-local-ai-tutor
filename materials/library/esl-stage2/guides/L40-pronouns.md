# Lesson 40: Pronouns

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 4: Words, Word Order and the Essay*
*Adapted from Building Academic Writing Skills by Lin Cui, Harper College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): Unit 17 Pronouns.*

## In this lesson
You review personal, indefinite (everyone, something) and demonstrative (this, that, these, those) pronouns. You practise noun-pronoun agreement and five rules that prevent common pronoun errors, with texts about population, the census and migrant workers. Correct pronouns make academic writing concise and easy to follow.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Choose the correct subject, object, reflexive and possessive forms.
- Use singular verbs and pronouns with indefinite pronouns such as "everyone" and "something".
- Use "this", "that", "these" and "those" for a noun or for an idea just mentioned.
- Make every pronoun agree with its noun in number and gender.
- Correct common errors such as "my neighbors they", "me and my cousin" and "their / they're".

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pronoun | a word that represents (replaces) a noun | it, they, everyone, this |
| subject pronoun | a pronoun used as the subject, usually before the verb | **They** live in different parts of the globe. |
| object pronoun | a pronoun after a verb or a preposition | We were very proud of **her**. |
| reflexive pronoun | a -self / -selves pronoun that refers back to the subject; "by" + reflexive means "without help" | Children like to try out new things by **themselves**. |
| possessive adjective | a word before a noun that shows who owns it | he brought **his** whole family |
| possessive pronoun | a pronoun that replaces "possessive adjective + noun" | His company hires more foreign workers than **ours**. |
| indefinite pronoun | a pronoun for people, things or ideas in general | everyone, something, nobody |
| demonstrative pronoun | this, that, these, those | **That** is hard work for him. |
| noun-pronoun agreement | a pronoun matches its noun in number (singular / plural) and gender | my niece → her |
| census | an official count of all the people in a country | Many countries conduct a census every five to ten years. |
| concise | clear, without unnecessary words | Pronouns make the language more concise. |
| awkward | unnatural, uncomfortable | Repeating "he or she" can be awkward. |
| conduct | have, perform, do | Does your native country conduct a census regularly? |

## Rules and explanations

### Personal pronouns
| | Subject | Object | Reflexive | Possessive adjective | Possessive pronoun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| singular | I, you, he, she, it | me, you, him, her, it | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself | my, your, his, her, its | mine, yours, his, hers, (none for "it") |
| plural | we, you, they | us, you, them | ourselves, yourselves, themselves | our, your, their | ours, yours, theirs |

- **Subject pronoun**: the subject, usually before the verb. "There are many people in the world. **They** live in different parts of the globe."
- **Object pronoun**: after a verb or a preposition. "We celebrated **it** with a big party." "We were very proud of **her**."
- **Reflexive pronoun**: refers back to the subject. "My niece was able to feed **herself** at ten months old." With "by" it means "independently": "Children like to try out new things by **themselves**."
- **Possessive adjective** + noun: "**her** new life".
- **Possessive pronoun**: replaces "possessive adjective + noun" already mentioned. "Now, **hers** has more than **mine**." (hers = her country; mine = my country). Also: "a friend of **mine**".

### Indefinite pronouns
- For people: everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, no one, nobody. Use them like "he" or "she".
- For things and ideas: everything, anything, something, nothing. Use them like "it".
- They are singular, so the verb and the other pronouns are singular: "Everyone **is** doing **his/her** best for the family."
- Repeating "he or she" can be awkward, so writers often use a plural noun instead: "All people are doing their best for the family."
- In this book's exercises, use "his/her" or a plural noun. (Many style guides today, including APA style, which psychology uses, also accept "they / their" for one person, as in "Everyone should report their information". Follow your teacher's rule.)

### Demonstrative pronouns
| | Close by | Far away |
|---|---|---|
| singular | this | that |
| plural | these | those |

- "Joe went on two short trips with his family. **Those** were happy trips."
- "This" and "that" can also mean the idea or situation just mentioned: "Joe had not been home for two years. **That** was hard for the family." (That = the situation that Joe had not been home for two years.)

### Noun-pronoun agreement
A pronoun must match the noun it refers to:
- in **number**: singular noun → singular pronoun; plural noun → plural pronoun.
- in **gender**: masculine (he, him, his), feminine (she, her) or gender-neutral (it, its, they). "My uncle ... **he** brought **his** whole family." "They love the U.S. for **its** better educational and career opportunities."

### Five more rules
1. Do not add a pronoun after the subject. Wrong: My neighbors they migrated to the U.S. last year. Right: My neighbors migrated ...
2. Do not use "me and my ...". Wrong: Me and my cousin live in the same city. Right: My cousin and I live in the same city. Our grandparents visit my cousin and me every year.
3. If a pronoun is confusing, write the noun. "Sanjay told Guang that he was happy about his job." (Who is "he"?) Clear: "Sanjay was happy about Guang's job, and he told him so."
4. Change the pronouns in reported speech so the meaning is logical. Wrong: He said that you should check them online. Right: He said that I should check them online. / He said, "You should check them online."
5. Do not confuse: they're (they are) ≠ their; you're (you are) ≠ your; it's (it is) ≠ its. "**It's** 100 miles away from home." "**Its** location is not convenient, but **its** salary is good."

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic has no neuter "it": every noun is masculine or feminine. In English, use "it" (or "they" for plurals) for things, ideas and organizations: "The Worldometer is an organization ... **It** provides real time statistics."
> - Arabic adds a pronoun inside relative clauses ("the book that I read it"). English does not use extra pronouns: "the book that I read"; "My neighbors migrated ..." (not "My neighbors they migrated").

## Read aloud

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 150 to 200 words on one topic from Exercise 9:
- What do you think of leaving home to work in a far-away city or country? What would be the advantages and disadvantages for you and your family?
- Does your native country conduct a census regularly? If so, how often? How are the census results used?

Include a title, a topic sentence, supporting ideas with details, and a concluding sentence. Use subject, object, possessive and reflexive pronouns, at least one indefinite pronoun (everyone, someone...) and one "this" or "that" for an idea.

Self-editing checklist:
- Every pronoun clearly refers to one noun and agrees with it in number and gender ("it" for things and organizations).
- Indefinite pronouns have singular verbs and pronouns (or I used a plural noun).
- No extra pronoun after a subject ("my parents they").
- "My family and I", not "me and my family".
- they're / their, you're / your and it's / its are spelled correctly.
