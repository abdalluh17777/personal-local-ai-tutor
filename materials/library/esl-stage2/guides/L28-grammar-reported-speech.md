# Lesson 28: Grammar: Reported Speech

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 8.4: Grammar Presentation-Direct and Indirect (Reported) Speech; 8.5: Applying Grammar to Reading; 8.6: Communication Practice.*

## In this lesson
You will learn to report what other people said or wrote: the changes in verb tenses, pronouns, time and place words, *that* or *if*, and *said* or *told*. Then you will find the reporting verbs in "Mirror Stage" (Lesson 27). Academic writers report researchers' ideas all the time, so this grammar helps you summarize sources for your psychology course.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Change direct speech into reported speech, with the correct verb tenses.
- Change pronouns, possessive adjectives, time words and place words.
- Use *that* for statements, *if* or *whether* for yes/no questions and *to* + verb for commands.
- Choose between *say* and *tell*, and avoid mixing "according to" with a reporting verb.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| direct speech | someone's exact words, inside quotation marks | The man said, "I can't come to your party." |
| reported (indirect) speech | the same information in your own words, without quotation marks | The man said that he couldn't come to my party. |
| reporting verb | a verb that introduces speech, e.g. say, tell, ask, inquire, reply, state, report, complain, explain, remark, note | Wallon noted that ... |
| tense change | moving the verb one step back into the past in reported speech | "I study hard." → He said that he studied hard. |
| imperative | a command; it starts with the base verb and has no subject | "Bring your books to class tomorrow." |
| yes/no question | a question that you answer with yes or no | "Do you want a job?" → He asked if I wanted a job. |
| direct object | the noun or pronoun that receives the action; after *tell*, the person you speak to | Pedro told Danielle to turn off the lights. |
| according to | a phrase that names the source of an idea, followed by a comma and a complete sentence | According to Claude Steele, stereotype threat affects students' performance. |

## Rules and explanations

### Direct and reported speech
- **Direct speech** repeats the exact words in quotation marks. **Reported speech** gives the same information in your own words; native speakers almost always use it. Subjects, verbs, possessive adjectives (my, his ...) and places change: *My wife told the delivery person, "Put the boxes here."* → *My wife told the delivery person to put the boxes there.*

### Verb tense changes
After a **past** reporting verb (said, told, asked), move the verb back:

| Direct speech | Reported: He said (that) he ... | Change |
|---|---|---|
| "I study hard." | studied hard. | simple present → simple past |
| "I am studying hard." | was studying hard. | present progressive → past progressive |
| "I will study hard." | would study hard. | will → would |
| "I am going to study hard." | was going to study hard. | am/is/are going to → was/were going to |
| "I can study hard." | could study hard. | can → could |
| "I have studied hard." | had studied hard. | present perfect → past perfect |
| "I studied hard." | had studied hard. | simple past → past perfect |

- **No change** after a reporting verb in the simple present, present perfect or simple future: *He says, "I have studied hard."* → *He says that he has studied hard.* (A present reporting verb usually means you are reporting immediately.)
- **That is optional:** *He said (that) he studied hard.* Native speakers often leave it out.

### Pronouns, time words and place words
- I → he/she · you → I/we/she/he/they (it depends who is meant) · my → his/her · our → their · your → my/our
- here → there · now → then · this/these → that/those · today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before

### Three patterns: that, if, to
- Statement → **that**: *He said that the cake was beautiful.*
- Yes/no question → **if** (or *whether*): *He asked, "Do you want a job?"* → *He asked if I wanted a job.*
- Command (imperative) → **to** + verb (an infinitive): *He told them, "Do your homework."* → *He told them to do their homework.* (The book's table also has "He demanded them to stop talking"; this is not standard English. Use "He told them to stop talking".)
- Question with a question word → the question word: *"How much will it cost?"* → *I asked how much it would cost.* (More in Lesson 33.)

### Said or told?
- **tell** needs a direct object (the person): *Pedro told Danielle to turn off the lights.*
- **say** has no direct object: *Pedro said to turn off the lights.* (*Pedro said to Danielle, "..."* is possible but less common.) So: not ~~He said me~~.

### Common error: "according to"
- Correct: *According to Claude Steele, stereotype threat affects students' performance.* (comma, then a complete sentence)
- Correct: *Claude Steele states that stereotype threat affects students' performance.*
- Incorrect: ~~According to Claude Steele states stereotype threat affects students' performance.~~ Do not use "according to" and a reporting verb together.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - In Arabic, the verb after "said that" usually keeps the tense of the original words. In English, after a past reporting verb, move the verb back: *She said that she was tired* (not ~~She said that she is tired~~).

**1. Exercise 3: Verb changes in reported speech.** Choose the correct verb for the reported speech.
1. Alex said, "I will be home in August." → Alex said he ________ be home in August. a) will b) would c) is going to be

4. The woman said to her wife, "We have to fix the broken window." → The woman said to her wife that they ________ to fix the broken window. a) have b) has c) had

8. The teacher told the students, "Bring your books to class tomorrow." → The teacher told the students to ________ their books to class the next day. a) bring b) brought c) bringing

## Read aloud
In reported speech, *that* is weak: /ðət/. Read each sentence twice, with *that* and without it.
1. The man said that he couldn't come to my party.
2. My sister says that she wants to win the lottery.
3. Claude Steele states that stereotype threat affects students' performance.
4. He said that he would study hard.

## Writing task
Write 100 to 150 words that report what Molly Wright said in "How Every Child Can Thrive by Five" (Lesson 27; use the transcript). Name the speaker and the talk in your first sentence. Report at least five of her ideas with different reporting verbs (said, explained, told, noted ...), and write one sentence with "According to ...,". For a fact that is still true, you may use a present reporting verb with no tense change (*Molly Wright explains that ...*).

Self-editing checklist:
1. Does every sentence with her ideas have a reporting verb?
2. After each past reporting verb, did I change the tense (is → was, can → could, will → would)?
3. Did I change the pronouns (I → she, our → their)?
4. Did I use *tell* + a person and *say* without a person?
5. Did I write "According to Molly Wright," with a comma and no reporting verb after it?

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
