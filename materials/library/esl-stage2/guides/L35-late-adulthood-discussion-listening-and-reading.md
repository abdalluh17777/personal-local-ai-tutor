## In this lesson
You will think about long life, watch a TED-Ed lesson about population pyramids, and read two short texts about the "graying" (aging) of the population in the United States and around the world. You will learn how demographers describe a population and predict its future, and the language academic texts use for predictions. This prepares you for the simple future in Lesson 36 and for reading statistics in your psychology course.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Explain what a population pyramid shows and what its shape predicts.
- Answer detail questions about a short academic video and a text full of statistics.
- Recognize the language of predictions: will, be projected to, be expected to, it's projected that.
- Describe the "graying" of the population in the United States and in other countries.
- Discuss the factors that contribute to living a longer life.

|---|---|---|

| de·MOG·ra·pher | a scientist who studies populations | One of the ways that demographers represent the internal distribution of a population is the population pyramid. |
| de·mo·GRAPH·ic | about a population (its size, ages, origins) | The year 2030 marks an important demographic turning point in U.S. history. |
| re·pro·DUC·tive years | the ages when people usually have children (15-44 in the video) | As today's children reach their reproductive years ... |
| life ex·PEC·tan·cy | how many years people live on average | Countries ... typically see an increase in life expectancy. |
| birth rate / death rate | how many people are born / die in a year, compared with the size of the population | Both birth and death rates are low. |
| in·dus·tri·al·i·ZA·tion | the change from a farming economy to one with factories and industry | Countries that have only recently begun the process of industrialization ... |
| baby boomers | people born in the baby boom (a time when many babies were born) after World War II | By 2030, all baby boomers will be older than age 65. |

## Rules and explanations
### How a population pyramid works (from the video)
- Each bar shows the population numbers for a five-year age group, from 0-4 up to 100 and up. Females are on one side, males on the other.
- The groups: pre-reproductive (0-14), reproductive (15-44) and post-reproductive (45 and up).
- The shape predicts the future:

| Country | Where most people are | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| Rwanda | in the youngest age groups, at the bottom | fast growth: the population is almost certain to double within the next few decades |
| Canada | around the middle of the graph | the population will grow more slowly |
| Japan | in the post-reproductive years | the population will begin to decline |

- These three pyramids show three stages of a demographic transition, as a country moves from a pre-industrial society to an industrial or post-industrial economy. Early in industrialization, life expectancy goes up and child mortality goes down, while birth rates stay the same: a population boom. Later, birth rates fall (more education and opportunities for women, a move from rural to urban living). In advanced stages, birth and death rates are both low, and the population is stable or even begins to decline.
- A pyramid is also a record of the past: Russia's shows World War II, China's shows the one child policy, and the United States' shows the baby boom after World War II.

### The language of predictions
Texts about populations make many predictions. Notice these forms; you will study will and be going to in Lesson 36.

| Form | Example from the video or the reading |
|---|---|
| will + base verb | The number will grow rapidly in the coming years. |
| won't (will not) + base verb | But this similarity won't last long. |
| by + a future time, + will | By 2030, all baby boomers will be older than age 65. |
| be projected to + base verb | Japan ... is projected to retain (keep) this position through at least 2050. |
| be expected to + base verb | The population is expected to grow at a slower pace. |
| it's projected that + clause | By 2035, it's projected that there will be 76.7 million people under the age of 18. |
| be almost certain to + base verb | The total population is almost certain to double. |

- "By 2030" means "not later than 2030".
- "Is projected to" and "is expected to" tell the reader that the prediction comes from data, here the U.S. Census Bureau's National Population Projections. They are useful in academic writing when you report what researchers predict.

### Questions with "not"
Listening question 4 and reading question 2 ask what is NOT given or NOT projected. Check every option against the video or the text. The answer is the option that the source does not support.

## Answer key
**Discussion** (example answer)
1. The oldest person I know is my grandfather. He is 91 years old, and he still walks to the market every morning.
2. I believe that several factors contribute to living a longer life. The first is a healthy lifestyle: eating well, staying active and not smoking. Good medical care is also important. Finally, close family and friends keep older people busy and happy, and this may help them live longer.

**Exercise 1**
1. d) 1,663,200 (book). 165 per minute × 60 minutes × 24 hours × 7 days = 1,663,200.
2. d) all of the above (book). The video names industrialization and education and opportunities for women; it also shows that events such as a war can change a pyramid.
3. c) become more rectangular (book). Like Canada's pyramid: fewer young people, so the bars become more equal.
4. c) life expectancy (book). The pyramid shows the population numbers for each age group.
5. a) Africa (book). Rwanda, the video's fast-growing country, is in Africa.
6. a) (book). "Countries in advanced stages of industrialization reach a point where both birth and death rates are low, and the population remains stable or even begins to decline."

**Exercise 2**
1. b) False (book). The term means that the population is "becoming more dominated by older people": the median age is going up.
2. c) The population will grow quicker. (book) The text says that it "is expected to grow at a slower pace". Careful: option b is not exactly true either. Migration will overtake natural increase (births minus deaths), not births: births will still be "nearly four times larger" than net migration. The book means b as a true change, so c is the answer.
3. a) (book). "A rising number of deaths will increasingly offset how much births are able to contribute to population growth."
4. c) Europe (book). "Of the world's 25 oldest countries, 22 are in Europe."

## Read aloud
- Russia, with the largest territory in the world, has roughly the same total population as Nigeria, a country 1/16 (one-sixteenth) its size.
- But this similarity won't last long.
- Such a population PYR·a·mid can be a powerful predictor of future pop·u·LA·tion trends.
- By 2030 (twenty thirty), all baby boomers will be older than age 65 (sixty-five).
- The population is expected to grow at a slower pace, age considerably and become more racially and ethnically diverse.
- Japan, however, is currently the oldest nation in the world and is projected to retain this position through at least 2050 (twenty fifty).
