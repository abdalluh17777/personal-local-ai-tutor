# Lesson 10: Grammar: Phrasal Verbs

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 1: Academic Writing Essentials · Psychology*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 3.4: Grammar Presentation-Phrasal Verbs; 3.5: Applying Grammar to Reading; 3.6: Communication Practice; Phrasal Verbs.*

## In this lesson
You will learn what phrasal verbs are, the difference between separable and inseparable phrasal verbs, where to put a pronoun object, and how one phrasal verb can have several meanings. You will also find phrasal verbs in "The Value of Confusion". Lecturers use them all the time, so they help you follow your classes.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Explain what a phrasal verb is and give the meaning of common ones.
- Put a noun or pronoun object in the correct place with a separable phrasal verb (gave it up, not "gave up it").
- Keep an inseparable phrasal verb together (keep up with it).
- Choose the correct meaning of a phrasal verb with several meanings from its context.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| phrasal verb | a verb + a small word (up, off, out, down, on ...) with one meaning | put off = delay or postpone |
| separable | a noun or pronoun can go between the verb and the small word | I look the new word up in the dictionary. |
| inseparable | the words must stay together | I look up to my grandmother. |
| object | the noun or pronoun that receives the action | Hyun-Ki gave up smoking. |
| object pronoun | it, them, him, her, me, us, you | Hyun-Ki gave it up. |
| transitive | can have an object | The teacher is trying to keep up with it. |
| intransitive | has no object | My car broke down. |
| three-word phrasal verb | a verb + two small words; most cannot be separated | keep up with, put up with |

## Rules and explanations
### What is a phrasal verb?
- A verb followed by a small word (the book calls it a preposition) that together form one meaning: put off = delay or postpone. The meaning is often different from the meanings of the two words, so learn each one like a new word.
- Two kinds: I looked up the new word in the dictionary. / I look the new word up in the dictionary. (separable: kept together or separated) I look up to my grandmother. (inseparable)

### Separable phrasal verbs
| Pattern | Example | |
|---|---|---|
| verb + small word + noun | Hyun-Ki gave up smoking. | correct |
| verb + noun + small word | Hyun-Ki gave smoking up. | correct |
| verb + pronoun + small word | Hyun-Ki gave it up. | correct |
| verb + small word + pronoun | Hyun-Ki gave up it. | WRONG |

- A noun can go in either place. A pronoun (it, them, him, her) must go between the two words.
- A few must always be separated: ask over (invite) and call back (return a phone call).
- Examples: bring up (bring attention to), call off (cancel), carry out (implement), find out (learn new information), leave out (exclude), let down (disappoint), put off (postpone), take off (remove). Full list: Table 11 in the lesson material.

### Inseparable phrasal verbs
- They must stay together, and a noun or a pronoun comes after them: The teacher is trying to keep up with grading the homework. The teacher is trying to keep up with it.
- Tip: most three-word phrasal verbs cannot be separated.
- Examples: carry on (continue), get over (recover), go over (review), keep up with (maintain pace), look after (care for), put up with (tolerate), stand for (represent), take after (resemble). Full list: Table 12 in the lesson material.

### Phrasal verbs with multiple meanings
The same words can have several meanings. They can be separable with one meaning and inseparable with another, or transitive with one meaning and intransitive with another: My car broke down (no object). The teacher broke down the math problem (object). The book's examples are in Table 13 in the lesson material. Meanings for Exercise 9:

- **work out:** a) exercise b) solve a problem
- **pick up:** a) lift something b) give someone a ride c) learn a new skill d) buy something at the store e) notice something; detect f) start again g) improve
- **turn down:** a) reduce intensity b) reject an offer or request
- **make up:** a) invent b) compose c) no longer be upset d) compensate for a lack of something
- **make out:** a) be able to identify or see with great effort b) write details on a check c) kiss in a sexual way
- **look up:** a) search b) improve c) admire
- **cut off:** a) remove something b) stop the supply of something c) interrupt someone d) cause disconnection of a phone or internet
- **go out:** a) leave your home b) stop burning

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic has no phrasal verbs like give up or put off, and their meaning usually cannot be guessed from the two words. Learn each one with its meaning and an example sentence, as in the book's tables.

## Read aloud
In phrasal verbs like these, the small word (up, back, off) is usually stressed, especially at the end of a sentence.
1. Hyun-Ki gave it UP.
2. Wei forgot to give it BACK.
3. Please turn it OFF.
4. The teacher will not put UP with them.
5. Before you give UP, make sure you have someone to help you.

## Writing task
**A chain story on your own (Exercise 11).** Write a story of 120 to 160 words that begins: "Yesterday began like any other day." It can be shocking, funny or embarrassing, but it should make sense. Use at least six phrasal verbs: two separable ones with a pronoun object (picked it up), two inseparable ones (got over it), and one with more than one meaning. Use the simple past, and underline the phrasal verbs.

Checklist:
- Every pronoun object is between the verb and the small word (turned it off, NOT turned off it).
- My inseparable phrasal verbs stay together (looked after them).
- Each phrasal verb has the meaning I want (I checked the book's lists).
- Irregular past forms are correct: gave up, took off, broke down, made up.
- The story has a clear beginning, middle and end.

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
