# Lesson 26: Present Tenses in Writing

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from Building Academic Writing Skills by Lin Cui, Harper College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): Unit 11 Expressing Meaning in Present Tenses.*

## In this lesson
You will learn how to choose and form the four present tenses (simple present, present progressive, present perfect and present perfect progressive) by starting with your meaning and matching your time words. You will take a pretest (do Practice 1 before you read the rules), correct paragraphs about money and parking, complete paragraphs about hobbies and write a short letter about a hobby. Academic writing uses these tenses all the time: the simple present for facts and the present perfect for situations from the past up to now.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Choose a present tense by meaning, and make the time words, tense and verb form match.
- Form the simple present correctly ("be" verb or "do" verb, -s with he/she/it, no -s after does).
- Use the present progressive for actions now and in the present period, but not with non-action verbs.
- Use the present perfect and present perfect progressive for situations that started in the past, actions at an unspecified past time, and repeated actions.
- Find and correct present-tense errors in a paragraph.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simple present | base verb (the verb with no to, -s, -ed or -ing), or base verb + -s for he/she/it | Money plays an important role in society. |
| present progressive (pro·GRES·sive) | am/is/are + verb-ing | The professor is checking our homework now. |
| present perfect | have/has + past participle (the third form: gone, written, loved) | Jiali has loved stamps since she was a child. |
| present perfect progressive | have/has + been + verb-ing | She has been accumulating stamps for over ten years. |
| "be" verb / "do" verb | am, is, are / every other main verb | Money is important. / Money plays a role. |
| non-action verb | a verb for a state, not an action: feelings, thoughts, senses, possession (know, need, see, look, have, cost); not used in progressive tenses | The parking lots at my college look full. |
| time expression, frequency adverb | words that show time (now, since, these days) or how often (always, usually, never) | My sister and I usually go shopping on the weekends. |
| convey (con·VEY) | verb: express, show | The purpose of communication is to convey meaning. |
| guarantee (guar·an·TEE) | verb: promise, ensure | Does money guarantee happiness? |
| sibling (SIB·ling) | noun: a brother or sister | taking care of a younger sibling |
| addictive (ad·DIC·tive) | adjective: becoming a habit, cannot stop | addictive computer games |
| accumulate (ac·CU·mu·late) | verb: gather, collect | How many years has she accumulated stamps? |
| pursue (pur·SUE) | verb: follow with great interest | How long have you been pursuing it? |

## Rules and explanations
### Three principles
1. **Start with meaning.** Ask: What ideas am I trying to express?
2. **Tense and verb form are an inseparable pair.** For current activities, use a present tense, not a past form with -ed.
3. **Time words are like traffic signs.** Time words, tense and form must match: do not use "yesterday" for an event in progress now.

### Simple present
1. With am/is/are as the main verb, use no "do" verb; with a "do" verb, use no am/is/are. Not "Money is plays an important role" but "Money plays an important role"; not "People are use different currencies" but "People use different currencies". Every sentence needs a verb: not "Money useful in people's lives" but "Money is useful in people's lives."
2. He/she/it + a "do" verb: add -s. "Many people save money because it gives them a sense of security."
3. In questions and negatives with does/doesn't, leave off the -s: "Does money guarantee happiness? No, it does not."

- **Meaning 1: facts and truths.** Often with always, constantly, frequently, usually, often, generally, sometimes, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, never. "The Department of Treasury manages the currency in the U.S."
- **Meaning 2: current habits, routines, repeated activities.** Every day, each night, once a week, twice a day, on Tuesdays. "My sister tries to save money on many things, but she does not save money on shoes."

### Present progressive: am/is/are + verb-ing
- **Meaning 1: happening at the moment of speaking.** Now, right now, at the moment, at present. "Is Eva sitting next to Maria?"
- **Meaning 2: happening during the present period.** Currently, today, these days, nowadays, this semester. "More students are working out in the new fitness center these days."
- It can also show future plans (Lesson 34, Future Tenses in Writing).
- **No non-action verbs.** From Lesson 5: feelings and thoughts (believe, know, like, love, mean, need, prefer, understand, want), the five senses (appear, feel, hear, look, see, seem, smell, taste), possession (belong, contain, have, own) and others (cost, find). Not "The parking lots at my college are looking full" but "... look full" (the book's example in Exercise 2).

### Present perfect: have/has + past participle
- **Meaning 1: started in the past, continues till now, may go on.** Since, for, recently, lately, so far, to date, till now, in the past/last two weeks, today, these days, this week, all life. "Jiali has loved stamps since she was a child."
- **Meaning 2: completed at an unspecified time in the past.** Already, before, just, never, yet (sometimes no time words). "Jiali has just joined a stamp-collecting club at her college." "The gifts have not arrived yet."
- **Meaning 3: repeated in an unspecified past time or since a past time, and may happen again.** Once, two times, a few times, many times. "So far, she has gathered over five hundred stamps from different countries." "She has attended the club meeting three times since the beginning of the semester."
- Irregular past participles: Appendix D (Lesson 29).

### Present perfect progressive: have/has + been + verb-ing
- **Meaning 1:** the same as present perfect Meaning 1, **with more emphasis**. "Jiali has been collecting stamps since she was ten years old."
- **Meaning 2: still going on, with no time expression.** "The stamp club has been expanding."
- No non-action verbs here either: "has loved", not "has been loving".

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic present-tense sentences need no "be", so "Money useful" is a common error. English always needs a verb: "Money is useful."
> - Arabic has no present perfect and uses the present tense with "since/for". English needs the present perfect (progressive): not "She is waiting for ten minutes" but "She has been waiting for ten minutes."

## Read aloud
Mark the -s ending sound: /s/ after voiceless sounds, /z/ after voiced sounds, /ɪz/ after s, z, sh, ch, j sounds.

1. Money plays /z/ an important role in society.
2. Many people save money because it gives /z/ them a sense of security.
3. The amount of money starts /s/ from a few dollars a week and increases /ɪz/ as the children grow.
4. Many parents believe that the allowance helps /s/ the children learn the value of money.
5. The Department of Treasury manages /ɪz/ the currency in the U.S.

## Writing task
**Book Exercise 6.** Write a letter (to a friend, or to your tutor) of about 150-200 words on one topic:

- What is your favorite hobby? How long have you been pursuing it? Why do you like it? Do you enjoy it alone or with other people?
- What do you think are some of the reasons why some people do not have a hobby? Can people remain happy without a hobby? Why or why not?

Use all four present tenses at least once, each with a matching time expression.

Self-editing checklist:
- Every sentence has a main verb.
- I never put am/is/are before the base form of a "do" verb ("is play").
- He/she/it + "do" verb has -s; after does/doesn't there is no -s.
- I used the present progressive only for now or the present period, and never with a non-action verb.
- I used the present perfect (progressive) with since, for, so far, already, yet, and so on.
- My time words, tenses and verb forms match.
