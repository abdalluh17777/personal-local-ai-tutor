# Lesson 16: Grammar: Modals

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 2: Sentences and Paragraph Types · American History*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 5.4: Grammar Presentation-Modals.*

## In this lesson
You will study the eight main modal auxiliaries and the expressions have to and used to. You will use them to show how certain you are, to make polite requests, to give advice, to talk about the past, and to express regret and criticism. In academic writing, modals show how sure you are of a claim (must, may, might) and how strong a recommendation is (should, must).

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Form statements, negatives and questions with a modal and a base verb.
- Show your level of certainty with must, may, might and could.
- Make polite requests and give advice with the right modal.
- Talk about past habits, abilities and necessities with used to, would, could and had to.
- Express regret and criticism with should have, could have and might have + past participle, and correct common modal errors.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| modal auxiliary (modal) | a helping verb used only with a main verb; it adds mood: ability, possibility, advice, necessity | James may call. |
| base verb (base form) | the simple form of a verb, with no to, -s, -ed or -ing | Should I **drive**? |
| possibility | something that is perhaps true or may happen | She might be sick. |
| inference | a strong guess based on what you see or know | He must be sick. |
| advisability (advice) | what is a good idea to do | You should drink hot water with honey. |
| obligation, necessity | something you must do; a rule or a requirement | I have to be at work at 9AM. |
| past participle | the third form of a verb (brought, eaten, called) | I should have brought a jacket. |
| regret | a feeling that you wish you had acted differently | I should not have eaten all the chocolate. |
| criticism | saying that someone did something wrong | You could have kept my secret! |

## Rules and explanations

### Form
| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | subject + modal + base verb | James may call. |
| Negative | subject + modal + not + base verb | James may not call. |
| Yes/no question | modal + subject + base verb? | Should I drive? |

A modal never changes its form: no -s (he must, not he musts), no -ed, and no to after it. (Have to and used to are not among the eight modals, so they keep their to.)

### The eight main modals and their uses
| Modal | Uses |
|---|---|
| can | ability; possibility; polite request |
| could | past ability; present possibility or suggestion; past or future permission; polite request |
| may | uncertain future action; permission; polite request |
| might | uncertain action |
| should | obligation; expectation; advisability |
| will | intended future action; asking a favor (Will you buy me some chocolate?) or for information |
| would | preference (I would like the steak, please); polite request; explaining an action; habitual past action |
| must | obligation; inference |

### 1. Possibility and inference: how certain are you?
Your teacher is absent. You might say:
- She is sick. (100% certain: no modal)
- She must be sick. (about 95% certain)
- She may be sick. / She might be sick. / She could be sick. (less than 50% certain)

### 2. Polite requests
- Could, would and may are equally polite. Can is less formal but still polite: use it with people you know well.
- May asks permission, so its subject is I: May I have a cup of water? The other modals can have any subject: Could you give me a cup of water?
- Add please: Could I please have a cup of water? Also: Would you mind giving me a cup of water? (would you mind + verb-ing)

### 3. Advice, expectation, obligation and necessity
- **should** = advice (You should drink hot water with honey.), expectation (You should be finished with your reading by now.) or obligation (I should be at work at 9AM.).
- **must / have to** = strong necessity: I must be at work at 9AM. I have to be at work at 9AM.

### 4. Past situations that are no longer true
- used to + base verb (past habit): I used to travel to many countries.
- would + base verb (past habit): I would visit new and exciting places.
- could + base verb (past ability): I could handle the long flights with no problems.
- had to + base verb (past necessity): I had to save my money to buy my plane tickets.

### 5. Regret and criticism (past modals)
- Regret: subject + should / should not + have + past participle. I should have brought a jacket. I should not have eaten all the chocolate.
- Criticism: subject + might / could + have + past participle. You might have called me to say you were late. You could have kept my secret!
- Contractions: should've, shouldn't have, might've, could've.
- Present or past? should + base verb gives advice for now or the future. should have + past participle talks about the past, and the action did not happen.

### Common errors
| Error | Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| infinitive after a modal | I can to move this heavy table. | I can move this heavy table. |
| gerund (-ing) after a modal | I could moving to the United States. | I could move to the United States. |
| two modals in a row | I should must renew my passport. | I must renew my passport. / I should renew my passport. |
| no modal | I renew my passport. | I must renew my passport. |

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - In Arabic, the words for "must" and "can" are followed by a small word that works like "to" (yajib an, yastatee' an + verb), so learners often add to in English: "I must to study", "He can to swim". After the eight modals, use the base verb with no to.

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 8-10 sentences about your childhood, like Rachel's paragraph in Exercise 6.
- Begin with a topic sentence, for example: "When I was a child, I lived in a small town."
- Use used to, would, could and had to at least once each.
- End with one regret (I should have / I should not have + past participle) and one piece of advice for children today (Children should ...).

Checklist:
- Is there a base verb after every modal (could lift, not could lifted)?
- Is there no to after can, could, would, should or must, while used to and had to keep their to?
- Is there only one modal in each verb (no "should must")?
- Is there a past participle after should have (should have studied, not should have study)?
- Are your other past verbs in the simple past?

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
