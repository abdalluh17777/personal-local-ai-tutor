# Lesson 23: Grammar: Passive Voice

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 2: Sentences and Paragraph Types · American History*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 6.4: Grammar Presentation-Passive Voice; 6.5: Applying Grammar to Reading; 6.6: Communication Practice.*

## In this lesson
You will learn how to form the passive voice in nine tenses, which verbs can be passive, and when to choose the passive instead of the active. Then you will find passive sentences in the reading "Japanese Relocation During World War II" and ask a critical question: when a history text uses the passive, who is the hidden performer? Passive voice is very common in academic textbooks, so recognizing and using it helps you read and write about research and history.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Tell active sentences from passive sentences.
- Form the passive with be (in the right tense) + past participle, with or without by + performer.
- Explain why only transitive verbs can be passive.
- Choose the passive when the receiver is more important or the performer is unknown.
- Find the hidden performer in a passive sentence in a history text.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| active voice | the subject is the performer of the verb | The boy ate the cookies. |
| passive voice | the subject is the receiver of the action: be + past participle | The cookies were eaten by the boy. |
| performer | the person or thing that does the action | by the boy |
| receiver | the person or thing that the action happens to | the cookies |
| direct object | the noun after a verb that receives the action | Sarina played the piano. |
| transitive verb | a verb that has a direct object; it can be passive | I drink coffee. |
| intransitive verb | a verb with no direct object; it cannot be passive | The student arrived early. |
| past participle | the third form of a verb | eaten, written, given, done |
| conjugate (v) | to change the form of a verb for its subject and tense | is, was, has been, will be |
| tense | the form of a verb that shows time | simple past, present perfect |
| critical reading | reading carefully and asking what a text shows and what it hides | Who are the performers, and why aren't they mentioned? |

## Rules and explanations

### Active and passive
- **Active:** subject (performer) + verb + direct object (receiver). The boy ate the cookies.
- **Passive:** direct object (receiver) + be + past participle + (by + performer). The cookies were eaten by the boy.
- The be verb is conjugated for the tense. The by + performer part is not required: you can leave it out.
- Passive = be + **past participle**. Be + verb-ing is a progressive tense, and it is active: Patty is tutoring the student.

### Only transitive verbs can be passive
The passive needs a direct object, so only transitive verbs can be passive.
- Transitive: I drink coffee. → Coffee is drunk. Sarina played the piano. → The piano was played by Sarina. They have done their homework. → Their homework has been done.
- Intransitive (no object, no passive): I slept. The accident happened. The student arrived early.

### Verb tense and voice
| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| simple present | Patty tutors the student. | The student is tutored by Patty. |
| present progressive | Patty is tutoring the student. | The student is being tutored by Patty. |
| present perfect | Patty has tutored the student. | The student has been tutored by Patty. |
| simple past | Patty tutored the student. | The student was tutored by Patty. |
| past progressive | Patty was tutoring the student. | The student was being tutored by Patty. |
| past perfect | Patty had tutored the student. | The student had been tutored by Patty. |
| simple future | Patty will tutor the student. | The student will be tutored by Patty. |
| future (be going to) | Patty is going to tutor the student. | The student is going to be tutored by Patty. |
| future perfect | Patty will have tutored the student. | The student will have been tutored by Patty. |

Negatives and questions work like other be sentences: The student was not tutored by Patty. Was the student tutored by Patty?

### Which voice should you use?
1. Use the passive when the verb or the receiver is more important than the performer. Active: People eat black-eyed peas for New Year's Day. Passive: Black-eyed peas are eaten for New Year's Day. A verb with two objects has two passives: My son was offered a scholarship. OR A scholarship was offered to my son.
2. Use the passive when the performer is unknown: The bank was robbed last night. Thousands of innocent people were killed.

### Passive voice and history: who is hidden?
When history is told in the passive voice, important performers are sometimes hidden. The writer focuses on the victims and protects the image of the performer: Native Americans were often relocated by force. (By whom?) When you read a passive sentence in a history text, ask: Who is the performer? Why is it not mentioned?

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic forms the passive by changing the vowels inside the verb, with no helping verb, so learners often leave out be: "Relocation centers situated many miles inland." English needs be + past participle: Relocation centers were situated many miles inland.

## Read aloud
- The cookies were eaten by the boy. (were = /wər/)
- The student is being tutored by Patty.
- Their homework has been done. (been = /bɪn/)
- Black-eyed peas are eaten for New Year's Day.
- My son was offered a scholarship. (was = /wəz/)
- Relocation centers were situated many miles inland, often in remote and desolate locales.

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 8-10 sentences about your favorite celebration (Exercise 11). Begin with a passive topic sentence, like the book's example: "Eid al-Fitr is celebrated after the month of Ramadan."
- Describe the food and drinks, clothes, activities, decorations and places.
- Use at least six passive verbs. Use the simple present for traditions (Special sweets are made ...) and the simple past for one memory (Last year, our house was decorated ...).
- Include the performer (by + noun) only when it is important.

Checklist:
- Does every passive verb have be + past participle (are made, not are make or made)?
- Is the be verb in the right tense and does it agree with its subject (sweets are, the house is)?
- Are the past participles correct (eaten, worn, given, thrown)?
- Did you use only transitive verbs in the passive (not "was happened")?
- Did you leave out "by + performer" when the performer is unknown or not important?
