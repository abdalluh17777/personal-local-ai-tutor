# Lesson 44: Editing and Grammar Review

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 4: Words, Word Order and the Essay*
*Adapted from Building Academic Writing Skills by Lin Cui, Harper College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): Unit 20 Editing / Grammar Review.*

## In this lesson
This is the last lesson of Stage 2. You review the editing skills of the course by correcting real sentences from student writing. Then you write a five-paragraph essay and edit it one error type at a time, the way good academic writers check their work.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Find and correct errors in sentence structure, punctuation, verbs, nouns, pronouns, word forms and word order.
- Correct sentences with several types of errors, and know which types you make most often.
- Plan, write and edit a five-paragraph essay.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fragment | an incomplete sentence (no subject, no verb, or only a dependent clause) | Is rich in vitamin C. |
| run-on sentence | two sentences joined with no punctuation or connecting word | Many of us went through many experiences some of them are interesting. |
| comma splice | two sentences joined only with a comma | I will see her in a few weeks, she will come to Chicago. |
| dependent clause | a clause (subject + verb) that cannot stand alone, for example after "when, because, although" | Whenever a storm comes, ... |
| subject-verb agreement | a singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb | Nobody **has** enough time. |
| noncount noun | a noun with no plural form | advice, homework, information |
| noun-pronoun agreement | a pronoun matches its noun in number and gender | my friends forced me to take **them** (those rides) |
| word form | the form of a word for each part of speech: noun, verb, adjective, adverb | kind, kindly |

## Rules and explanations

The sentences are not connected; they show typical mistakes in student writing. Notice which ones you also make. The best way to improve is to write and then edit your own writing.

### What to check (and where you studied it)
| Area | Check for | Wrong → right |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence structure and punctuation (Lessons 13, 14, 18) | fragments, run-ons, comma splices; a comma after a dependent clause at the start; a comma before "and, but, so" joining two sentences; "; however," between sentences | Whenever, a storm comes, I remember ... → Whenever a storm comes, I remember ... |
| Verbs (Lessons 5, 17, 24, 26, 29, 34, 37) | subject-verb agreement; the right tense; "be" before adjectives such as "afraid, late, bored"; modal + base verb | Everyone should has a textbook. → ... should have ... |
| Nouns (Lesson 39) | plural -s; no -s on noncount nouns; a determiner before a singular count noun; "one of" + plural noun | I waited ten minute. → ... ten minutes. |
| Pronouns (Lesson 40) | agreement; no extra pronoun after the subject; "my cousins and I"; clear reference; it's / its | I and my cousins played. → My cousins and I played. |
| Word forms (Lesson 41) | the right part of speech; -ed (feeling) / -ing (cause); -er / more, the -est / the most | He treated us kind. → ... kindly. |
| Word order (Lesson 42) | adjectives before the noun and in order; frequency words; embedded questions (question word + subject + verb) | Our family every year goes ... → Every year, our family goes ... |

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic often joins many sentences with "and" or commas. In English, a comma alone cannot join two sentences: use a period, a semicolon, or a connecting word such as "because", "so" or "but".
> - Arabic present-tense sentences can have no verb "be". English needs it: "People like cats because they **are** quieter than dogs."

## Writing task
Your final task brings together the paragraph and essay skills of Stage 2.
1. Choose an enumerative or narrative paragraph that you wrote in this course, or an essay topic from Lesson 43.
2. Expand it into a five-paragraph essay of 400 to 600 words: a title; an introductory paragraph with a hook and a thesis statement; three supporting paragraphs, each with a topic sentence, details and a transition; and a concluding paragraph.
3. Edit the essay in six rounds, one area at a time (see "What to check" above).
4. Keep an error log: the error, its type and the correction.

Self-editing checklist:
- The thesis statement ends the introduction; each supporting paragraph starts with a topic sentence.
- Transitions connect the supporting paragraphs; the conclusion has no new topic.
- No fragments, run-ons or comma splices.
- Verbs agree with their subjects; tenses are correct and consistent.
- Nouns and pronouns: plurals, noncount nouns, determiners, agreement.
- Word forms and word order: parts of speech, -ed / -ing, comparisons, adjectives, frequency words, embedded questions.

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
