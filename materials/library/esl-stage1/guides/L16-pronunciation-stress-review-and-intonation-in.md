# Lesson 16: Pronunciation: Stress Review and Intonation in Statements and Questions

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 2.3: Review- Counting Syllables and Identifying Stress; 2.4: Intonation- Statements and Questions.*

## In this lesson
First you review syllables and word stress with 18 words from Andy's interview (Lesson 15). Then you learn how your voice goes up or down at the end of statements, wh- questions and yes/no questions. At the end you record yourself asking 15 interview questions.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Count the syllables in words from this unit and divide the words into syllables.
- Find and say the stressed syllable in each word.
- Use falling intonation at the end of statements and wh- questions.
- Use rising intonation at the end of yes/no questions.
- Record interview questions with the correct intonation.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| syllable | a part of a word with one vowel sound; one "beat" | wa·ter has 2 syllables |
| word stress | the syllable that is longer, louder and higher than the others | com·PU·ter |
| intonation | the music of your voice: it goes up or down | Where did you grow up? (↘) |
| rise (↗) | the voice goes up at the end | Are you a vegan? (↗) |
| fall (↘) | the voice goes down at the end | What's your job? (↘) |
| statement | a sentence that gives information | He drives a pickup truck. (↘) |
| wh- question | a question with what, where, when, why, who or how; it asks for new information | Why do you like your job? (↘) |
| yes/no question | a question you can answer with "yes" or "no" | Do you have a car? (↗) |
| sus·TAIN·a·ble | can continue without being used up | Wind energy is sustainable. |
| en·vi·ron·MEN·tal | about the natural world (air, water, land) | the environmental impact of airplanes |

## Rules and explanations

### Syllables (review)
- A syllable is one beat of a word. Each syllable has one vowel sound. Tap the table once for each syllable.
- Count vowel **sounds**, not vowel letters. "pollutes" has three vowel letters (o, u, e) but only two vowel sounds: pol·LUTES.
- The book's example: computer = 3 syllables = com·pu·ter.

### Word stress (review)
- In a word with two or more syllables, one syllable has the main stress. Say it longer, louder and a little higher: com·PU·ter.
- The other syllables are short and quiet.
- Stress can change the word. In this unit, "produce" is a noun (fresh fruit and vegetables): PRO·duce. The verb "to produce" (to make) is pro·DUCE.

### Intonation: the book's three rules
1. **Statements:** the voice usually **falls** at the end. He drives a pickup truck. (↘)
2. **Wh- questions** that ask for new information: the voice usually **falls** at the end. What's your name? (↘)
3. **Yes/no questions:** the voice usually **rises** at the end. Do you have any pets? (↗)

### How to know which question it is
- A wh- question starts with a question word: what, where, when, why, who, how.
- A yes/no question starts with a helping verb: do, does, did, are, is, was, have, can...
- In exercise 3, each one-word question is the last word of a longer interview question. Think of the full question. "Car?" is the end of "Do you have a car?", a yes/no question, so it rises (↗). "Job?" is the end of "What's your job?", a wh- question, so it falls (↘). One-word answers ("yes.", "farmer.") are statements, so they fall.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no /p/ sound. Say /p/ as in "pen", not /b/ as in "bag", in "repair", "podcasts", "pollutes", "impact", "production" and "pets".
> - In Standard Arabic a syllable does not begin with two consonants, so learners sometimes add a vowel. Keep consonants together: "pr" in pro·DUC·tion, "str" in in·DUS·tri·al.

## Speak with your tutor
- "Say a word from Andy's interview. I will tell you how many syllables it has and which syllable is stressed. Then tell me if I am right."
- "I will ask you 10 questions to get to know you. For each one, tell me: is it a wh- question or a yes/no question, and should my voice rise or fall at the end?"
- "You are a new neighbour. Interview me with 5 wh- questions and 5 yes/no questions, one at a time. Then I will interview you with my own questions. Correct my question grammar."
- "Let's play a guessing game. You think of a job. I can ask only yes/no questions, like 'Do you work outdoors?', to guess the job."
- "Give me 5 yes/no questions. I will change each one into a wh- question about the same topic, for example 'Do you have a car?' to 'What kind of car do you have?'"
