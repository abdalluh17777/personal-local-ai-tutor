# Lesson 05: Project: A Virtual City Tour

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 1: People and Places*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 1.8: Unit Project- City Virtual Tour.*

## In this lesson
You will make a short video tour of a city you know well, like the video "25 Things to do in Berlin". You will plan, write and memorize a 2 to 4 minute presentation about three interesting places in the city. You will use the vocabulary and the pronunciation (stress, vowels, consonants and linking) from this unit.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Plan a short presentation about three places in a city you know well.
- Use phrases for describing places, such as "dates back to" and "head over to".
- Write a draft with 2-3 sentences about each place.
- Give a memorized 2-4 minute presentation on video, with good volume and eye contact.
- Check your own video with the book's rubric.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| one of the largest | among the biggest | Balboa Park is one of the largest parks in San Diego. |
| dates back to | was made or started at a time in the past | Old Town, San Diego dates back to the 18th century. |
| You can't come to ... and not ... | you must do this when you visit; don't miss it | You can't come to San Diego and not visit the beaches. |
| head over to | an informal way to say "go" | You can also head over to Belmont Park. |
| This ... actually used to be ... | in the past, this place was something different | These cafes actually used to be old warehouses. |
| Every ... people flock to ... | many people go to a place at the same time, again and again | Every weekend people flock to the beach. |
| born and raised | born and grew up in a place | I was born and raised in San Diego. |
| tourist destination | a place many visitors travel to | San Diego is a well-known tourist destination. |
| site | a place where something important is or happened | It's the site of the first Spanish settlement in California. |
| eye contact | looking at people (in a video: at the camera) when you speak | Look directly at the camera. |
| volume | how loud your voice is | Speak loudly enough to be heard comfortably. |

## Rules and explanations

### The task (for self-study)
In the book, groups of 3 make one video, and each student describes one place. You will do the whole video alone:
- Choose a city (or another interesting location) that you know well.
- Choose 3 places or sites in that city. For each place, say its name, why it is important or interesting to you, and what a tourist can do there.
- Use at least 3 phrases or vocabulary words from the "25 Things to do in Berlin" video.
- Show at least one picture for each place (3 pictures minimum). You can use slides or photos, but they must be in your video.
- Your presentation must be 2 to 4 minutes long. Memorize it and record it with your phone.

### Why are we doing this?
- You practice speech skills: volume, eye contact, organization, and presenting information about a place you know well.
- You review the pronunciation from this unit: vowel and consonant sounds, syllable stress, and linking.

### How the example video is organized
The example "Top Three Things to do in San Diego" is a good model (the transcript is in your printed lesson):
- **Introduction:** the speaker's connection to the city and why it is interesting: *I was born and raised in San Diego, and it's also a well-known tourist destination.* Then: *Here's my list of the 3 best things to do in San Diego.*
- **Three places:** for each one, a few sentences: what it is, a fact, and what you can do there (*You can go on a tour..., taste excellent Mexican food, and listen to live music.*).

### How your video is graded (rubric, 50 points)
There are 5 parts. Each part gets 10 (excellent), 7 (good), 5 (needs improvement) or 0 points. With 5 or 0 points in a part, the book asks you to make the video again.
1. **Content:** the name of the place and 3 interesting sites; why each place is important or interesting; 3 phrases from the "25 Things..." video; 2-4 minutes long. (All four = 10 points.)
2. **Organization and clarity:** the speech is well organized, interesting and clear.
3. **Delivery:** you speak loudly enough, look directly at the camera, and speak confidently with little help from notes.
4. **Pronunciation:** syllable stress, vowel and consonant sounds, and linking are clear and easy to understand.
5. **Visuals:** at least 3 pictures, one for each place.

## Practice

**1. Study the example.** Watch "Top Three Things to do in San Diego" (link below) and read the transcript in your printed lesson. Answer:
a) What are the three places?
b) Find the sentences with these phrases: one of the largest; dates back to; You can't come to ... and not ...; head over to.

**2. Brainstorm and plan.** Think of a city (or other interesting location) you know well. Choose 3 places in that city. Brainstorm things a tourist can do in each place. Write notes:

**3. Functional language.** Write one sentence about your city with each phrase. Book example: *The Empire State Building is one of the largest buildings in New York City.*
- one of the largest
- dates back to
- You can't come to ... and not ...
- head over to
- This ... (place) actually used to be ...
- Every ... (day, week, month) people flock to ...

**4. Write your draft.** See the Writing task below.

**5. Practice and memorize.** Read your draft aloud many times. Mark the stressed syllable in long words and mark the links (for example, one_of, head_over). Practice until you can say it without notes. Time yourself: 2 to 4 minutes.

**6. Record your video.** Use your phone. Look directly at the camera and speak loudly enough. Show your 3 pictures. Then watch your video and score it with the rubric (____ / 50). If any part gets 5 or 0 points, practice and record again.

**7. After the project.** In the book, classmates watch each other's videos and post comments. Instead: present your tour to your tutor in Live conversation and answer the tutor's questions. Then write one thoughtful comment or question about the example San Diego video.

## Speak with your tutor
- "I am planning a short video tour of my city. Ask me questions about three places in my city, one at a time."
- "You are a tourist. Ask me what to do in my city. I will answer with 'head over to', 'dates back to' and 'You can't come to ... and not ...'."
- "Listen to my presentation. Time it, and tell me which phrases from the Berlin video I used."
- "After my presentation, ask me two questions like a viewer would. Then tell me one thing I did well and one thing to improve."
- "Listen to my presentation again and tell me which words were hard to understand."
