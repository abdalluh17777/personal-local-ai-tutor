# Lesson 32: Pronunciation: Reduced Words in Sentences; -Ed Endings

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 4: Education and Creativity*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 4.3: Review - Reduced Words in Sentences; 4.4: -Ed Endings.*

## In this lesson
First you review reduced words: small structure words like "and", "a" and "to" that we say quickly. Then you learn the three ways to say the -ed ending of regular past tense verbs, /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/, and how to link it to the next word. You practice with the story of Gillian Lynne.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Hear and write the small structure words in a fast sentence.
- Choose the correct -ed sound (/t/, /d/ or /ɪd/) from the last sound of the verb.
- Feel the difference between voiced and unvoiced sounds.
- Link -ed endings to the next word.
- Describe the important events of a life in the past tense.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| structure word (reduced word) | a small grammar word, like and, a, an, in, to; we say it quickly and quietly | Gillian and I had lunch one day. |
| final sound | the last sound you say in a word (not the last letter) | "judge" ends in the sound /ʤ/ |
| voiced sound | a sound that makes your throat vibrate | /n/ as in "explain" |
| unvoiced (voiceless) sound | a sound with no vibration in your throat | /k/ as in "talk" |
| /t/ ending | -ed after an unvoiced sound | talked, watched, helped |
| /d/ ending | -ed after a voiced sound | explained, bothered, turned |
| /ɪd/ ending (also written /əd/) | -ed after /t/ or /d/; it adds a syllable | needed, started, graduated |
| linking | joining the end of a word to the next word | I lived in Thailand. (live-din) |
| timeline | a line that shows the important events of a life in order | Brittany's timeline |

## Rules and explanations

### Reduced words (review)
- In a sentence, structure words such as **and, a, an, in, to** are usually not stressed. We say them quickly and quietly, so they are hard to hear.
- For example, **to** often sounds like /tə/, and **and** like /ən/ or /n/ (see Lesson 28).

### The three -ed sounds
The -ed sound depends on the **last sound** of the base verb, not the last letter.

| Last sound of the verb | Say -ed as | Book examples |
|---|---|---|
| /t/ or /d/ (need, start) | /ɪd/, an extra syllable | needed, started, waited |
| unvoiced: /f/, /k/, /p/, /s/, /ʧ/ as in "watch", /ʃ/ as in "push", /θ/ as in "think" | /t/ | talked, looked, pushed, asked, watched, helped, dressed |
| voiced: /b/, /g/, /ʤ/ as in "judge", /l/, /m/, /n/, /ŋ/ as in "sing", /r/, /ð/ as in "breathe", /v/, /z/, and all vowels | /d/ | explained, bothered, sobbed, roamed, believed, judged, filled, enjoyed |

- Only /ɪd/ adds a syllable. "Talked" has one syllable; "need·ed" has two.
- Look at the sound, not the spelling: "believe" ends in the letter e but the sound /v/, so "believed" ends in /d/. "Enjoy" ends in a vowel sound, so "enjoyed" ends in /d/.

### Voiced or unvoiced?
- Put your hand on your throat. A vibration at the end of the word = voiced. No vibration = unvoiced. "Unvoiced" and "voiceless" mean the same thing.
- Careful: in Video Questions 1 the book lists /p/ as voiced and /b/ as unvoiced. This is a mistake: /p/ is unvoiced and /b/ is voiced, as the book's own table shows. Exercise 4 below is corrected.

### Linking -ed endings
- If the next word begins with a **vowel**, the -ed is clear. Link it: I lived in Thailand. (sounds like live-din)
- If the next word begins with **/t/ or /d/**, the -ed is difficult to hear: Tommy looked tired. (sounds like look-tired)

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Many learners say every -ed as an extra syllable ("talk-ed"). Add a syllable only after /t/ and /d/.
> - Arabic has no /p/ sound, so "helped" can sound like "helbed". Keep /p/ unvoiced (no vibration), then add a /t/ sound.

**2.** (book) a /ɪd/, b /t/, c /d/.

**3.** /t/: danced, cooked, washed. /d/: vacuumed, married, joined, lived, belonged. /ɪd/: invented, created, ended. (Last sounds: dance /s/, cook /k/, wash /ʃ/; vacuum /m/, marry a vowel, join /n/, live /v/, belong /ŋ/; invent and create /t/; end /d/.)

**4.** Video Questions 1: 1 /t/, 2 /d/, 3 /ɪd/, 4 feel, 5 don't feel, 6 true, 7 voiced (a vowel sound), 8 unvoiced (/k/), 9 unvoiced (/p/), 10 voiced (/z/).

**5.** (book answer sheet) 1 b, 2 a, 3 a, 4 b, 5 b, 6 a, 7 b, 8 a, 9 b, 10 a. Remember: the -ed is easy to hear before a vowel (bothered other, turned in, studied every, turned on, lived in) and hard to hear before /t/ (talked to, started to, worked too, needed to, looked tired).

**6.** talked to: talk-to (hard to hear). explained /d/. bothered other: bother-dother. turned in: turn-din. turned on: turn-don. started to: the ending is hard to hear.
