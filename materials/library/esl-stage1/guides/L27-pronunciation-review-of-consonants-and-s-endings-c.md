# Lesson 27: Pronunciation: Review of Consonants and -S Endings; Content Words

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 3: Zero Waste*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 3.3 Review - Voiced and Unvoiced Consonants and -S Endings; 3.4 Content Words.*

## In this lesson
First you will review two rules from Unit 2 with the new zero waste words: voiced and unvoiced final sounds, and -s endings. Then you will learn the difference between content words and function words. You will practise the rhythm of English with two nursery rhymes and a paragraph from Lauren's video.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Decide if the final sound of a word is voiced or unvoiced.
- Say -s endings correctly as /s/ or /z/.
- Find the content words and the function words in a sentence.
- Stress content words and say function words quickly and softly.
- Read a nursery rhyme and a short paragraph with English rhythm.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| final sound | the last sound you say (not the last letter) | "waste" ends with /t/; the e is silent. |
| voiced | your throat vibrates (buzzes) when you say the sound | the /m/ at the end of "foam" |
| unvoiced | no vibration; only air | the /k/ at the end of "chopstick" |
| -s ending | the s on plural nouns and on verbs after he, she, it | recipes, makes |
| content word | a word that gives the most important information; usually stressed | bank in "Dad's at the bank." |
| function word | a small word that makes the sentence grammatically correct; usually not stressed | at, the in "Dad's at the bank." |
| stress | saying a syllable or word stronger, longer and higher in pitch | TWIN·kle |
| rhythm | the beat of speech: strong and weak words | Twinkle, twinkle, little star |
| nursery rhyme | a short traditional poem or song for children | The incy wincy spider |
| negative auxiliary | a helping verb with "not"; it is a content word | can't, isn't, don't |

## Rules and explanations

### Voiced and unvoiced final sounds (review)
- Put your hand on your throat. If you feel a vibration on the final sound, it is **voiced**. If you feel no vibration, it is **unvoiced**.
- Listen to the final **sound**, not the final letter. "Waste" ends with the sound /t/. "Recipe" ends with a vowel sound.
- Vowel sounds are always voiced.

### -S endings (review)
| Say | When | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| /s/ | after an unvoiced final sound such as /f/, /k/, /p/, /t/, /θ/ (as in "think") | look - looks, help - helps, wait - waits |
| /z/ | after a voiced final sound such as /b/, /d/, /g/, /l/, /m/, /n/, /r/, /v/, /ð/ (as in "mother"), and after vowels | roam - roams, need - needs, enjoy - enjoys |
| /əz/ (extra syllable) | after /s/, /z/, /ʃ/ (as in "push"), /ʧ/ (as in "watch"), /ʤ/ (as in "judge") | push - pushes, watch - watches, judge - judges |

### Content words and function words
- **Content words** give the most important information. They are usually **stressed** and **higher** in pitch.
- **Function words** make your sentences grammatically correct. They are usually **not stressed** and **lower** in pitch.

| Content words | Examples | Function words | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| nouns | friend, Bob, work | articles | a, an, the |
| verbs | eat, play, run | prepositions | of, to, for, with |
| adjectives | beautiful, good, angry | pronouns | he, she, I, you |
| adverbs | always, cheerfully, easily | auxiliaries | can, have, is |
| interjections | Wow! Oops! | conjunctions | but, or, so |
| wh- words | why, where, what | | |
| negative auxiliaries | can't, isn't, don't | | |

### Rhythm
- English has a beat. The stressed syllables of content words come on the beat. The function words between them are short and weak.
- It is easiest to hear this rhythm in songs and nursery rhymes. Clap on the beat.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no /v/ sound, so /v/ can become /f/. "Alternative" ends with voiced /v/, so "alternatives" ends with /z/, not /s/.
> - Many Arabic speakers say small words like "to", "the" and "of" with a full, clear vowel. In English, function words are usually weak and quick.

## Answer key
**1.** (book) Unvoiced: toothpaste, chopstick, coconut, fluctuate, waste. Voiced: recipe, alternative, cinnamon, combine, tablespoon, liquid, foam. (The unvoiced words end with /t/ or /k/. "Recipe" ends with a vowel sound, so it is voiced.)

**2.** (book) /s/: makes, chopsticks, takes, benefits, coconuts, fluctuates, wastes, forks, ingredients. /z/: recipes, alternatives, combines, tablespoons, liquids, oils, foams, stirs. (Every /s/ word has /k/ or /t/ before the s.)

**3.** a. Nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs (check this with the video). b. 1 information, 2 grammar, 3 stressed, 4 higher, 5 not stressed, 6 lower. c. 1 Dad, bank; 2 went, store; 3 here, soon; 4 took, red, car; 5 Sometimes, hard, walk, single, woman's, shoes; 6 Bob, finally, received, wedding, gift, Michael. ("Be" in sentence 3 is usually not stressed, like "is".)

**4.** (example answer) nouns: book; verbs: write; adjectives: happy; adverbs: quickly; interjections: Ouch!; wh- words: when; negative auxiliaries: won't. Articles: none (a, an and the are the only articles); prepositions: in; pronouns: they; auxiliaries: will; conjunctions: and.

**6.** a. Twinkle: four beats in each line. Line 1: TWIN-, TWIN-, LIT-, STAR. Line 2: HOW, WON-, WHAT, ARE. Line 3: UP, -BOVE, WORLD, HIGH. Line 4: LIKE, DIA-, IN, SKY. The beats fall on the content words. The song also puts a beat on a few small words (are, up, above, like, in) to keep the rhythm. Incy wincy: the content words are emphasized (incy, wincy, spider, climbed, water, spout, down, came, rain, washed, out, sun, dried, again). b. Weak words: I, you, a, the, so, and. c. Only one syllable: TWIN·kle, LIT·tle, WON·der, DIA·mond, SPI·der, WA·ter, a·GAIN, IN·cy, WIN·cy (check). One-syllable content words (star, world, sky, rain, sun, spout) are stressed as a whole word.

**7.** Content words: 1 Toothpaste, usually, foams up, mouth, brush, teeth. 2 upcycle, means, find, new, way, use, instead, throwing, trash. 3 lead, zero, waste, lifestyle, means, don't, make, trash. 4 Lauren, said, consistency, toothpaste, fluctuate, lot, cold, toothpaste, gets, hard, hot, toothpaste, become, liquid. 5 Peppermint, cinnamon, two, examples, flavors, put, toothpaste. 6 use, baking soda, cookie, cake, recipes, also, use, toothpaste, recipe. ("When" and "if" join two parts of a sentence here, so they are usually not stressed. Numbers like "two" give information, so they are stressed.)

**8.** a. (example answer) Hey, everybody, Lauren, Trash, Tossers, today, show, make, Zero Waste Toothpaste / absolutely, love, recipe / great, alternative, buying, toothpaste, tubes, difficult, impossible, recycle / also, works, extremely, well / using, years, still, have, almost, teeth. b. (example answer) Listen and decide. A speaker can give extra stress to a word to show strong feeling, and may not stress every content word when speaking fast.
