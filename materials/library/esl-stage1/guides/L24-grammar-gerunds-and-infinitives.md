# Lesson 24: Grammar: Gerunds and Infinitives

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Getting to Know You - An Intermediate Integrated-Skills Module for English Learners by Sara Ferguson, Grossmont College (CC BY-NC-SA): Grammar: Using Gerunds and Infinitives for Sentence Variety.*

## In this lesson
You will learn what gerunds (reading) and infinitives (to read) are, and how they work like nouns in a sentence. You will learn which verbs take a gerund, which take an infinitive, and which can take both. Then you will use them to make your writing more varied and more formal.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Form gerunds (verb + -ing) and infinitives (to + verb).
- Tell the difference between a gerund and an -ing verb ("Hiking is fun" / "They are hiking").
- Use a gerund as a subject, as the object of a verb, and after a preposition.
- Choose a gerund or an infinitive after common verbs (enjoy eating, promise to study).
- Write a short paragraph about a person with gerunds and infinitives.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| gerund | verb + -ing that works like a noun | I like reading. |
| infinitive | to + the base form of a verb; it works like a noun | I like to read. |
| base form | the simple form of a verb, with no ending | talk, read, say |
| subject | the person or thing that does the action; usually at the start of the sentence | Being grateful can improve our sense of well-being. |
| object of a verb | the word or words after the verb that receive the action | She enjoys eating chocolate. |
| preposition | a small word like on, in, by, about, to | Another method to boost happiness is by using our strengths. |
| linking verb | a verb like be, seem or appear; it links the subject to an adjective | It's important to notice the good things in life. |
| sentence variety | using different kinds of sentences | Gerunds and infinitives add sentence variety. |
| formal | serious and careful, for school and work | Gerunds and infinitives make your writing more formal and academic. |
| boost | to make bigger or stronger | In order to boost happiness, there are five methods people can use. |
| optimistic | thinking that good things will happen | Be optimistic. |
| look forward to | to feel happy about something in the future | What are you looking forward to doing tomorrow? |

## Rules and explanations
### Gerunds
- Form: base form + -ing: talking, reading, saying.
- A gerund is **not** the verb of the sentence. It works like a noun.
- An **-ing verb** is part of a continuous tense: it follows the subject and comes after am, is or are. People feel happier when they are using their natural abilities. ("are using" = verb)
- A **gerund** works like a noun. Another method to boost happiness is by using our strengths. ("using" = gerund, after the preposition "by")
- Like a noun, a gerund can be:
  - the **subject**: Gerund + verb + object. Being grateful can improve our sense of well-being.
  - the **object of a verb**: Subject + verb + gerund. She enjoys eating chocolate.
  - the **object of a preposition**: Subject + verb + preposition + gerund. ... is by using our strengths.
  - at the start, after a preposition: Preposition + gerund + subject + verb.

### Infinitives
- Form: to + base form: to talk, to read, to say.
- An infinitive is **not** the verb of the sentence. It works like a noun.
- Two common patterns:
  - Subject + verb + infinitive: We should always try to keep a positive attitude.
  - It + linking verb + adjective + infinitive: It's important to notice the good things in life.

### Which verbs take which form?
Some verbs take a gerund, some take an infinitive, and some can take both.
- I like reading. / I like to read. (both are correct)
- I promise to study. (NOT: I promise studying.)
- She enjoys eating chocolate. (NOT: She enjoys to eat chocolate.)

| Followed by a gerund | Followed by an infinitive | Either, with little change in meaning | Either, with a different meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| dislike, appreciate, enjoy, (not) face, look forward to, consider, imagine, recall, miss, recommend, admit (to), deny, describe, mention, prohibit, report, suggest, postpone, delay, put off, risk, avoid, mean (= involve), practice, go | would like, expect, hope, need, agree, appear / seem, ask, get (be allowed), offer, refuse, plan, wait, promise, intend, tend, afford | like, love, cannot bear / cannot stand, prefer, hate, continue, begin / start | remember, forget, try, stop, regret |

- The last group (remember, forget, try, stop, regret) **changes meaning** with a gerund or an infinitive. Ask your tutor to explain (see "Speak with your tutor").

### Why use gerunds and infinitives?
They make your writing better because they:
- add sentence variety
- help you develop your ideas
- make your writing more formal and academic

## Practice
**1. Gerund or verb?** Is "hiking" the verb in each sentence? How do you know?
a) Hiking is one of his favorite activities.
b) They are hiking in the mountains today.

**2. What does the gerund do?** Is the gerund the subject of the verb, the object of the verb, or the object of a preposition?
a) Mentoring other employees is his favorite part of the job.
b) Her decision to major in medicine was based on volunteering at the hospital.
c) They love inspiring other students at the college.

**3. Find the verb and the infinitive.**
a) After he finishes college, he wants to work as a geologist.
b) It's important to listen during an interview.

**4. Compare two paragraphs.** Read Paragraph 1 and Paragraph 2 (about five ways to be happy) in your lesson material. Then answer:
a) What verb forms does the first paragraph use? What person (you, he/she/it, we/they) does each paragraph use?
b) Which paragraph is more formal and sophisticated? Why?
c) The second paragraph is more developed. What did the author do to develop the ideas more completely?
d) Find all the gerunds and infinitives in the second paragraph.

**5. About you.** Answer each question in a full sentence. Use the table to choose a gerund or an infinitive.
1. What do you like to do in your free time?
2. Describe your favorite memory from your childhood. What do you remember doing?
3. What activity have you tried and failed to do?
4. What movies or TV shows do you recommend seeing?
5. What do you hope to do next year? In five years?
6. What do you dislike most about your everyday routine? Why?
7. What are you looking forward to doing tomorrow?

**6. About another person.** Ask your tutor to play a friend or family member, and ask the seven questions in exercise 5. Take notes. Then write the paragraph in "Writing task".
