# Lesson 21: Project Part 1: Interview; Vocabulary Review

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): Unit Project Part 1- Interview; and from Getting to Know You - An Intermediate Integrated-Skills Module for English Learners by Sara Ferguson, Grossmont College (CC BY-NC-SA): Vocabulary Review: Getting to Know You.*

## In this lesson
You will plan, practice and record an interview in English. Your AI tutor plays a person with a job and a life story, and you ask the questions. You will also review the unit's words about college, careers and education.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Write at least 5 yes/no questions and 5 wh- questions about a person's job, hobbies, family and values.
- Ask questions with the correct intonation: rising for yes/no questions, falling for wh- questions.
- Mark rising and falling intonation on a written list of questions.
- Use words about college and careers, such as major, semester, scholarship and mentor.
- Understand the idioms "not by a long shot", "don't mess with", "lead through example" and "on board".

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| interview (n, v) | a meeting where one person asks another person questions | Make a video of yourself interviewing someone you know. |
| interviewee | the person who answers the questions | The student looks at the interviewee often. |
| yes/no question | a question with the answer "yes" or "no"; it starts with do, are, is, can... | Do you have a car? |
| wh- question | a question that starts with what, where, when, why, who or how | Where did you grow up? |
| values | the things that are most important to a person | Ask about the person's values. |
| intern (n, v) | a person who works for little or no pay to learn a job; to work in this way | When he was in college, he interned at an architectural company. |
| (not) by a long shot | (usually negative) not at all; a strong "no" | Did you pass the final exam? Not by a long shot. |
| don't mess with | (informal) don't make this person angry; don't cause trouble with this thing | Don't mess with the dog next door. It might bite you. |
| lead through example | act in a way that shows others how to act | It's important that parents lead through example. |
| on board | on a team; working here or with us | Now that Mike is on board at this company, our work will be much easier. |
| multi- / un-, in-, im-, non- | prefixes: multi- means "many"; un-, in-, im- and non- mean "not" | multilingual, undeclared, impossible, non-verbal |

## Rules and explanations
### The interview project
- Write at least **5 yes/no questions** and **5 wh- questions**. You can use questions from this unit.
- Ask about the person's **job**, **hobbies**, **family** and **values** (what is most important to the person).
- Ask at least **2 questions that are not** in the example video (Andy's interview).
- Put your questions in order. Practice them until you can ask them without reading.
- After the interview, write a list of at least 10 questions you asked. Mark each one "rise" (↗) or "fall" (↘).

### Intonation in questions
- **Yes/no questions** rise at the end: Do you eat meat? (↗)
- **Wh- questions** fall at the end: Why are you vegan? (↘)

### What makes a good interview (from the book's checklist)
- Speak loudly and confidently. Look at the interviewee often. Use your notes very little.
- Pronounce vowels and consonants clearly, link consonants to vowels, and use the correct intonation for every question.

### Vocabulary points
- Some words are **both nouns and verbs** with the same meaning: intern, volunteer, design, mentor, challenge. As verbs, they need the correct tense and -s ending (he interned).
- Some verbs **need an object** (somebody or something after them): inspire (somebody), motivate (somebody), pursue (somebody or something), develop (something).

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - In Arabic, a yes/no question can keep the word order of a statement. A normal English question puts a helping verb first: "Do you have a car?" (not "You have a car?"), "Are you a vegan?"
> - Wh- questions need the helping verb too: "Where do you live?" (not "Where you live?")

## Practice
**1. Plan your questions.** Write at least 5 yes/no and 5 wh- questions to ask a person about their life (job, hobbies, family, values). At least 2 must not be in the example video. Put them in order and mark each one ↗ or ↘.

**2. Practice aloud.** Record yourself reading your questions and listen. Does your voice go up at the end of yes/no questions and down at the end of wh- questions?

**3. The interview.** In Live conversation, ask your tutor to play a person with a job and a life story (see "Speak with your tutor"). Ask your questions one at a time and listen. Ask a new question if an answer is interesting. Record yourself on your phone (video if you can). Take notes: name, where the person is from, job, daily tasks, free time, family, values. **Keep your notes for Lesson 25.**

**4. Check your interview.** Listen to your recording. Did you ask about the job, hobbies, family and values? Did you ask 5 yes/no and 5 wh- questions? Did you speak clearly, without reading every question? Was your intonation correct? Write your list of 10 questions with ↗ or ↘. Then write one thing you did well and one thing to do better.

**5. College and university.**
a) Write the four years of high school or college: Freshman, ______, ______, ______.
b) Write four degrees: ______, ______, ______, Doctoral degree.
c) Match the words (major, graduation, dormitory, specialize, semester, scholarship) with the examples:
1. A small room or apartment where students sleep
2. Spring, Summer, or Fall in college
3. Free money for students to attend school
4. Child development, biology, psychology, engineering
5. To study a specific topic deeply and in detail
6. A special celebration of the completion of a course of study

d) Fill in the blanks with: program, graduate, undergraduate, declare a major.
1. A counselor can help you ______.
2. Students who are studying for an associate's degree or bachelor's degree are called ______ students.
3. Students who are studying for a degree after their bachelor's are called ______ students.
4. A ______ is a plan of action to achieve a goal.

**6. Nouns and verbs.** Use volunteer, design, mentor or challenge. Check the tense.
1. When Jennifer gets her degree, she wants to ______ buildings and parks.
2. The ______ for the Empire State Building is a beautiful art deco style.
3. When I was a student, I ______ in the food pantry. I worked for free because I wanted to help others.
4. Razia is a ______ at the symphony. She hands out programs and sells tickets, but doesn't receive any pay.
5. It is a ______ to learn English. It takes a lot of time, energy, and hard work!
6. Our instructor always ______ us to learn more vocabulary. After we learn 5 words, she says, "Try one more!"
7. More experienced students ______ new students by showing them the campus.
8. When you start a new job, it's so valuable to have a ______.

**7. Verbs with an object.** Use inspire, motivate, pursue, develop.
a) Two words mean "to cause someone to want to do something": ______ and ______. One word means "to follow": ______. One word means "to build something or cause it to grow": ______.
b) Fill in the blanks:
1. Her eighth grade teacher ______ her to study physics because he made it so interesting. (two answers)
2. After college, he decided to ______ a career in photography.
3. In geography class, she ______ an interest in geomatics.

**8. My dream career.** Use adventure, travel, goal, employer, skills, in high demand.
1. I prefer a career where my ______ is a small business, not a large organization.
2. I want my job to be an ______.
3. I also like to ______, whether it's by plane or by car.
4. My ______ is to earn a degree from a good university in a field that is ______.
5. I will learn all of the ______ that I need to pursue a satisfying career.

**9. Match the words with the definitions.** Words: resource, recipient, journey, public service, professional, personal, opportunity, valuable, task.
1. A person who receives something
2. Chance; suitable time
3. A job that helps everyone in the community; sometimes a job in the government
4. The act of traveling
5. A small job
6. A place or thing that is useful
7. Private; affecting just one person
8. Affecting the career or job
9. Very helpful or important, or worth a lot of money

## Answer key
**1-2.** (example answer)
- Yes/no: Do you like your job? (↗) Are you married? (↗) Do you have any children? (↗) Do you play any sports? (↗) Is your family important to you? (↗)
- Wh-: What do you do? (↘) Where do you work? (↘) What do you do in your free time? (↘) How many brothers and sisters do you have? (↘) What is most important to you in life? (↘)

**3-4.** Answers depend on your interview.

**5.** a) Sophomore, Junior, Senior. b) Associate's degree, Bachelor's degree, Master's degree. c) 1 dormitory, 2 semester, 3 scholarship, 4 major, 5 specialize, 6 graduation. d) 1 declare a major, 2 undergraduate, 3 graduate, 4 program.

**6.** 1 design, 2 design, 3 volunteered, 4 volunteer, 5 challenge, 6 challenges, 7 mentor, 8 mentor.

**7.** a) inspire and motivate; pursue; develop. b) 1 inspired / motivated, 2 pursue, 3 developed.

**8.** 1 employer, 2 adventure, 3 travel, 4 goal, in high demand, 5 skills.

**9.** 1 recipient, 2 opportunity, 3 public service, 4 journey, 5 task, 6 resource, 7 personal, 8 professional, 9 valuable.

## Speak with your tutor
- "Please play a person with a job and an interesting life. Choose your name, job, family, hobbies and values. I will interview you. Answer in two to four sentences, and do not ask me questions."
- "After my interview, tell me which questions had a grammar mistake or the wrong intonation. Then give me three follow-up questions I could ask."
- "Now interview me about my job, hobbies, family and values. Ask one question at a time and correct my answers."
- "Ask me about my education. Use the words major, semester, degree, scholarship and mentor in your questions."
- "Give me one sentence with a blank for each idiom: by a long shot, don't mess with, lead through example, on board. I will fill in the blanks."
