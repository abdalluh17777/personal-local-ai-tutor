# Lesson 23: Pronunciation: -S Endings

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): -S Endings.*

## In this lesson
You will learn the three ways to say the -s ending in English: /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/. You will practice -s endings on plural nouns and on verbs after he, she and it, and link the -s ending to the next word. You will also describe a dream job and read a short text about Andy, the farmer.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Choose the correct -s sound, /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/, for a word.
- Use the final sound of a word (voiced, unvoiced, or hissing and buzzing) to decide the -s sound.
- Add an extra syllable only after hissing and buzzing sounds (watch·es, judg·es).
- Link the -s ending to the next word (works a lot sounds like "work-sa lot").
- Describe what a person does in a job, with the third person -s.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -s ending | the -s or -es at the end of a plural noun, or of a verb after he, she or it | Did she break one leg or two legs? |
| third person -s | the -s on a present verb after he, she, it or a name | In her imaginary job, she helps students. |
| vocal cords | the parts in your throat that move (vibrate) when you make a voiced sound | Where are your vocal cords? |
| vibration | movement or buzzing | Do you feel vibration or no vibration? |
| lack of | not enough of something | The student's problem is lack of sleep. |
| voiced / unvoiced | with vibration / with no vibration | /b/ is voiced; /p/ is unvoiced. |
| hissing or buzzing sound | /s/, /z/, /ʃ/ as in "push", /ʧ/ as in "watch", /ʤ/ as in "judge" | dress, push, watch, judge |
| extra syllable | one more beat in the word | watch (1 syllable), watch·es (2 syllables) |
| linking | joining the end of one word to the start of the next word | Jen likes poodles. (like-spoodles) |
| dream job | the job you would most like to have | Brittany's dream job is to be a full-time ESL teacher. |

## Rules and explanations
### Why the -s ending is important
- Sometimes the meaning is not clear without it. "Jen fell off her bike and broke her leg(s)." Did she break one leg or two legs?
- Sometimes the meaning is clear, but the missing -s distracts the listener: "My teacher give two quiz every Friday." (See exercise 2.)

### Three sounds for the -s ending
The sound **before** the -s decides the -s sound. Listen to the **sound**, not the spelling.

| -s sound | Use it after... | Examples from the book |
|---|---|---|
| /s/ as in "looks" | unvoiced final sounds, such as /f/, /k/, /p/, /t/, /θ/ (th) | look - looks, ask - asks, help - helps, wait - waits |
| /z/ as in "needs" | voiced final sounds, such as /b/, /d/, /g/, /l/, /m/, /n/, /r/, /ð/ (th), /v/, and all vowel sounds | sob - sobs, roam - roams, believe - believes, need - needs, fill - fills, enjoy - enjoys |
| /ɪz/ as in "watches" (extra syllable) | hissing or buzzing sounds: /s/, /z/, /ʃ/ (sh), /ʧ/ (ch), /ʤ/ (j) | push - pushes, watch - watches, dress - dresses, judge - judges |

- The book writes this third sound as /əz/ or /ɪz/. They mean the same thing: an extra syllable that sounds like "iz".
- Only /ɪz/ adds a syllable. "Likes" ends with the letters -es, but it has one syllable and the sound /s/.

### Linking -s endings
Native speakers often join the -s ending to the first sound of the next word. This makes your speech sound more natural and helps you understand native speakers.
- **-s + vowel sound:** Andy works a lot. (sounds like "work-sa lot")
- **-s + /s/ sound:** Brittany runs so fast. (sounds like "run-so fast")
- **-s + another consonant:** Jen likes poodles. (sounds like "like-spoodles")

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Words like "asks", "helps", "wants" and "drinks" end with a group of consonants. Do not add a vowel between them ("as-kis") and do not drop the -s. Keep them one syllable: asks, helps, wants, drinks.

## Speak with your tutor
- "Play a person with a funny job, for example a professional cuddler. I will ask what you do every day, then describe your job with 'he' or 'she'. Correct my -s endings."
- "Say ten plural nouns, one at a time. I will say if the ending is /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/. Tell me if I am right."
- "Ask me 'Do you...?' questions, like 'Do you often cook at home?' Then I will tell you about my family: 'My sister often cooks at home.'"
- "Listen to me read 'Andy's Job'. Tell me which -s endings I missed."
