# Lesson 22: Pronunciation: Voiced and Unvoiced Consonants

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): Voiced and Unvoiced Consonants.*

## In this lesson
You will learn the difference between voiced and unvoiced consonant sounds. You will hear and say word pairs like "price" and "prize", where only the final sound is different. You will also learn that a vowel sounds longer before a voiced final consonant.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Feel the difference between voiced and unvoiced sounds with your hand on your throat.
- Say if the final sound of a word is voiced or unvoiced.
- Make the vowel longer before a voiced final consonant (prize) and shorter before an unvoiced one (price).
- Hear and say word pairs such as seat and seed, buck and bug, peace and peas.
- Find voiced and unvoiced final sounds in real English.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| consonant | a sound that is not a vowel | /b/, /p/, /d/, /t/, /s/, /z/ |
| vowel sound | a sound like a, e, i, o, u; vowel sounds are always voiced | play, go, die, knee |
| voiced | you feel a vibration in your throat when you say it | /v/ as in "leave" |
| unvoiced | no vibration, only air | /θ/ as in "thumb" |
| vibration | movement or buzzing that you can feel | Put your hand on your throat. Do you feel a vibration? |
| final sound | the last sound of a word (not the last letter) | The final sound of "price" is /s/, not the letter "e". |
| long / short vowel sound | here: the vowel takes more time / less time to say | The vowel in "robe" is longer than the vowel in "rope". |
| word pair | two words that are the same except for one sound | price / prize |
| buck | (informal) a dollar | He gave me a buck! |
| bug | an insect | He gave me a bug! |
| prize | something you get when you win | Did you see the prize? |
| peas | small, round, green vegetables | We need peas. |

## Rules and explanations
### Voiced and unvoiced sounds
- Put your hand on your throat and say a sound.
- **Voiced:** you feel a vibration (movement or buzzing).
- **Unvoiced:** you feel nothing. Only air comes out.
- Many consonants come in pairs. Your mouth does the same thing, but one sound is unvoiced and one is voiced. Example: /f/ as in "leaf" (unvoiced) and /v/ as in "leave" (voiced).
- Look at the **sound**, not the spelling. "Price" ends with the letter "e", but the final sound is /s/.

### Rule 1: Vowels are longer before voiced final consonants
Only the final sound is different in these pairs. Play the clip **Rules_to_Remember_1.m4a** and listen to the vowel.

| Final consonant is unvoiced (shorter vowel) | Final consonant is voiced (longer vowel) |
|---|---|
| rope | robe |
| coat | code |
| pick | pig |
| leaf | leave |
| price | prize |

- Say the vowel before a voiced consonant long and clear: "prize" sounds like "priiize".
- Say the vowel before an unvoiced consonant shorter: "price".
- This is important because many words are the same except for the final sound. If the vowel length is wrong, the listener may hear a different word.

### Rule 2: Vowel sounds are always voiced
"Play", "go", "die" and "knee" all end with a vowel sound. Put your hand on your throat: you feel a vibration. Vowel sounds are always voiced.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has /b/ but no /p/, so /p/ and /b/ are easy to mix up. Practice these pairs carefully: rope / robe, lap / lab, cap / cab.
> - Arabic has /f/ but no /v/. Make /v/ voiced (feel the vibration): leaf / leave, proof / prove.
