# Guest Lecture 2: The Psychology, Biology, and Politics of Food

*Introduction to Psychology · Yale University, Spring 2007 · Professor Kelly Brownell*
*Study guide based on the official course overview. Source: https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/guest-lecture-2 (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0).*
*Note: Open Yale Courses published no transcript for this guest lecture. This short guide is based on the official overview only.*

## Before you start
In this guest lecture, Professor Kelly Brownell talks about why people eat what they do, and what might be done to improve diet in the U.S. and around the world.

## Learning goals
After this lecture you should be able to:
- Name the main topics of the lecture.
- Explain what "economic factors" and "food marketing" mean.
- Describe the idea of changing nutrition through legislation.

## Key ideas
### 1. Why people eat what they do
The lecture covers the reasons people eat what they do, with emphasis on the economic factors that drive food choices.

### 2. Food, marketing and the food industry
It discusses the benefits and disadvantages of modern food and agriculture approaches. It also looks at how biology intersects with (connects to) food marketing practices, and at the role of the food industry.

### 3. Improving diet through law
A key area of focus is novel (new) approaches to changing nutrition through legislation and legal approaches.

## Key vocabulary
| Term | Meaning in simple English | Example from the lecture |
|---|---|---|
| nutrition | the food we take in and its effect on the body | changing nutrition through law |
| agriculture | farming: growing plants and raising animals for food | modern agriculture approaches |
| economic factors | things related to money | factors that drive food choices |
| food marketing | the ways companies advertise and sell food | marketing practices and biology |
| legislation | laws, or making laws | novel legal approaches |
