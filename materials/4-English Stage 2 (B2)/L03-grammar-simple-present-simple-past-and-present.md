# Lesson 03: Grammar: Simple Present, Simple Past and Present Perfect

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 1: Academic Writing Essentials · Psychology*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 1.4: Grammar Presentation-Simple Present, Simple Past, and Present Perfect Tenses; 1.5: Applying Grammar to Reading; 1.6: Communication Practice; Irregular Verbs.*

## In this lesson
You will review three tenses that academic texts use all the time: the simple present (facts and theories), the simple past (finished events and studies) and the present perfect (time up to now). You will find them in "The Story Behind Dreams", practise the -ed ending, and write a paragraph that introduces a person.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Use the simple present with correct subject-verb agreement (he/she/it + -s).
- Choose between the simple past and the present perfect by looking at signal words.
- Form the past and past participle of common irregular verbs.
- Identify the tenses in an academic reading.
- Pronounce the -ed ending as /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simple present | the base form (the verb with no ending), or base form + -s after he/she/it | The Earth rotates on its axis. |
| third-person singular | he, she, it, or one person or thing | Mika plays video games. |
| subject-verb agreement | the verb matches its subject (singular or plural) | You play video games. |
| simple past | a completed action in the past | Yesterday, Victoria went to the bank. |
| signal word | a word or phrase that tells when something happened | yesterday, three months ago, last week |
| present perfect | have/has + past participle | Anastasiya has eaten sushi. |
| past participle | the verb form used after have/has | eaten, written, woken |
| irregular verb | a verb whose past is not made with -ed | go, went, gone |

## Rules and explanations
### Simple present
| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| General facts | The Earth rotates on its axis. |
| Routines and habits | She practices gratitude every day. |
| Feelings | He enjoys hiking. |
| Possessions | Yun-Ji has a car. |

### Subject-verb agreement
- After a third-person singular subject (he, she, it, or one person or thing), the verb changes: Mika plays video games.
- After I, you, we, they and plural subjects, use the base form: You play video games. Two subjects joined with "and" are plural.
- Three ways to make the he/she/it form:

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| add -s | play → plays |
| add -es or -ies | miss → misses; study → studies (consonant + y → -ies) |
| irregular | have → has; do → does |

### Simple past
- A completed action in the past, often with a signal word: yesterday; [length of time] + ago; last night, week, month, year or a specific day.
- Yesterday, Victoria went to the bank. Three months ago, Ayman visited Los Angeles, California. Last week, we studied paragraphs.

### Present perfect
| Form | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | have/has + past participle | Anastasiya has eaten sushi. |
| negative | have/has + not + past participle | The package still hasn't arrived. |
| question | have/has + subject + past participle | Have you ever broken a bone? |

The present perfect has five uses:
1. The time of the event is unknown (the action is finished): Anastasiya has eaten sushi.
2. The action started in the past and continues now: Kyaw and Sunil have been friends for several years.
3. The action has been repeated several times: Rafael and Jianhua have been absent many times this semester.
4. An uncompleted action is expected to finish: The package still hasn't arrived.
5. The action happened recently: The children have just woken up.

### Simple past or present perfect?
- A finished time is given (yesterday, ago, last ..., in February, when I was a child): use the simple past. NOT: Yesterday, Victoria has gone to the bank.
- No time, or time up to now (for, since, so far, this week, ever, already, just, still): use the present perfect.

### Irregular verbs
Learn them in the book's groups (base form, past, past participle):

| Group | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | all three the same | cut, cut, cut; put, put, put |
| 2 | i, a, u | sing, sang, sung; swim, swam, swum |
| 3 | -ew, -own | know, knew, known; fly, flew, flown |
| 4 | -ought, -aught | think, thought, thought; teach, taught, taught |
| 5 and 6 | participle with -en | write, wrote, written; break, broke, broken |
| 7 | d becomes t | send, sent, sent; build, built, built |
| 8 | past = participle | pay, paid, paid; keep, kept, kept |

The book's table has three printing mistakes: bring, brought, brought, **bringing** (not "brining"); forgive, forgave, **forgiven** (not "forgiving"); tell, **told**, told (not "sold").

### Pronunciation of -ed
| Sound | When | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| /t/ | after the voiceless sounds /p/ /k/ /f/ /s/ /ʃ/ /tʃ/ | shopped, baked, laughed, kissed, pushed, punched |
| /d/ | after voiced sounds (/b/ /g/ /v/ /z/ /m/ /n/ /l/ /r/ /ŋ/ /ð/ /ʒ/ /dʒ/) and after all vowel sounds | robbed, loved, prayed, used, called, changed |
| /ɪd/ | after /t/ and /d/; it adds a syllable | needed, wanted |

A voiced sound makes your vocal cords vibrate; a voiceless sound does not. To test a sound, put your hand on your throat: if you feel a vibration, the sound is voiced.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic has no present perfect form, so it is easy to use the simple past or present for a situation that continues now ("I live here since 2020"). In English, use have/has + past participle: Kyaw and Sunil have been friends for several years.
> - Arabic has no /p/ sound. Say /p/ clearly in shopped, and remember that /p/ is voiceless, so the ending is /t/: shopped /t/.

## Writing task
**Introduce a person (Exercise 13).** After your interview with your tutor (see Speak with your tutor), write a paragraph of 120 to 160 words that introduces this "classmate" to a new class, or introduce yourself in the third person (He is from ...). Include: name, country of origin, current city, arrival in this city, classes this semester, previous classes, length of time studying English, hobbies and interests. Use the simple present for the situation now, the simple past for finished events with a time, and the present perfect with for or since.

Checklist:
- Every simple present verb after he, she or a name has -s, -es or -ies.
- Every finished time (ago, last ..., in 2020) has a simple past verb, never the present perfect.
- I used the present perfect with for or since for situations that continue now.
- I checked my irregular verbs in the table (took, began, spent).
- My first sentence introduces the person, and my last sentence closes the paragraph.

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
