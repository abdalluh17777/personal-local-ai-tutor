# Lesson 27: Infants: Discussion, Listening and Reading

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 8.1: Discussion; 8.2: Listening-How Every Child Can Thrive by Five; 8.3: Reading-Mirror Stage; 16.6: Chapter 6 (audio transcript).*

## In this lesson
This lesson starts the chapter "Infants". You will discuss how children's brains develop, watch a TED talk by seven-year-old Molly Wright, "How Every Child Can Thrive by Five", and read a short academic text, "Mirror Stage", about when babies recognize themselves in a mirror. You will practise finding the central idea of a text and the evidence for it, a key skill for your psychology reading, and you will notice how writers report what researchers said, which prepares you for Lesson 28 (reported speech).

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Understand the main points and the numbers in a short talk about early brain development.
- Identify the central idea of an academic text and choose the quotation that best supports it.
- Describe how a theory changed over time and why other researchers disagreed with it.
- Recognize reporting verbs such as *show*, *propose* and *claim* in an academic text.
- Give and support your opinion about child development and technology.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| infant | a baby; a very young child | Lacan proposed that the mirror stage was part of an infant's development from 6 to 18 months. |
| toddler | a young child who has just learned to walk | ... when infants and toddlers start recognizing their reflections in the mirror. |
| thrive | to grow, develop and do well | How Every Child Can Thrive by Five |
| neural connection | a link between nerve cells in the brain | It can create up to one million neural connections every second. |
| serve and return | the name scientists use for the back-and-forth contact between a child and an adult; Molly says it means "connect, talk and play with us" | Scientists call it serve and return. |
| hardwired | natural, present from birth, not learned | Kids are hardwired to seek out meaningful connections. |
| empathy | the ability to understand and share another person's feelings | Copycat games build imagination and empathy. |
| reflection | the image of yourself that you see in a mirror | Human infants and chimpanzees both seem to recognize their reflection in a mirror. |
| identity | your sense of who you are | ... the mirror stage's importance in identity formation |
| phenomenon (plural: phenomena) | something that happens and that people can observe and study | "The mirror stage is a phenomenon to which I assign [two values]." |

| notion | an idea or a belief | Many other psychologists disagreed with the notion that ... |

## Rules and explanations

### How to work through this lesson
1. **Discussion:** think about the two questions before you watch (Practice 1).
2. **Listening first:** watch the talk without the transcript and answer Exercise 1. Only then read the transcript (at the end of your lesson material) to check your answers and find the words you missed.
3. **Reading:** read "Mirror Stage" in your lesson material and answer Exercise 2.

### Before you watch
- The speaker, Molly Wright, is seven years old. On stage with her are Ari, a baby, and his dad, Amarjot, Molly's neighbor. They show an "experiment" with the game peekaboo.
- The questions ask about a number, a list of five things, a definition and a feeling. Listen for these.

### Reading questions: central idea and evidence
- **Part A** asks for the *central idea*: the idea of the whole text, not one detail. Check each option: Is it in the text? Does it cover the whole text or only one paragraph?
- **Part B** asks for *evidence*: the quotation that best supports your Part A answer.
- **"The evolution of a concept"** means how an idea changed over time. Look for the time expressions in paragraph 2: *In 1936 ...*, *By the early 1950s ...*.

### Notice: how the reading reports research
"Mirror Stage" reports the ideas of several psychologists. Writers often use a **reporting verb** (a verb that introduces what someone said, wrote or found) + a *that*-clause (a part of the sentence that begins with *that* and has its own subject and verb):
- *Research **has shown** that ... they do not begin to recognize that the images in the mirror are reflections of their own bodies until the age of about 15 to 18 months.* (paragraph 1)
- *In 1936, psychologist Jacques Lacan **proposed** that the mirror stage was part of an infant's development from 6 to 18 months.* (paragraph 2)

Paragraph 3 also has **direct speech**, Lacan's exact words in quotation marks: *In 1953, Lacan wrote, "The mirror stage is a phenomenon ..."*. In Lesson 28 you will find all the reporting verbs in this reading and learn the rules of reported speech.

## Practice

**1. Discussion (before you watch).** Answer the questions based on your experience and opinion. Say your answers aloud and record them on your phone (1 to 2 minutes for each question), or discuss them with your tutor (see "Speak with your tutor").

2. Can technology be beneficial for children? How? Why not?

**3. Exercise 2: Reading comprehension.** Read "Mirror Stage" in your lesson material (five paragraphs). Then answer the questions.
1. PART A: Which of the following statements best summarizes a central idea of the text?
   - a) The human brain is wired to recognize faces everywhere, even in inanimate objects.
   - b) Recognizing one's own face, as well as the faces of others, is crucial to forming a healthy identity.
   - c) Mirror stage describes the period in which infants begin recognizing their reflection, though its significance in identity development has been debated.
   - d) Mirror stage is the phenomenon in which two or more close individuals subconsciously mimic each other's expressions, actions, and/or gestures.
2. PART B: Which of the following quotes best supports the answer to Part A?
   - a) "...children are fascinated with images of themselves and others in mirrors...." (Paragraph 1)
   - b) "The 'mirror stage' refers to the point in time when infants and toddlers start recognizing their reflections in the mirror.... In psychology, there has been a debate about the mirror stage's importance in identity formation." (Paragraph 1)
   - c) "Lacan felt that understanding and processing one's reflection was a key part in the development of identity." (Paragraph 2)
   - d) "...human infants typically become very interested and devote much time and effort to exploring the connections between their bodies and their images." (Paragraph 4)
3. Which of the following best describes the evolution of Jacques Lacan's concept of the mirror stage?
   - a) Lacan changes the ages of the infantile mirror stage from 15-18 months to 6-18 months, allowing for a larger age range when babies can recognize their reflections.
   - b) Lacan calls the mirror stage as a phenomenon but later labels it as a general part of normal human (and chimpanzee) development.
   - c) Lacan's theory becomes more abstract, the concept taking on a more philosophical tone than a scientific one.
   - d) Lacan alters his concept so that it is not considered just a small period of an infant's life, but an essential and lasting element of a person's identity.

## Answer key

**1. Discussion** (example answer)
1. Children's brains develop best when adults connect with them, talk to them and play with them, and when they have a healthy home and a community around them. Parents can talk to their baby often, play simple games like peekaboo and naming games, and give their full attention instead of looking at their phones.
2. Technology can be beneficial in some ways: a child can video-call grandparents who live far away, and some apps teach letters and numbers. But it cannot replace people. In the talk, Molly Wright says that adults need to use their devices sometimes, but kids are hardwired to seek out meaningful connections, and not receiving them causes confusion and stress. So I think technology is useful only in small amounts, and adults should use it together with the child.

**2. Exercise 1** (book)
1. d (90%). Molly: "by my age, it's almost 90 percent the volume of an adult brain."
2. d (Technology). The five things are connecting, talking, playing, a healthy home and community.
3. a. "That's just a grown-up way of saying connect, talk and play with us."
4. b. "... not receiving them causes confusion and stress."

**3. Exercise 2** (book)
1. c. Options a and d are not in the text. Option b is close to Lacan's idea, but the text says other psychologists disagreed with it, so it is not the central idea of the whole text.
2. b. It gives both parts of the central idea: what the mirror stage is (the definition) and the debate about its importance.
3. d. Paragraph 2: by the early 1950s, "he no longer considered the mirror stage as a moment in the life of the infant, but as representing a permanent structure of identity." (Option a is wrong: the age of 15 to 18 months comes from the research in paragraph 1, not from a change in Lacan's idea.)

## Read aloud
Read each sentence slowly, then at normal speed. The stressed syllable of the long words is in capitals.

2. Copycat games build i·mag·i·NA·tion and EM·pa·thy.
3. Naming games build vo·CAB·u·lar·y and at·TEN·tion.
4. "Mirror stage" refers to the point in time when infants and TOD·dlers start recognizing their re·FLEC·tions in the mirror.
5. In psy·CHOL·o·gy, there has been a debate about the mirror stage's importance in i·DEN·ti·ty formation.
6. The mirror stage is a phe·NOM·e·non to which I assign two values.

## Writing task
In the book, the discussion is a written post for an online class forum. Write your own post on one discussion question from Practice 1 (120 to 180 words): a first sentence that answers the question directly, two reasons, each with an example from the talk, the reading or your own experience, and a final sentence that sums up your opinion. Name the talk and the speaker when you use her ideas, and use at least three words from the Key language table.

Self-editing checklist:
1. Does my first sentence answer the question clearly?
2. Did I give two reasons, each with an example?
3. Did I name my source (the speaker and the title) when I used an idea from the talk?
4. Did I use the simple present for facts and opinions (Children need ...) and the simple past for my own past experiences?
5. Does every sentence have a subject and a verb?

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it. Then ask your tutor to write two short posts as two classmates, and reply to each one: point out a similarity or a difference, ask a clarifying question, or give your opinion on a point they make.
