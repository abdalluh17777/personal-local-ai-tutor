# Lesson 36: Grammar: Simple Future

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 3: Verb Tenses and Narration · The Human Lifespan*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 10.4: Grammar Presentation-Simple Future; 10.5: Communication Practice.*

## In this lesson
In Lesson 34 you learned how to form will and be going to, when to choose each one, and how to write future time clauses. This lesson adds the full sentence patterns: negatives, yes/no questions, short answers and information questions. You will also decide whether a present verb is about present or future time, and make predictions and plans with your tutor. Correct questions and short answers matter in interviews, surveys and class discussions.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Make negative statements, yes/no questions, short answers and information questions with will and be going to.
- Use will for predictions, volunteering, promises and quick decisions, and be going to for predictions and plans.
- Tell whether a simple present or present progressive sentence is about present or future time.
- Make predictions about everyday situations and about the future of a college.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| statement | a sentence that gives information (not a question) | Ivana is going to take classes next semester. |
| negative statement | a statement with not | The population will not grow slowly. |
| contraction | a short form with an apostrophe | won't = will not; I'll = I will; isn't = is not |
| yes/no question | a question you answer with yes or no | Will the population grow slowly? |
| short answer | Yes or No + pronoun + will / won't / be | Yes, it will. No, she isn't. |
| information question | a question that starts with a question word (where, when, why, how, what) | Why will the population grow slowly? |
| pre·DIC·tion | a guess about the future | The event is going to be a success! |
| vol·un·TEER·ing | offering to do something | I will help you study for the exam. |
| promise | saying that you will certainly do something | I will take out the trash after dinner. I promise! |
| quick decision | a decision made at the moment of speaking | Is the movie at 5PM or 7:30PM? Ok, I'll go! |
| SCHED·ule / timetable | a list of the times when events happen | The movie starts at 8:30PM. |
| ar·RANGE·ment | a plan for a certain time and place, often made with another person | I am receiving tutoring tomorrow in the library. |

## Rules and explanations
### Four ways to talk about the future
- Simple future with will: The population will grow slowly.
- Simple future with be going to: The population is going to be more diverse.
- Simple present: The class begins at 8:00AM tomorrow.
- Present progressive: The bus is leaving the station at 7:30PM this evening.

### Will: patterns
| Sentence type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | Subject + will + base verb | The population will grow slowly. |
| Negative | Subject + will not (won't) + base verb | The population will not grow slowly. |
| Yes/no question | Will + subject + base verb? | Will the population grow slowly? |
| Short answers | Yes, pronoun + will. / No, pronoun + won't. | Yes, it will. / No, it won't. |
| Information question | Question word + will + subject + base verb? | Why will the population grow slowly? |

- Will never changes: no "wills", no -s on the verb after it, and the negative contraction is won't.
- In a short answer with yes, do not contract: "Yes, it will." The contraction 'll is fine inside a sentence: "Ok, I'll go!"

### Will: purposes
- Predictions: It is estimated that in 2030, the baby boomers will be over age 65.
- Volunteering: I will help you study for the exam.
- Promises: I will take out the trash after dinner. I promise!
- Quick decisions: Is the movie at 5PM or 7:30PM? Ok, I'll go!

### Be going to: patterns
| Sentence type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | Subject + be going to + base verb | Ivana is going to take classes next semester. |
| Negative | Subject + be not going to + base verb | Ivana is not going to take classes next semester. |
| Yes/no question | Be verb + subject + going to + base verb? | Is Ivana going to take classes next semester? |
| Short answers | Yes, pronoun + be verb. / No, pronoun + be verb + not (contraction). | Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. |
| Information question | Question word + be verb + subject + going to + base verb? | Where is Ivana going to take classes next semester? |

- The be verb (am, is, are) agrees with the subject and moves before the subject in questions. Never add do or does: the question is made with be.
- After going to, use the base verb (no -s, no -ed).

### Be going to: purposes
- Prediction: The event is going to be a success!
- Plans: I am going to visit my grandparents on Sunday.

These purposes match Lesson 34: both forms can predict; will is for decisions made at the moment of speaking and for promises; be going to is for plans made before.

### Present forms with a future meaning
- Simple present for a schedule or timetable: The movie starts at 8:30PM.
- Present progressive for a future arrangement: I am receiving tutoring tomorrow in the library.
- Present or future? Look for a future time expression (tomorrow, this evening, next semester) or a schedule. Without them, a present verb usually talks about present time.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic questions keep the word order of a statement, so learners often write "What you will study next year?" In English, put will or the be verb before the subject: "What will you study next year?" / "What are you going to study next year?"

## Read aloud
Rising intonation (↗) for yes/no questions, falling (↘) for information questions. Won't is /woʊnt/, not "want" /wɑnt/.
- Will the population grow slowly? (↗) Yes, it will. No, it won't.
- Why will the population grow slowly? (↘)
- Is Ivana going to take classes next semester? (↗) Yes, she is. No, she isn't.
- Where is Ivana going to take classes next semester? (↘)
- Is the movie at 5PM or 7:30PM? Ok, I'll go!
- I am receiving tutoring tomorrow in the library.

## Writing task
Book Exercise 9, Predictions about College. What will your college or university be like in 2050? Write a paragraph of 8 to 10 sentences with at least five predictions. What will change? What will be the same? Think about the buildings, services and instruction (how classes are taught).
- Start with a topic sentence, for example: "By 2050, my university will be very different from today."
- Use will and be going to, at least one negative (won't, isn't going to) and one future time clause (Lesson 34).

Self-editing checklist:
- Will is followed by a base verb, never by -s or -ing.
- Every going to has am, is or are before it.
- Negatives: won't + base verb; am/is/are not going to + base verb.
- Any questions use question word order (will or be before the subject).
- The time clause has a present verb.
- Each prediction has a reason or an example.

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
