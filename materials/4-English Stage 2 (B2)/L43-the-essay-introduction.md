## In this lesson
You move from writing paragraphs to writing essays. You compare a paragraph and an essay by the same student writer, learn the parts of a five-paragraph essay (hook, thesis statement, supporting paragraphs, concluding paragraph), and practise writing thesis statements, introductory paragraphs and an outline. Essays are the most common writing assignment in college courses, so this is a key step in your academic writing.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Explain the similarities and differences between a paragraph and an essay.
- Find the hook, the thesis statement, the topic sentences, the transitions and the conclusion in an essay.
- Write a thesis statement and an introductory paragraph.
- Make an essay outline and write a five-paragraph essay from it.

## Rules and explanations

### Paragraph and essay
A **paragraph** is a group of sentences about one main idea. An **essay** is a group of paragraphs about one main idea. An essay can be three paragraphs or many pages long; in this course you write five-paragraph essays.

**The same in both:** one topic and one main idea; academic style (logical, direct, formal, clear, concise); a title, a beginning, a body and a conclusion; interesting, detailed support for the main idea; transitions; good sentence structure, grammar, spelling, punctuation, capitalization and format.

| Differences | Paragraph | Essay |
|---|---|---|
| Name and position of the main idea | topic sentence, usually at the beginning | thesis statement, usually at the end of the introduction |
| Supporting details | some details | more details |
| Length | one paragraph | three or more paragraphs (here: five) |
| Planning and writing time | shorter | longer |

### The five-paragraph essay
- Paragraph 1, the **introductory paragraph**: a hook, then the thesis statement as the last sentence.
- Paragraphs 2 to 4, three **supporting paragraphs**: each begins with a topic sentence (one supporting idea of the thesis) and has many details: facts, explanations, examples.
- Paragraph 5, the **concluding paragraph**.

A good way to start is to expand a paragraph you have written. In the book's examples, the paragraph's main idea becomes the thesis statement, each supporting idea becomes a supporting paragraph with more details, and the essay adds an introductory paragraph and a concluding paragraph.

### The hook
A coat hook on the wall holds your coat; a hook at the beginning of an essay holds the readers' attention so that they keep reading. It introduces the topic and gets readers interested. The most common hooks give **background** ("When I was a child, I had an opportunity to come to the United States a few times ...") or **general information** about the topic ("A hobby is a regular activity performed for enjoyment during spare time.").

### The thesis statement
The rules for topic sentences also apply to the thesis statement:
- It has a topic and a controlling idea, and it may have predictors (words that name the supporting points): "The three places to make friends in the U.S. for me are my neighborhood, school, and church."
- It is a complete sentence (subject + verb).
- It cannot be a question, an announcement or a fact.

### Supporting paragraphs, transitions and conclusion
- The rules for a paragraph also apply to an essay, but an essay has more supporting details.
- Use transitions to connect the supporting paragraphs. The transition before the first supporting idea and the one before the conclusion are often optional. "Finally" is for the last supporting idea, not for the conclusion.
- Make an outline first. Delete information that does not support the main idea.
- The conclusion usually restates the main idea or summarizes the main points. It can also give an opinion, a prediction or a recommendation, or ask a question. It must not bring up a new topic.

## Answer key

**Warm-up**
1. Paragraph: "There are three places to make friends in the United States." Essay: "The three places to make friends in the U.S. for me are my neighborhood, school, and church." (the last sentence of the first paragraph)
2. Five.
3. The neighborhood, an American school and a church. Yes, the essay explains the same three places.
4. An introductory paragraph with a hook (the writer's background) and a thesis statement; more details in the supporting paragraphs (for example, swimming and playing with dogs, classmates and teachers who spoke slowly and patiently); and a separate concluding paragraph.

**Paragraph and essay: "My Joy in Baking"**
1. Paragraph: "Baking is my favorite hobby." Essay: "This is why baking became and has always been my favorite hobby."
2. Paragraph: the first sentence (the topic sentence). Essay: the last sentence of the introductory paragraph (the thesis statement).
3. Paragraph: one. Essay: five.
4. The same in both: (1) learning to bake as a child was not easy, but she enjoyed it and it gave her confidence; (2) as an adult, baking relaxes her and relieves her stress; (3) sharing her baking with family and friends gives her joy and makes the relationships stronger.
5. Paragraph: "When I was a child", "Now", "also" ("I also like to share ..."). Essay: no transition before the first supporting paragraph (it is optional), then "Now" and "Most importantly".
6. Paragraph: the main idea again ("These are all the reasons why baking is my favorite hobby.") and a recommendation ("I would recommend it to anyone who loves sweets ..."). Essay: a general statement about hobbies, the main idea with a plan for the future ("I intend to enjoy it for many more years to come."), and the same recommendation.

**Exercise 1** (example answers)
1. Parents have a strong influence on their children's habits, values and goals.
2. Even happily married couples have fights, but they solve them in healthy ways.
3. Getting my dream job as a psychologist will take years of hard work.
4. Self-discipline is the most important key to success in college.
5. Being bilingual brings benefits at work, in travel and in family life.
6. Having too much money can cause as many problems as having too little.
7. The government should assist college students by offering more scholarships and cheaper housing.
8. A positive personality is one of the keys to happiness.

**Exercise 2** (example answer, for thesis 4) Many students start college with big dreams. They want good grades, a degree and an interesting job. However, college life is full of distractions, such as social media, part-time jobs and friends who want to go out. The students who reach their goals are not always the most talented ones. Self-discipline is the most important key to success in college.

**Exercise 3** (example answer, for thesis 4)
- Hook: big dreams; many distractions in college life.
- Thesis statement: Self-discipline is the most important key to success in college.
- Topic sentence 1: managing time. Details: weekly schedule, study before social media.
- Topic sentence 2: going to every class prepared. Details: reading, notes, questions.
- Topic sentence 3: taking care of health. Details: sleep, exercise, healthy meals.
- Concluding paragraph: the three habits again; self-discipline is hard but brings success.

**Essay analysis**
1. Background: she came to the U.S. from Tokyo with her son in 2017, and living in a foreign country was a totally new experience for her.
2. "I encountered some surprises in my first few weeks of living here." It is the last sentence of the introductory paragraph.
3. "The first surprise was about the nature." / "Second, I was not prepared for some customs here." / "What shocked me the most was that people were very friendly toward strangers."
4. "The first surprise", "Second", "What shocked me the most".
5. Enumerative: it lists three surprises (nature, customs, people). Order of importance: it ends with what "shocked me the most".
6. A summary of the three surprises (nature, customs, people), the writer's opinion ("This is a wonderful country.") and her feelings now ("I'm enjoying my life here now and feel that this is my second hometown.").

## Writing task
Write a five-paragraph essay of about 400 to 600 words (the book's essay writing assignment). Choose one topic:
1. Expand one of the paragraphs you have written in this course into an essay.
2. Use one of your thesis statements, introductory paragraphs and outlines from Exercises 1 to 3.
3. A new topic: three things you want to change about your college or university; three things (or events, or people) you wish to forget; the three most important ways people in your country express love; the most memorable day in your life; how you overcame a challenge in your life.

Brainstorm, make an outline with the template, then write. Your essay needs a title, an introductory paragraph with a hook and a thesis statement, three supporting paragraphs (each starting with a topic sentence followed by lots of details), and a concluding paragraph, with transitions between the supporting paragraphs. Type it double spaced and indent the first line of each paragraph.

Self-editing checklist:
- Is my essay clear and easy to understand?
- Do I have an interesting hook, and a clear thesis statement at the end of the introductory paragraph?
- Does each supporting paragraph begin with a topic sentence and include lots of details?
- Did I connect the supporting paragraphs with appropriate transitions?
- Does my concluding paragraph restate the main idea or summarize the main points, without a new topic?
- Did I use correct sentence structure (simple, compound, complex)?
- Did I check nouns, pronouns, word forms, word order, spelling, punctuation and capitalization?
- Did I follow the format (title, indented paragraphs)?
