# Lesson 39: Nouns

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 4: Words, Word Order and the Essay*
*Adapted from Building Academic Writing Skills by Lin Cui, Harper College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): Unit 16 Nouns.*

## In this lesson
You review the types of English nouns (proper and common, singular and plural, count and noncount) and learn seven rules for using them. You practise finding noun errors in sentences and paragraphs about food and nutrition. Noun errors ("informations", "two cup", "I eat apple") are very common in student writing, so this helps you edit your own academic work.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Tell proper nouns from common nouns and capitalize proper nouns.
- Spell the plural forms of regular and irregular nouns.
- Use count and noncount nouns with the right determiners, verbs and pronouns.
- Use nouns correctly after "one of", "each" and "every", and in number + noun adjectives.
- Find and correct noun errors in a paragraph.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| noun | a name of a person, place, thing or idea | scientist, Chicago, safety |
| proper noun | a specific name of a person, place or organization; it starts with a capital letter | Harper College |
| common noun | a general name; no capital letter | president, office |
| count noun | a noun you can count (1, 2, 3...); it has a singular and a plural form | one egg, three eggs |
| noncount noun | a noun you cannot count; it is always singular | water, information |
| determiner | a word before a noun: an article (a, an, the), a number, a possessive adjective (my, his) or a demonstrative adjective (this, that) | **This** label |
| nutrient-dense | full of nutrients | nutrient-dense foods and beverages |
| beverage | a drink | 85% of our calories from nutrient-dense foods and beverages |
| garnish | food or drink decoration to increase flavor, color, etc. | garnish made of vegetables |
| rinse | wash lightly by pouring water over something | rinse the beans with water |
| vegetarian | a person who eats only vegetables | Nutrients for Vegetarians |
| fixture | something that is fixed in a place | a weekly fixture on my family's dinner table |

## Rules and explanations

### Proper nouns and common nouns
- **Proper nouns** are specific names: Albert Einstein, Michigan Avenue, the Department of Agriculture. They start with a capital letter.
- **Common nouns** are general names: scientist, city, computer, safety. No capital letter (except at the start of a sentence).

### Singular and plural: spelling
(Vowels are a, e, i, o, u. Consonants are all the other letters.)

| The singular noun ends in | Plural | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| most letters | add -s | apple - apples, grape - grapes |
| ch, sh, x, s, ss, z | add -es | dish - dishes, box - boxes, bus - buses (but quiz - quizzes, ox - oxen) |
| vowel + y | add -s | day - days, key - keys |
| consonant + y | delete y, add -ies | family - families, strawberry - strawberries |
| vowel + o | add -s | radio - radios, zoo - zoos |
| consonant + o | add -es | hero - heroes, potato - potatoes (but piano - pianos, photo - photos) |
| f or fe | delete f or fe, add -ves | leaf - leaves, knife - knives, half - halves (but chef - chefs, roof - roofs, belief - beliefs) |

- Irregular plurals: man - men, child - children, person - people, foot - feet, tooth - teeth, goose - geese, crisis - crises, analysis - analyses.
- The same form for one and many: sheep, deer, fish, shrimp. Always plural: glasses, scissors, jeans, pants.

### Count and noncount nouns
- **Count nouns** can be counted: one brother, three eggs. Use the plural form for more than one.
- **Noncount nouns** cannot be counted or are too small to count. They are always singular: water, coffee, rice, salt; English, math; danger, love.
- To count a noncount noun, count a unit: a cup of fruit juice, two slices of bread, three pieces of new equipment.

### Seven rules for using nouns
1. Do not put "a/an" or a number before a noncount noun, and do not add -s to it.
   - Wrong: The restaurant has installed three new equipments. Right: ... some new equipment / three pieces of new equipment.
2. Do not use "a/an" and -s on the same noun. Wrong: Many children eat an apples every day. Right: an apple / apples.
3. A singular count noun needs a determiner. Wrong: I eat apple every day. Right: I eat an apple (one apple) every day. Wrong: Please join discussion on healthy living. Right: our discussion / the discussion.
4. A singular count noun or a noncount noun takes a singular verb and pronoun; a plural noun takes a plural verb and pronoun.
   - "A medium tomato **has** about twenty-two calories ... **It** does not contain any cholesterol."
   - "Fresh tomatoes **are** on sale ... **They** taste great."
5. "one of" + plural noun + singular verb (the subject is "one"): "One of the nutrients from dairy **is** calcium."
6. A number + noun that works as an adjective (before another noun) stays singular, because English adjectives have no plural: a 3-credit-hour course, 9-year-old boys. Compare: a child nine years old.
7. "each" and "every" + singular count noun: "**Each glass** is filled with juice." "**Every guest** is having a fun time."

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Some English noncount nouns are plural words in Arabic (for example "information", "equipment" and "advice"), so learners write "informations" or "equipments". In English these nouns never take -s.
> - Arabic has no separate word for "a/an", and after numbers above ten Arabic uses a singular noun. In English a singular count noun needs a determiner ("an apple"), and every number above one takes a plural noun ("thirty minutes").

## Read aloud
The plural ending sounds /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/.
- One egg contains many nutrients /s/.
- Most people eat three meals /z/ a day, with a snack in between.
- Some sell computers /z/, and some sell groceries /z/.
- There are two slices /ɪz/ of bread in my lunch box.
- Each glass is filled with juice for children and wine for adults /s/.

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 150 to 200 words (Exercise 11): What is your favorite food from your native country? What food group does it belong to? Why is it your favorite? Can you buy it where you live now? Include a short recipe if you can.
- Include a title, a topic sentence, three supporting points with details, and a concluding sentence.
- Use five or more plural nouns, two noncount nouns with a unit (a cup of rice), one "one of" + plural noun, and one "each" or "every" + singular noun.

Self-editing checklist:
- Proper nouns start with a capital letter.
- Plural nouns are spelled correctly (-s, -es, -ies, -ves, irregular).
- No noncount noun has "a/an", a number or -s.
- Every singular count noun has a determiner (a, an, the, my, this...).
- Verbs and pronouns agree with their nouns.
- "One of" + plural noun + singular verb; "each/every" + singular noun.
- Number + noun adjectives are singular: a 30-minute recipe.
