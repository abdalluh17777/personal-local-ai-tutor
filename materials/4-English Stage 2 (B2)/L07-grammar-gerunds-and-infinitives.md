# Lesson 07: Grammar: Gerunds and Infinitives

*English Stage 2 (B2) · Unit 1: Academic Writing Essentials · Psychology*
*Adapted from High-Intermediate Academic Grammar for ESL Students by Rebecca Al Haider, Reedley College (CC BY 4.0): 2.4: Grammar Presentation-Gerunds and Infinitives; 2.5: Communication Practice; Gerunds and Infinitives.*

## In this lesson
You will learn how -ing forms (gerunds) and to + verb forms (infinitives) work in a sentence, which words they follow, and when the choice changes the meaning. Academic texts use them all the time to name processes, as in the reading "Coping Mechanisms": "Problem-solving focuses on locating the source of the problem."

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Identify a gerund as a subject, an object or the object of a preposition, and tell it apart from a progressive verb or an adjective.
- Use a singular verb after a gerund subject.
- Choose a gerund or an infinitive after common verbs, using the book's lists.
- Use infinitives of purpose and infinitives after adjectives and nouns.
- Explain the change in meaning after stop, remember, forget and regret.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| gerund | the -ing form of a verb used as a noun | Reading is fun. |
| infinitive | to + the base form of a verb | I want to live a long happy life. |
| base form | the verb with no ending and no "to" | live, help, be |
| object of a preposition | the noun or gerund after a preposition (in, about, for, of) | Jose is interested in reading that book. |
| progressive verb | be + -ing: an action in progress | The baby is sleeping soundly. |
| modal | a helping verb such as have to or used to, followed by the base form | Fatima has to visit the embassy. |
| infinitive of purpose | to + verb that gives the reason (Why?) | I went to the store to buy the missing ingredients. |
| verb + object + infinitive | a verb, then a person, then to + verb | His mother wants him to stay close to home. |

## Rules and explanations
### Gerunds: -ing forms used as nouns
| Function | Example |
|---|---|
| subject | Reading is fun. |
| object (after a verb) | I enjoy reading. |
| object of a preposition | Jose is interested in reading that book. |

- A gerund subject is one action, so the verb is singular: Baking pies **is** my hobby. Two gerunds joined with "and" are plural: Baking pies and crocheting hats **are** my hobbies.
- **Verbs followed by gerunds:** admit, anticipate, appreciate, avoid, consider, defend, delay, deny, discontinue, discuss, dislike, enjoy, explain, fear, finish, go (in expressions: go camping), imagine, keep, mention, miss, practice, recommend, suggest, understand. Emily enjoys jogging in the mornings. I suggest enrolling in a math class.
- **After a preposition, use a gerund:** Jinan is excited about playing with her cousins.

### Gerund, progressive verb or adjective?
- My hobby is baking pies. (gerund: you can reverse it: Baking pies is my hobby.)
- The baby is sleeping soundly. (verb: be + -ing, the progressive)
- The sleeping baby looks so peaceful. (adjective: it describes a noun)

### Infinitives: to + base form
- "To" before a noun is a preposition, not an infinitive: I gave a gift to my mom. We studied from 6PM to 10PM.
- "Have to" and "used to" are modals + base form: Eçe used to live in Cairo, Egypt.
- "Be used to" is adjective + preposition, so a gerund follows: Ruth is used to waking up early.
- An infinitive as a subject is rare and sounds odd. Writers usually start with "It": It is important to wear a seatbelt.

### Verbs, adjectives and nouns followed by infinitives
- **Verb + infinitive:** agree, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, choose, decide, demand, deserve, expect, fail, get, hope, learn, need, offer, pay, plan, pretend, seem, strive, struggle, tend, threaten, wait, want. Habib offered to buy me coffee.
- **Verb + noun/pronoun + infinitive:** advise, allow, ask, encourage, expect, force, invite, order, remind, tell. Need and want take both patterns: Geraldo wants to transfer to a university. His mother wants him to stay close to home.
- **Infinitive of purpose** (the answer to "Why?"). "In order" is optional, and the infinitive can come at the end or at the start: I am learning Arabic (in order) to communicate with my mother-in-law. (In order) to communicate with my mother-in-law, I am learning Arabic.
- **Adjective + infinitive:** Tomoki was surprised to see his mother at the store.
- **Noun + infinitive** (the infinitive comes after the noun and describes it): Your reluctance to help is frustrating. I am happy with his decision to take the day off. Common nouns: ability, chance, decision, goal, need, opportunity, plan, promise, way (full list: Table 8 in the lesson material). (In "I went to the store to buy ...", to buy does not describe the store: it is a purpose.)

### Gerund or infinitive?
- **No real change in meaning:** begin, continue, hate, like, love, prefer, propose, start.
- **A big change in meaning:** forget, quit, regret, remember, stop, try. Cynthia stopped smoking (= she quit smoking). Cynthia stopped to smoke (= she stopped doing something so that she could smoke).

| Verb | + infinitive | + gerund |
|---|---|---|
| stop | stop one activity in order to do another | quit an activity |
| remember / forget | (not) do a task that you need to do | (not) have a memory of a past action |
| regret | be sorry about bad news that you are giving now | be sorry about something you did in the past |

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic often uses "that" + a verb after verbs like want and expect. English uses an object + infinitive: NOT My mother wants that I study medicine. Say: My mother wants me to study medicine.

## Writing task
**How I cope with stress.** Write a paragraph of 120 to 160 words about the ways you deal with stress as a student. Use at least three gerunds (one as a subject, one after a verb from the list, one after a preposition) and three infinitives (one after a verb, one infinitive of purpose, one after an adjective). Underline them.

Checklist:
- A gerund subject has a singular verb (Exercising helps me ...).
- After a preposition, I used -ing (by talking, instead of worrying).
- I checked each verb in the lists: enjoy, avoid, keep + -ing; decide, plan, need + to.
- After want, ask or encourage + a person, I used to + verb (My friends encourage me to ...).
- For a purpose, I used to + verb, not "for" + verb.
