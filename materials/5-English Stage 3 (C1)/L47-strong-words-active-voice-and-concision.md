# Lesson 47: Strong Words, Active Voice and Concision

*English Stage 3 (C1) · Unit 5: Argument and Academic Style*
*Adapted from Reading, Writing, Research, and Reasoning: An Advanced ESL Text by Gabriel Winer and Elizabeth Wadell, ASCCC Open Educational Resources Initiative (CC BY-NC 4.0): 6.5 Using Strong and Specific Words; 6.7 Using Active Voice; 6.8 Avoiding Wordiness.*

## In this lesson
You will practice three strategies for clear sentences: choosing strong, specific words; using active voice where it is appropriate; and cutting wordy phrases. You will advise the student writer Amanda Wu on her draft about food deserts (Lesson 46) and then revise a paragraph yourself. These strategies make research writing, including descriptions of psychology studies, shorter, clearer and more lively.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Replace general words and weak verbs (get, make, have, do) with strong, specific ones.
- Identify passive voice and rewrite it in active voice.
- Decide when passive voice is acceptable, for example in the methods section of a research report.
- Find and cut wordy expressions such as "there is", "due to the fact that" and "in order to".

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| specific (adj) | exact, with a precise meaning (opposite: general) | Instead of "things," tell your reader what the "things" are. |
| weak verb | a general verb such as be, do, get, make or have | The committee made a decision to revise the community guidelines. |
| strong verb | a verb with a specific meaning that carries the action | The committee decided to revise the community guidelines. |
| active voice | the subject does the action | Regulators require documentation of pesticide use. |
| passive voice | be + past participle: the receiver of the action is the subject | Documentation of pesticide use is required by regulators. |
| scrutinize (v) | examine closely and carefully | Environmental regulators have scrutinized the strawberry industry. |
| wordiness (n) | using more words than you need | Eliminating wordiness helps all readers because it makes your ideas clear, direct, and straightforward. |
| empty phrase | an expression that adds little meaning | these days, in terms of, due to the fact that, on the subject of, more or less |
| methods section | the part of a research paper that describes how the study was done | Writers usually use passive voice in the methods section of research papers. |
| control group / placebo | the group in an experiment that does not receive the real treatment / a "treatment" with no active effect | Patients in the control group were given a saline solution placebo. |

## Rules and explanations

### Strong and specific words
- **Each word counts.** Choose words with specific meanings. Instead of "things," or even "vegetables," name and describe them (for example, the colorful bell peppers in the book's photo of a farmers market).
- **Strong verbs.** A noun with a weak verb (be, do, make) slows the sentence down and adds words: *made a decision* → *decided*. The strong verb gives the same meaning with fewer words and more lively action.
- Depending on your meaning, try these stronger verbs from the book:

| Instead of | try |
|---|---|
| get | obtain, understand, fetch, receive, attain |
| make | cause, require, produce, create, construct |
| have (as a main verb) | possess, experience, keep, own, exhibit |
| do (as a main verb) | attempt, complete, finish, achieve, participate in, contribute to |

### Active and passive voice
- **Active:** the subject does the action. *Over the past several years, environmental regulators have scrutinized the strawberry industry.*
- **Passive:** the grammatical object (the strawberry industry) moves to the subject position, and the doer comes at the end with by, or disappears. *The strawberry industry has been scrutinized over the past several years.*
- To find passive voice, look for a form of **be + past participle** (has been scrutinized, is required). The book describes the participle as the "-ed" form; irregular participles count too (was given, is sold).
- To revise, ask who or what does the action in each clause, and make that the subject: *Documentation of pesticide use is required by regulators.* → *Regulators require documentation of pesticide use.*
- Active voice usually makes writing clearer, livelier and less wordy.

### When is passive voice acceptable?
When you want to focus on **what** was done, not **who** did it, and the meaning stays clear. Writers usually use passive voice in the methods section of research papers and in lab reports:
- *The compound was heated to 75 degrees Celsius.*
- *Patients in the control group were given a saline solution placebo.*
- *Respondents who agreed were contacted with follow-up questions.*

In other sections and in most writing, use active voice if possible.

**In psychology (APA style):** APA style prefers active voice even in methods sections ("We randomly assigned participants to two groups"), but accepts passive when the person who did the action does not matter ("Participants were randomly assigned to two groups").

### Avoiding wordiness
| Type | Wordy | Revised (the book's examples) |
|---|---|---|
| "There is / There are" openings | There is an abundance of agriculture in California. | Agriculture is abundant in California. |
| empty phrases (these days, in terms of, due to the fact that, on the subject of, more or less) | These days, food deserts exist in many parts of the state; ... people do not have access to fresh, affordable foods due to a variety of reasons. | Food deserts exist in many parts of the state; ... people do not have access to fresh, affordable foods for a variety of reasons. |
| constructions that sound important but are empty | In order to combat skepticism about phenomena such as gentrification, it's absolutely essential that community members are invited to be involved in opening new supermarkets in their own communities. | To combat skepticism about gentrification, it's essential that community members be involved in opening new supermarkets in their own communities. |

Common short replacements: in order to → to; due to the fact that → because; on the subject of → about; absolutely essential → essential.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Formal Arabic often uses a general verb + verbal noun (literally "carried out a visit", "made a decision"). Translated word for word, this gives wordy English with weak verbs. Use the strong verb: visited, decided.
> - Arabic news style often says that something "was completed" (the signing of the agreement was completed). In English, name the doer: the two governments signed the agreement.

**Exercise 3.** (book) Sentence 1 is best because it is in active voice: it focuses on the subject (pesticides) doing actions (eliminating, killing) to objects (destructive insects, bees). Sentence 2 is in passive voice: it gives the same information, but it focuses on the objects and adds the subject (pesticides) as if it were extra information; it is not wrong, but it is less clear. Sentence 3 is in passive voice and does not name the subject (pesticides) at all.

**Exercise 4.** (book; revision 3 corrected)
1. ...because not enough fresh produce is sold by stores in their communities. → ...because not enough stores in their communities sell fresh produce.
2. ...and vehicles are not owned by one-third of its residents. → ...and one-third of its residents do not own vehicles.
3. ...it can reasonably be assumed that if people don't have a car and taking the bus is needed..., their willingness to purchase healthy food will be diminished. → Given the facts above, one can reasonably assume that if people don't have a car and need to take the bus to the market, they will be less willing to purchase healthy food. (The book's key says "it can reasonably be assumed"; this is a mistake because that phrase is still passive voice. Amanda's draft has the active "one can reasonably assume"; "we can reasonably assume" is also correct.)

Other passive forms in the paragraph can stay: "is based on" (a common fixed expression), "published" and "As specified by" (short participle phrases), and "is considered" (inside a quotation, which you cannot change).
