# Lesson 50: Commonly Confused Words and Spelling

*English Stage 3 (C1) · Unit 5: Argument and Academic Style*
*Adapted from College ESL Writers: Applied Grammar and Composing Strategies for Success by Barbara Hall and Elizabeth Wallace, Georgia State University - Perimeter College (CC BY-NC-SA): 6.1 Commonly Confused Words; 6.2 Spelling.*

## In this lesson
You will study words that writers often confuse because they sound, look or mean alike (affect and effect, its and it's, than and then), the main spelling rules, commonly misspelled words and homonyms. A spell checker does not catch a correctly spelled wrong word, so you will practise proofreading paragraphs yourself. Accurate words and spelling build your credibility with professors and other readers.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Choose correctly between commonly confused words such as affect / effect, its / it's and than / then.
- Apply seven common spelling rules for ie / ei, final y, final e and plurals.
- Recognize the problem areas in commonly misspelled words.
- Distinguish common homonyms such as principle / principal and lead / led.
- Proofread a paragraph for word errors that a spell checker misses.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| commonly confused words | words that share a similar pronunciation, meaning or spelling with another word | new / knew |
| homonym | a word that sounds like another word but has a different meaning (often called a homophone) | peace / piece |
| contraction | two words joined with an apostrophe that replaces letters | it's = it is; who's = who is or who has |
| possessive pronoun | a pronoun that shows possession; it never has an apostrophe | its, their, whose, your |
| vowel / consonant | the letters a, e, i, o, u / the other letters | delay (vowel + y); happy (consonant + y) |
| ending (suffix) | letters added to the end of a word | -able, -ence, -ing, -ity, -less, -ment, -ly |
| spell checker | software that flags misspelled words | It will not usually catch "too days" for "two days". |
| mnemonic device | a memory trick | "be a beautiful person": beautiful begins with "be a" |
| skim | read quickly without attention to every word | Skimming, you may overlook misspelled words. |
| credibility | the reader's trust in the writer | Error-free spelling enhances your credibility. |

## Rules and explanations
### Commonly confused words
| Words | How to choose | The book's example |
|---|---|---|
| a / an / and | **a** before a consonant sound, **an** before a vowel sound; **and** joins words | a key, an ocean, pen and pencil |
| accept / except | **accept** (verb) = take or agree to something offered; **except** = but, only | They accepted our proposal for the conference. |
| affect / effect | **affect** (verb) = create a change; **effect** (noun) = a result | Hurricane winds affect the amount of rainfall. The heavy rains will have an effect on the crop growth. |
| are / our | **are** = a form of "be"; **our** = belonging to us | We will bring our cameras to take pictures. |
| by / buy | **by** = next to; **buy** = purchase | My glasses are by the bed. |
| its / it's | **its** = belonging to it; **it's** = it is | The butterfly flapped its wings. |
| know / no | **know** = understand; **no** = a negative | I have no time to visit the zoo this weekend. |
| loose / lose | **loose** (adjective) = not tight; **lose** (verb) = misplace, give up, fail to earn | Without a belt, her pants are loose on her waist. |
| of / have | after could, should, would, must, write **have** (a helping verb), never "of" | I should have helped her with that heavy box. |
| quite / quiet / quit | **quite** = really; **quiet** = not loud; **quit** = stop | I need a quiet room to complete the assignments. |
| right / write | **right** = correct, or the opposite of left; **write** = put words on paper | After the team members bowl, I will write down their scores. |
| set / sit | **set** = put something down (also a group of objects); **sit** = lower yourself onto a seat | She set the mug on the saucer. |
| suppose / supposed to | **suppose** = think, or suggest; **be supposed to** = be required or expected to | She was supposed to create the menu. |
| than / then | **than** compares; **then** = next, or at that time | Doctors first complete medical school and then obtain a residency. |
| their / they're / there | **their** = belonging to them; **they're** = they are; **there** = in that place, or "there is / are" | There are more treats if the dogs behave. |
| to / two / too | **to** = toward, or before a verb (to play); **two** = 2; **too** = also, or more than is good | The tents were too loud, and we left. |
| use / used to | **use** = apply for a purpose; **used to** = a past habit that has stopped | He used to hire a team to landscape, but now he landscapes alone. |
| who's / whose | **who's** = who is / who has; **whose** = belonging to whom | Whose schedule allows them to take the new student on a campus tour? |
| your / you're | **your** = belonging to you; **you're** = you are | You're the girl with the unzipped book bag. |

(The book defines **too** as "also or very"; more exactly, it means "more than is good or needed": the tents were too loud, so we left.)

**Three strategies:** use a dictionary (print, online or an app); keep a list of the words you commonly confuse and check it before you submit; study the list before you write. Remember that a spell checker will not usually catch these words, because they are real words.

### Spelling rules
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| i before e, except after c, or when it sounds like "ay" (neighbor, weigh) | achieve, niece, alien; receive, deceive |
| consonant + y: change y to i before an ending (but not before -ing: crying) | happy + er = happier; cry + ed = cried |
| vowel + y: keep the y | delay + ed = delayed (exceptions: daily, laid, said, paid) |
| ending that begins with a vowel (-able, -ence, -ing, -ity): drop the final e | write + ing = writing; pure + ity = purity |
| ending that begins with a consonant (-less, -ment, -ly): keep the final e | hope + less = hopeless; advertise + ment = advertisement |
| many words ending in consonant + o: add -s | photos, sopranos |
| words ending in s, ch, sh, x: add -es | churches, faxes |

Some rules have exceptions, so check new words in a dictionary.

### Commonly misspelled words
The table in your material shows the problem area of each word in bold. Some useful ones for academic writing: arg**u**ment, beha**vi**or, def**ini**te, envir**on**ment, gove**rn**ment, jud**gm**ent, ne**ce**ssary, o**cc**asion, **psy**cholo**gy**, ref**er**ence, sep**ara**te, su**cc**ess, th**orou**gh, wri**tt**en.

**Tips:** copy each word a few times and underline the problem area; test yourself with flashcards; read your work word by word instead of skimming; use mnemonic devices (say Wed-nes-day to spell Wednesday); keep a list of the words you misspell and add words from your corrected papers; check spelling at the editing stage, after the big ideas and paragraphs.

### Commonly misused homonyms
| Words | Meanings |
|---|---|
| principle / principal | a fundamental concept accepted as true / the head of a school, or the original amount of a debt |
| where / wear / ware(s) | in which place / have on the body (clothes) / goods for sale |
| lead / led | a metal (it sounds like "led") / the past tense of the verb lead |
| which / witch | one out of a group / a person who practises sorcery |
| peace / piece | calm, no conflict / a part of a whole |
| passed / past | the past tense of pass (went by) / the time before now |
| lessen / lesson | reduce / something a student studies |
| patience / patients | the ability to wait or suffer calmly / people under medical care |
| sees / seas / seize | perceives with the eyes / large bodies of salt water / take by force |
| threw / through | the past tense of throw / in one side and out of the other |

> **In psychology:** "affect" is also a noun meaning feeling or emotion, stressed on the first syllable: "positive affect", "flat affect". In everyday academic writing, the rule above (affect = verb, effect = noun) still holds.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic writing usually leaves out short vowels, so Arabic speakers often drop or confuse English vowel letters (importent, seperate, opinien). Look hard at the vowels in the problem areas of the misspelled-words table.
> - Arabic has no /p/ sound, so p and b are easily mixed in spelling (probably, not brobably). Say the word slowly and check it.

## Read aloud
In each pair, the two words sound the same or almost the same. Say them clearly and think about the spelling.
1. I liked her new sweater. I knew she would wear that sweater today.
2. The principal held a conference for both parents and teachers.
3. The principle of human equality is an important foundation for all nations.
4. Hurricane winds af·FECT the amount of rainfall. The heavy rains will have an ef·FECT on the crop growth.
5. They ac·CEPT·ed our proposal for the conference.
6. She threw the football with perfect form. She walked through the door and out of his life.

## Writing task
Write a paragraph of 150-200 words about a topic from PSYC 110, for example how sleep affects memory or the effects of stress on health. Use at least eight words from this lesson's lists correctly (for example affect, effect, than, then, its, it's, their, there, principle, led, past, lessen), and underline them. Then proofread the paragraph slowly, word by word, and add any word you were unsure of to your error log from Lesson 30.

Self-editing checklist:
- Did I read word by word, without skimming?
- its / it's, their / they're, your / you're, who's / whose: does each apostrophe replace missing letters?
- affect (verb) or effect (noun)? than (comparison) or then (time)?
- Did I write could / should / would have, never "of"?
- Did I follow the rules for final y and final e?
- Did I check words from the misspelled list in a dictionary?
- Did I run the spell checker, and then think about each suggestion before accepting it?

Paste your draft into the chat and ask your tutor for feedback. Then rewrite it.
