# Lesson 04: Pronunciation: Consonants and Linking

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 1: People and Places*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 1.6: Consonants; 1.7: Linking Consonants to Vowels.*

## In this lesson
You will look at the consonant sounds of American English and notice how they move from the front to the back of the mouth. Then you will learn to link a consonant at the end of a word to a vowel at the start of the next word. You will practice with sentences from the Berlin video and record yourself.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Explain the difference between consonant letters and consonant sounds.
- Give an example word for each consonant sound.
- Name pairs of unvoiced and voiced consonants, such as /p/ and /b/.
- Link a final consonant to a vowel at the start of the next word.
- Hear linking in fast speech and use it when you read aloud.

| linking | joining the last consonant of a word to the first vowel of the next word | time off → ti-moff |

## Rules and explanations

### Consonant letters and consonant sounds
- There are 21 consonant letters in the English alphabet: all the letters that are not vowels (A, E, I, O, U).
- A phonemic chart shows **sounds**, not letters. On the American English Phonemic Chart (by Adrian Underhill), the consonant sounds are in the bottom three rows:
  - Row 4: /p/ /b/ /t/ /d/ /tʃ/ /dʒ/ /k/ /g/
  - Row 5: /f/ /v/ /θ/ /ð/ /s/ /z/ /ʃ/ /ʒ/
  - Row 6: /m/ /n/ /ŋ/ /h/ /l/ /r/ /w/ /j/

### Sounds move from front to back
Say the sounds in row 4 from left to right. /p/ and /b/ start at your lips. /t/ and /d/ use the front of your tongue. /k/ and /g/ are at the back of your mouth. The sound moves backwards.

### Voiced and unvoiced pairs
The sounds come in pairs. /p/ is unvoiced (no voice, only air) and /b/ is voiced (you use your voice). Other pairs: /t/ and /d/, /k/ and /g/, /f/ and /v/, /s/ and /z/. You will learn more in Unit 2.

### Linking consonants to vowels
- When a word ends in a consonant and the next word begins with a vowel, link the consonant to the vowel: the consonant seems to start the next word.
- Why? The final consonant is easier to say, your speech sounds more natural, and you will start to hear native speakers do this.
- Examples: time off → ti-moff; date of → da-tof; keep up → kee-pup; at a → a-ta.
- Listen for **sounds**, not letters. "hours" starts with a vowel sound (the h is silent), so "ten hours" links: te-nours. "one" starts with /w/ and "unity" starts with /j/ as in "yes", so these words start with a consonant sound.

### Final /t/ becomes a fast "d"
When a final /t/ links to a vowel, it often sounds like a fast "d": date of = da-dəv; at a = a-də. You can also hear it in "a lot of" (a-lo-dəv) and "Forget about it" (for-ge-da-bou-dit).

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic has no /p/ sound, so "pick" and "bick" can sound the same to you. For /p/, close your lips and push out a small puff of air, with no voice.
> - Arabic has /f/ but no /v/. For /v/ (as in "vintage"), put your top teeth on your bottom lip, like /f/, and use your voice.

## Practice
The audio clip for exercise 5 is in the attachments folder for 1.7 Linking Consonants to Vowels on your computer (folder "01.07-linking-consonants-to-vowels"), or on the original web page.

**1. Consonant sounds.** For each consonant sound in rows 4, 5 and 6, write one word that has the sound. Use words from this unit when you can. Book examples: /p/: people, place, pick. /b/: ball, bike, birthday. Then say row 4 from left to right and feel the sound move back in your mouth. Ask your tutor to check your words.

**2. Watch.** Watch Rachel's English, "Linking Consonants to Vowels" (link below).

**3. Recording 1.** Write these phrases and mark each link with _ (example: time_off). There are 11 links. Check your answer in the Answer key. Then say the phrases, record yourself, listen, note your mistakes, and record again. Score: ____ / 11
- What's up?
- ten hours a day
- That's what I thought.
- What is it?
- It's his anniversary.
- I'm on the train.
- This is too much.
- Forget about it.

**4. Phrases from the book's slides.** Say each phrase, then each example, with linking. Mark the link in each phrase.
- covered in: It's covered in art and graffiti. / The children are covered in paint.
- symbol of unity: The Olympic rings are a symbol of unity for all the countries that participate.
- green escape: Parks in New York and London offer a nice quiet green escape from the busy, noisy, stressful cities around them.
- full of: It's full of wild deer and other animals. / The glass is full of water.
- head over to: I'm going to head over to campus after my meeting.
- You can't come to ... and not ...: You can't go to Thailand and not eat mango with sticky rice.

Then make your own sentence with each phrase about your city, and say it to your tutor.

**5. Listening quiz.** Play "Linking_Listening_Practice" and write the missing words. Spell them correctly. You can do this as many times as you want.
1. The Berlin Wall is between East and West Berlin. It's covered ___ ___ and graffiti.
2. Brandenburg Gate is a symbol ___ ___ between East and West Germany, so it's ___ ___ site to visit.
3. Tiergarten is ___ ___ the biggest parks in the city. It's a nice quiet ___ ___ from the city. It's ___ ___ wild deer ___ ___ animals.
4. If you want to see some cool ___ ___, then ___ ___ to Friedrichshain. There are ___ ___ ___ old warehouses that are now cafes and art galleries.
5. You can't come to Berlin and ___ ___ ___ Currywurst!

**6. Notice linking.** Use the five full sentences in "Notice: Linking Practice" in your printed lesson (or exercise 5 with the missing words). Find each final consonant and draw a line to the vowel at the start of the next word.

**7. Recording 2.** Read the five sentences aloud with all the links. Record yourself, listen, make sure you linked every place you marked, and record again. Then read them to your tutor and ask where you stopped between words.
