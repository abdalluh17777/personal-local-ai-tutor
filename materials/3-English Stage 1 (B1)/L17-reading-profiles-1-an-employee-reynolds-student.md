# Lesson 17: Reading Profiles 1: An Employee, Reynolds, Student Spotlights

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 2: Getting to Know You*
*Adapted from Getting to Know You - An Intermediate Integrated-Skills Module for English Learners by Sara Ferguson, Grossmont College (CC BY-NC-SA): 1.1: Video: Amazon Employee Profile; 1.2: Reading: Profile Reynolds; 1.3: Reading: Student Spotlights.*

## In this lesson
You will study three kinds of profile: a company video about an employee, an article about the park ranger Leslie Reynolds, and two university "student spotlights". You will learn what a profile is, find facts and quotations, learn career and college words, and write the questions the writer asked in the interview.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Describe the main features of a profile.
- Name the genre, audience and purpose of a profile.
- Find facts and quotations in a profile.
- Use career words such as "recipient", "inspire", "mentor" and "rewarding".
- Write the open-ended questions an interviewer asked to write a profile.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| profile | a short text or video about one person | The article is a profile of a park ranger. |
| recipient (n) | a person or thing that receives something | the 2015 recipient of the Harry Yount National Park Ranger Award |
| inspire (v) | make someone feel that they want to do something and can do it | Reynolds continues to inspire the new generation of park rangers. |
| mentor (v) | advise or train someone younger or less experienced | She enjoys developing and mentoring others. |
| rewarding (adj) | giving satisfaction | a career that is rewarding |
| challenge (v) | give someone a task that needs great effort | a career that continues to challenge me every day |
| major (n) | the main subject you study at college | In her second year, Jennifer chose Civil Engineering. |
| despite | even though; in spite of (before a noun phrase) | Despite my head cold, I went to work today. |
| open-ended question | begins with a wh- word and allows a long answer | Why did you decide to attend Stony Brook University? |

## Rules and explanations

### What is a profile?
A profile is about **one person** (anyone, not only famous people). It is **short** and gives **a few facts** about the person's history. It focuses on **one part** of their life (work, school, a hobby or a special interest). It has a **positive tone**.

### Genre, audience, purpose and quotations
- **Genre**: what kind of text it is and where you find it. **Audience**: who reads it. **Purpose**: why it was made (to inform, to convince...).
- Clues: the topic, the format, the register (formal or informal), the pictures and the vocabulary.
- A **quotation** is a person's exact words inside quotation marks: "word word word." Most profiles use quotations. Ask: who is speaking?

### Profiles come from interviews
The writer first interviews the person. You can work backwards: read a paragraph and write the question the writer asked. Good interviewers ask **open-ended questions**: they begin with a wh- word and allow a long answer.
- Closed: Did you decide to attend Stony Brook University? (yes or no)
- Open-ended: Why did you decide to attend Stony Brook University? (long answer)

### Collocations (words that often go together)
recipient of; award to; develop a program/business/system/method; inspire (someone) to do (something); rewarding job/career/work; challenge (someone) to do (something); mentor (someone); journey to/through/from

### Despite
"Despite" means "even though" or "in spite of": the person was not stopped by something. Put a noun phrase after it: Despite the difficulty of the final exam, the students passed the class.

### U.S. college words
freshman (1st year), sophomore (2nd), junior (3rd), senior (4th). Undergraduate students study for an associate's (2-year) or bachelor's (4-year) degree; graduate students study after the bachelor's (master's, doctorate). To declare a major = to officially choose your main subject.

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
> - Arabic questions have no extra helping verb, but English past wh- questions need "did" before the subject: "Where did you go to high school?" (not "Where you went to high school?").

**2. Reynolds: comprehension.** Read "Congratulations to the 2015 recipient of the Harry Yount National Park Ranger Award!"

d) What programs and teams did she start or serve?
e) What is the name of the award, and what organization gave it to her?
f) Who is speaking in the final paragraph (with quotation marks)?
g) What are three of her professional accomplishments?
h) What inspires her, in her own words?
i) Purpose: To inform readers of the services at the park / To convince readers that Leslie Reynolds is an excellent employee / To persuade readers to visit a national park / To educate readers about the dangers at the park
j) Why do organizations give awards? Choose all possible answers and add one: To motivate employees to do a good job / To inform employees and visitors of the expectations for a good employee / To convince customers/visitors that the organization has good employees / To make employees feel valuable
k) The article is mostly about her ____ life. (professional / personal)
l) She has worked for the National Park Service for ____ years.
