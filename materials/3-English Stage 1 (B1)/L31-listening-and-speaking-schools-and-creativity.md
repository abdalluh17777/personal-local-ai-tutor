# Lesson 31: Listening and Speaking: Schools and Creativity

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 4: Education and Creativity*
*Adapted from Speaking, Listening and Pronunciation Projects for ELLs - Intermediate Level by Brittany Zemlick, Grossmont College (CC BY 4.0): 4.1: Listening and Speaking - Schools and Creativity; 4.2: Vocabulary Review.*

## In this lesson
You will watch two minutes of a famous TED Talk, "Do Schools Kill Creativity?" by Sir Ken Robinson. He tells a true story about his friend Gillian Lynne, a girl who could not sit still at school. You will learn words about school, learning problems and dance, and talk with your tutor about creativity.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Understand the main idea, the details and the order of the story about Gillian Lynne.
- Use new words such as concentrate, fidget, learning disorder, specialist and calm down.
- Say the genre, audience and purpose of a talk.
- Give your opinion about creativity and school subjects.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| concentrate | to give all your attention to what you are doing | She couldn't concentrate or sit still. |
| fidget | to make a lot of small, restless movements because you are nervous or bored | She fidgeted. |
| learning disorder | problems or difficulty with learning | The school thought Gillian had a learning disorder. |
| ADHD | the book calls it a learning disorder: children with it move around a lot and have problems concentrating | What is ADHD? |
| a specialist | a doctor who knows a lot about one kind of problem | Her mother took Gillian to see a special doctor, a specialist. |
| medication | medicine from a doctor | Did the doctor give Gillian medication? |
| calm down | to stop being angry, upset, excited or nervous | When children have too much energy, parents tell them to calm down. |
| soloist | someone who dances alone on the stage | Soloists are usually the most talented dancers. |
| musical theater | a kind of performance where people sing and dance on stage | She helped create some very successful musical theater productions. |
| founded | started (a company or a school) | She founded a dance company. |

## Rules and explanations

### Genre, audience and purpose
- **Genre** = the type of video (the book's choices: TED Talk, video from YouTube, news program, talk show).
- **Audience** = the people who will watch it. Ask: "Who will watch this video?"
- **Purpose** = why the speaker tells the story. A story can have more than one purpose.

### Word families and useful phrases
- invent (verb) - invented (past) - inventor (person) - inventions (things).
- **founded** is the past of *found* (= start a company). Do not confuse it with *found*, the past of *find*.
- **sit still** = sit without moving: Gillian couldn't sit still.
- **turn in homework** = give your homework to the teacher: She turned in her homework late.

## Speak with your tutor

- "Let's discuss: Is learning science, English, and math more important than learning art, music, and dance? Ask me why."
- "Ask me: What did you think of this story? What does Sir Ken Robinson want us to learn from it? Does moving help you think?"
- "Give me a definition of a word from this lesson, and I will say the word."
