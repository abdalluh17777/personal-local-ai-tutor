# Lesson 07: Listening: Immigrants; Grammar: Verbs, Simple Sentences, Imperatives

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 1: People and Places*
*Adapted from People and Places - An Intermediate Integrated-Skills Module for English Learners by Sara Ferguson, Grossmont College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): 1.4: Videos: The Contributions of Immigrants; 1.5: Grammar: What is a Verb?; 1.6: Grammar: Simple Sentences; 1.7: Grammar: Imperative Mood.*

## In this lesson
You will watch a video about immigrants in San Francisco's Mission District and a news report about Iraqi Chaldean immigrants in El Cajon, California. Then you will study verbs, simple sentence patterns and the imperative.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Find the genre, purpose and details of a short video.
- Use words like culture, immigrant and integration to talk about immigrants.
- Find the verb in a sentence and use the auxiliaries do, be and have.
- Find the subject, verb and predicate of a simple sentence.
- Use the imperative for requests, instructions and orders.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| immigrant | a person who comes to live permanently in a foreign country | Some students at this college are immigrants. |
| culture / cultural | the way of life of a group: art, music, religion, language, food, tradition | Immigrants contribute many cultural benefits to our community. |
| integration | when two cultures mix in a society and both have equal value | Does this video show the integration of Chaldean Iraqis in El Cajon? |
| Hispanic | coming from a Spanish-speaking country | What cultural contributions do Hispanic immigrants make? |
| auxiliary | a helping verb: be, do, have (and modals) | Did you visit the art museum? |
| predicate | the part of the sentence with the verb | We live in El Cajon. (predicate: live in El Cajon) |
| linking verb | a verb (be, seem) that can be followed by an adjective | The weather today seems dry. |
| imperative | the base form of a verb, used without a subject | Don't forget your umbrella! |

## Rules and explanations
### What is a verb?
- The verb is the center of an English sentence; every sentence needs one. A sentence can be just a verb: Study! Eat! Help!
- "Action" is not the test: tornado and wave (nouns) and windy (adjective) show activity but are not verbs; think and understand are verbs but not activities.
- A verb shows **tense** (time): flew (past), flies (present), will fly (future).
- A verb shows **aspect**: simple (flew, flies), continuous (is flying, was flying), perfect (has flown, had flown).
- Test: can the word show past tense (-ed or a new form)? show → showed, think → thought. Then it is a verb.

### Auxiliaries: be, do, have (plus modals)
- **do / does / did** makes questions and negatives in the simple present and past: Do you want a souvenir? I did not visit the art museum.
- **be (am, is, are, was, were) + -ing** makes the continuous: Are they flying a kite? We were sunbathing at the beach.
- **have / has / had + past participle** makes the perfect: That family has immigrated to El Cajon. We had already seen the zoo.
- The continuous always uses "be". An -ing word **without** "be" is often a gerund (a noun): "activities like hiking, camping, and mountain-biking". (Your printed text says "with"; the book's slides correctly say "without".)

### Simple sentences
- A **clause** is a group of words with a subject and a verb. A simple sentence has one clause.
- The **predicate** is the part with the verb: San Diego (subject) + has warm weather (predicate).
- Action verbs: jump, read, talk, ask. Stative (non-action) verbs: understand, like, be, seem.
- **Linking verbs** (be, seem) can be followed by an adjective. Other verbs cannot: NOT "He owned nice." NOT "She believed wonderful."
- The subject can be "it" or "there": It seems cold today. There are 26 students in this class.

Simple sentences use four patterns:

1. Subject + verb + noun phrase or prepositional phrase: San Diego has warm weather. We live in San Diego County.
2. Subject + linking verb + adjective: The weather today seems dry.
3. Subject + be + noun phrase: Some students at this college are immigrants.
4. Subject + be + prepositional phrase: Grossmont College is in El Cajon.

### The imperative
- Form: base form of the verb + (complement). The subject "you" is understood but not written: Open the door!
- Negative: Do not (Don't) + base form: Don't forget your umbrella!
- Use it for requests (Sit down, please.), instructions (Read the first part of the chapter.) and orders (Leave now!).

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - In Arabic, a present-tense sentence often has no verb ("the weather cool"). English always needs a verb: The weather today **is** cool and breezy.
> - Arabic makes negatives and questions without a helper like "do". English uses do, does or did: I **do not** want a souvenir (not "I not want"). **Do** you want a souvenir?

    - a) Grossmont College / is (be) / NP: a community college

    - c) El Cajon / is (be) / PP: in East County
