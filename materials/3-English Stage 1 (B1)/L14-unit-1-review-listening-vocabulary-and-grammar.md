# Lesson 14: Unit 1 Review: Listening, Vocabulary and Grammar

*English Stage 1 (B1) · Unit 1: People and Places*
*Adapted from People and Places - An Intermediate Integrated-Skills Module for English Learners by Sara Ferguson, Grossmont College (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): 4.1: Randall's ESL Listening Lab: Sightseeing; 4.3: Vocabulary Review; 4.4: Grammar Review; 4.5: Grammar Review 2.*

## In this lesson
This lesson reviews Unit 1. You listen to a recording about sightseeing and practice the vocabulary of the unit: sights, seasons, outdoor activities, immigration and culture. Then you review the grammar of the unit and correct the mistakes in two emails.

## Learning goals
After this lesson you should be able to:
- Recommend sights and activities in a city, using the unit vocabulary.
- Use the simple present, the present continuous and the imperative correctly.
- Find the subjects and verbs in a sentence, and write correct compound sentences.
- Use capital letters for proper nouns, and put adjectives before nouns.
- Find and correct mistakes with adjectives, comparatives and superlatives in an email.

## Key language
| Word or phrase | Meaning in simple English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| recommend (v) | to say that something is good and someone should try it | "What restaurant do you recommend to your classmates?" |
| souvenir (n) | a small thing you buy on a trip, to remember the place | "Have you ever brought a souvenir home from your travels?" |
| integration (n) | when two groups mix in a society and both have equal value | "The Mission District is a good example of integration." |
| grief / joy (n) | a feeling of sadness / a feeling of happiness | "The music allows them to experience their griefs and joys." |
| proper noun | the name of one specific person, place or thing; it starts with a capital letter | "Spider-man is our favorite superhero." |
| FANBOYS | words that join two clauses: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so | "She learned a lot about Spain, but she also discovered a lot about herself." |
| imperative | the base form of a verb, used for instructions and advice | "Don't miss the Crawford reservoir." |
| present continuous | am / is / are + verb-ing, for actions happening now | "My friends and I are going to the movies after class." |

## Rules and explanations
A short summary of the grammar in Unit 1.

### Subjects and verbs
- The subject is the person, place or thing doing the action. The verb shows the action or "being" (is, are, seem). A sentence can have more than one subject or verb.

### Simple present, present continuous and imperative
- Simple present: facts, routines and permanent situations. After he, she, it or one thing, add -s: "She drives from Mexico to San Diego once a week."
- Present continuous (am / is / are + verb-ing): actions happening now ("We are strolling on the beach"), or new and temporary actions ("This semester, she is working at a restaurant").
- Stative verbs (own, like, be, understand) are usually not continuous: "I own a car", not "I am owning a car".
- Imperative: the base form with no subject, for instructions: "Spend an afternoon ...". Negative: "Don't forget ...".

### Proper nouns
- Common nouns are general: a mountain, a school. Proper nouns are specific names (people, months, mountains, schools, cities, countries, nationalities, languages). They start with a capital letter.

### Compound sentences
- Two independent clauses (subject + verb, subject + verb), joined by a comma and a FANBOYS word. The most common are and, but, or and so.
- It is not compound if there is only one clause: one subject with two verbs ("We go to the beach and enjoy the water"), or a list of words joined by "and".

### Adjectives, comparatives and superlatives
- Adjectives usually come before the noun: "a small lake". The usual order is opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose: "a beautiful new Italian car".
- -ing adjectives describe the thing that causes a feeling ("The movie was amazing"). -ed adjectives describe how a person feels ("She felt excited").
- Two things: -er + than, or more / less + adjective + than: "larger than", "more difficult than".
- Three or more: the + -est, or the most / the least: "the highest point", "the most popular mountain".

> **Note for Arabic speakers**
>
> - Arabic sentences in the present often have no verb "be". In English you need it: "We are strolling on the beach", not "We strolling on the beach".
> - In Arabic, the adjective comes after the noun. In English, it usually comes before the noun: "delicious food", not "food delicious".

Make a conversation with your tutor: you are visiting San Diego and would like to see some sights. Where should you go and why?

**2. Immigrants: match.** In 4.3 ("Vocabulary from Videos 'Contributions of Immigrants'"), match the seven words (culture, immigrant, grief, joy, integration, Hispanic, public) with their definitions.

### Part B: Grammar review

**5. Simple present.** Fill in the blanks in the Mission District paragraph (4.4, "Simple Present Tense Practice") with the simple present of: have, speak, keep, allow, create, see, enjoy, show, mix.

**6. Imperative and simple present.** Write five instructions for a friend visiting your city. After each imperative sentence, explain it with a sentence in the simple present. Use these verbs or your own: Don't miss, Spend, Bring, Visit, Don't forget, Remember, Take, See.

**7. Subjects and verbs.** Find the subject and the verb in the ten sentences of "Verbs and Subjects Practice" in 4.4 (about grey whales and festivals).

**8. Find the compound sentences.** In the last paragraph of "Overcoming Culture Shock" (in 4.4), find two compound sentences. What is the FANBOYS word in each?

**9. Compound or not?** Is each sentence compound? If not, why not? Find the subjects, the verbs and the FANBOYS.
- (a) It was exciting, but she felt alone and uncomfortable.
- (b) Maria's mother kissed Caitlin on both cheeks, and Caitlin was surprised.
- (c) People feel incompetent and nervous because of culture shock.
- (d) She felt overwhelmed by the new customs, language, and surroundings.
- (e) There was new food, new daily schedules, and new rules of etiquette.
- (f) They spoke loudly and they argued about politics.
- (g) That night, Caitlin crawled into a strange bed, but she wanted to go home.
- (h) We relate to others through shared cultural norms, and ordinarily, we don't even notice them.

**10. Combine.** Join each pair with a comma and "and", "or", "but" or "so". Then write two compound sentences of your own.
- (a) Cultural norms are difficult to understand. Many people feel culture shock when they visit a new country.
- (b) I feel culture shock every time I visit France. I still enjoy visiting there.
- (c) You can avoid visiting a new country. You can visit and try to adjust quickly.
- (d) Learning the language is important. It is not the only cause of culture shock.

**11. Editing Practice One.** Correct the email from the Concierge at Del Mar Resorts (in 4.4). The book says: 3 adjective use errors, 4 comparative/superlative errors and 11 capitalization errors.

**12. Editing Practice Two.** Correct Joanne's email to Seth (in 4.4). The book says: 1 compound sentence error, 3 superlative adjective errors, 5 adjective errors, 2 imperative mood errors and 4 errors with activity vocabulary words.

### Part C: Grammar Review 2

**13. Subjects and verbs.** There may be more than one subject or verb.
- (a) My friends and I are going to the movies after class.
- (b) We want to see the new Spider-man movie.
- (c) Spider-man is our favorite superhero.
- (d) The movie theater at the mall is cheaper.
- (e) I like to eat popcorn, and sometimes I also buy a soda.

**14. Simple present or present continuous?**
- (a) We ________ (have) dinner at my grandmother's house every Sunday.
- (b) Tonight, she ________ (cook) vegetarian pasta because my sister ________ (not/eat) meat.
- (c) Pasta ________ (be) my favorite food.
- (d) Right now, I ________ (make) some salad to bring to her house.
- (e) My sister usually ________ (bring) dessert.
- (f) I ________ (be) so excited for dinner.

**15. Capital letters.** Correct the proper nouns. Some sentences have no proper nouns.
- (a) We love going to the mountains in the winter months.
- (b) This january we will go to big bear mountain.
- (c) My grandchildren go to lexington elementary school.
- (d) That elementary school is close to my house.

**16. Your compound sentences.** Write compound sentences about a family member or a close friend. Use "and", "but", "or" and "so" at least one time each.

**17. Describe your favorite thing.** Write a paragraph with adjectives to describe the favorite thing in your house. Use at least one compound sentence, and use simple present and present continuous verbs. Underline all the verbs. Then read it to your tutor and check your verb tenses together.
